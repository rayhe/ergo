# 7-Critic Panel Evaluation: "Cicadas as Unregulated Noise Emission Event"

## 1. General Editor — 9.0/10
The article follows the established Ergo format precisely: absurd-yet-rigorous headline, scholarly deck, Roman-numeral sections building a systematic case, genuine footnotes with a Sources section. Structure escalates naturally from statute → mechanism → scale → precedent (municipal codes, FHWA thresholds, OSHA) → enforcement impossibility → decline. The closing lands: "The Noise Control Act is still the law. The office is still defunded. The cicadas are still louder than the statute." Minor weakness: the Schedule I cathinone sidebar, while the funniest paragraph in the piece, briefly pulls focus from the noise thesis into drug-enforcement territory; the article recovers by folding it back into the enforcement-void argument, but the detour is noticeable.

## 2. Voice Coach — 9.5/10
Dead-serious investigative tone throughout, matching the tides and coral reef articles. The humor emerges exclusively from deadpan precision, never from jokes. Standout lines: "an amplifier with legs," "the most complete regulatory evasion strategy in American administrative law," "That is the entire enforcement history of the largest unregulated noise emission events in modern American history: a blog post," "The EPA's Office of Noise Abatement and Control was defunded in 1982. The DEA was not." The ending ("It sings. It mates. It dies. It returns in 2042.") mirrors the tides piece's cadence without repeating it. Excellent voice match.

## 3. Ethics — 9.0/10
All statutes cited are real: Noise Control Act of 1972 (Pub. L. 92-574, 42 U.S.C. §§ 4901–4918), Quiet Communities Act (42 U.S.C. § 4913), OSHA 29 CFR § 1910.95, DEA Schedule I listing of cathinone. Brood XIV emergence details (13 states, first since 2008, second-largest brood, 2042 return, Plymouth 1634 record) verified against USA Today Network, Smithsonian, and Weather.com reporting. The 88.5 dBA measurement is a real engineering firm's published measurement; the 101 dB figure is press-reported without published methodology, which the article does not overclaim — it attributes it. The 2023 lawsuit (1:23-cv-01649) is a real filed complaint. Article clearly serves Ergo's educational mission of teaching critical reasoning about logical chains built on true facts. No ethical concerns.

## 4. Social/Shareability — 9.0/10
"Cicadas are louder than the statute" is immediately graspable. The fungus-manufactures-a-Schedule-I-drug twist is social media gold. The 1803/2245 dual-emergence framing and the "impound the trees" line create strong shareable moments. The 88.5 dBA vs. Chicago's 65/55 dB code comparison gives readers a concrete local anchor. Slightly less universally graspable than "the Moon is unlicensed" because the premise requires one sentence of Noise Control Act setup — the deck does that work, but the headline carries the whole burden alone. Still very high share potential.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 8.5/10
NCA citations are accurate: 42 U.S.C. § 4901 findings (20M/40M, national uniformity), § 4902 "person" definition, § 4913 Quiet Communities Act, ONAC defunding in 1982 (EPA's own history page). OSHA 29 CFR 1910.95 action level (85 dBA) and PEL (90 dBA) are correct. Cathinone is correctly identified as Schedule I under 21 CFR § 1308.11. The FHWA 67/72 dBA thresholds are attributed to the engineering source rather than overstated from the CFR. The article's deliberately wrong conclusion — that cicadas "violate" a statute that regulates persons and products — is the genre's disclosed conceit, and the piece is honest about the definitional escape hatch in Section V. Passes threshold, with the standard Ergo deduction for creative inference from regulatory text.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0/10
Strong sourcing across independent outlets: AP (101 dB, 1.5M/acre), University of Illinois Extension (dual emergence, NIH 90 dB figure, 64°F trigger), Civiltech engineering (88.5 dBA, FHWA thresholds), Scientific American (Massospora/cathinone mechanism), Smithsonian (Brood XIV lineage, 1634 record), phys.org (Apple Watch 90 dB), EPA's own history page (1982 defunding), the filed 2023 complaint (dormant programs). Minor weakness shared with the tides piece: the key acoustic evidence is press and industry reporting, not a peer-reviewed acoustics paper — but for a satirical investigation of a regulatory void, the absence of official measurements is arguably the point. The article is transparent about its sources.

## 7. Data Presentation — 9.0/10
Numbers are presented with consistent precision and always sourced: 88.5 dBA sustained (82–89 fluctuating) vs. 75 dBA highway max vs. 67/72 dBA FHWA thresholds vs. 70 dB EPA hearing guideline vs. 65/55 dB Chicago code — a devastating comparison framework. The exceedance arithmetic (23.5 dB day, 33.5 dB night, 21.5 dB over FHWA, 1.5 dB under OSHA PEL) is all correct. The "more than three doublings" perceived-loudness framing is properly hedged. The daylight-hours qualification on the six-week chorus was added during review. Clear, devastating, verifiable.
