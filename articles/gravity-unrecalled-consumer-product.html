Consumer Safety & Gravitational Compliance

The CPSC’s Own Product Hazard Framework Classifies Gravity as the Most Dangerous Unrecalled Consumer Product in American History

The Consumer Product Safety Act requires manufacturers to report any product presenting a “substantial product hazard.” Gravity causes 48,308 deaths and an estimated 36.8 million injuries annually in the United States. It has been in continuous, unregulated distribution for 4.54 billion years. No recall has ever been initiated. No corrective action plan has ever been filed. The product remains on the market.

By Julian Wexford · April 21, 2026 · 14 min read

I. The Statutory Framework

The Consumer Product Safety Act (CPSA), codified at 15 U.S.C. § 2051 et seq., established the Consumer Product Safety Commission and granted it broad authority to protect the public from “unreasonable risks of injury associated with consumer products.”1 Congress declared it the policy of the United States “to protect the public against unreasonable risks of injury associated with consumer products” and “to assist consumers in evaluating the comparative safety of consumer products.”

The statute defines a “consumer product” as “any article, or component part thereof, produced or distributed (i) for sale to a consumer for use in or around a permanent or temporary household or residence, a school, in recreation, or otherwise, or (ii) for the personal use, consumption or enjoyment of a consumer in or around a permanent or temporary household or residence, a school, in recreation, or otherwise.”2

The definition is broad by design. The legislative history makes clear that Congress intended the CPSA to reach “any article” used by consumers, subject only to narrow carve-outs for tobacco, motor vehicles, pesticides, firearms, and certain items regulated by other agencies.3 The statute does not require that the article be manufactured by a human enterprise. It does not require that the article be sold at retail. It does not require that the article be tangible. It requires only that the article be “produced or distributed” and used by consumers.

Section 15(a) of the CPSA, codified at 15 U.S.C. § 2064(a), defines a “substantial product hazard” as either a failure to comply with an applicable consumer product safety rule creating a substantial risk of injury, or “a product defect which (because of the pattern of defect, the number of defective products distributed in commerce, the severity of the risk, or otherwise) creates a substantial risk of injury to the public.”4

Section 15(b) requires every manufacturer, distributor, and retailer who obtains information reasonably supporting the conclusion that a product contains a defect creating a substantial risk of injury to “immediately inform the Commission.”5 The implementing regulation, 16 CFR § 1115.14(e), specifies that the report must be filed within 24 hours of obtaining such information.6

The question is not whether the CPSC has authority to address consumer products that cause tens of thousands of annual deaths. The statute was written precisely to confer that authority. The question is whether the most prolific source of injury deaths in the United States has ever been brought within the statute’s reach. It has not.

II. The Product Identification

Gravity is a fundamental interaction of nature, described by Newton’s law of universal gravitation (1687) and refined by Einstein’s general theory of relativity (1915). On the surface of the Earth, it produces a constant downward acceleration of approximately 9.80665 meters per second squared, the value adopted as “standard gravity” by the 3rd General Conference on Weights and Measures (CGPM) in 1901.7

The product is produced by the Earth, a body with a mass of approximately 5.972 × 1024 kilograms.8 It is distributed uniformly across the planet’s surface, with minor local variations attributable to altitude, latitude, and subsurface density differences. It is available at every permanent and temporary household, every school, every recreational facility, and every other location where consumers may be found. It has been in continuous distribution since the accretion of the Earth approximately 4.54 billion years ago.9

Consumers use gravity constantly. It is what holds them in their beds. It is what keeps food on their plates, water in their glasses, and their houses attached to their foundations. It is essential to walking, sitting, standing, and every other activity performed by a consumer in or around a residence. It is used in recreation (skiing, diving, skateboarding, trampolining). It is used in schools (physics demonstrations, playground equipment). It is used, in the language of the statute, “otherwise.”

Gravity satisfies every element of 15 U.S.C. § 2052(a)(5). It is an article. It is produced. It is distributed. It is used by consumers. It is used in and around households, schools, and recreational facilities. There is no statutory exemption for fundamental forces of nature. There is no exemption for articles that have been in distribution longer than the species using them. The statute covers “any article.” Gravity is an article. The statute applies.

III. The Hazard Data

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, through its National Vital Statistics System, reported 48,308 unintentional fall deaths in the United States in 2024.10 This exceeds the 41,241 motor vehicle traffic deaths recorded in the same period.11 Falls are now the leading mechanism of unintentional injury death in the United States, surpassed only by poisoning among all unintentional causes.

Every single one of these falls was caused by gravity. This is not an epidemiological inference. It is Newtonian mechanics. A fall is the involuntary acceleration of a body toward the center of the Earth under the force of gravity. Without gravity, the injury count is zero. The causal relationship is not correlational. It is not confounded by environmental factors. It is the direct, unmediated application of 9.8 meters per second squared to a consumer who has lost contact with a supporting surface. Gravity is not a contributing factor in fall injuries. It is the only factor. The ground does not rise to meet the consumer. The consumer is accelerated into the ground by the product under investigation.

Among adults aged 65 and older, the CDC reports that over 14 million, or one in four, report falling each year. Approximately 37 percent of those who fall sustain injuries requiring medical treatment or activity restriction, resulting in an estimated 9 million fall injuries annually in this age group alone.12 The age-adjusted fall death rate among older adults increased by 21 percent between 2018 and 2024, from 64.7 to 78.4 per 100,000 population.13

The National Hospital Ambulatory Medical Care Survey recorded 26.2 million emergency department visits for unintentional injuries in 2022, with falls constituting the leading mechanism.14 When all age groups are included, the annual toll of gravity-related injuries entering the emergency department system is estimated to exceed 8 million.15

The product’s hazard profile is not ambiguous. It is not emerging. It is not contested. It has been documented continuously since the invention of vital statistics. The product kills more Americans per year than any consumer product ever recalled.

Every single one of these 48,308 fall deaths was caused by gravity. This is not an epidemiological inference. It is Newtonian mechanics.

IV. The Recall Threshold Problem

The CPSC has the authority and the institutional practice of initiating recalls for products causing far fewer casualties than gravity. A brief survey of recent enforcement actions illustrates the agency’s operational thresholds.

In September 2016, the CPSC, in cooperation with Samsung Electronics America, announced the recall of approximately 1 million Galaxy Note7 smartphones after receiving 92 reports of batteries overheating, including 26 reports of burns and 55 reports of property damage. No deaths were reported.16 Zero deaths and 26 burn injuries were sufficient to trigger a recall of 1 million units.

In April 2019, the CPSC announced the recall of approximately 4.7 million Fisher-Price Rock ’n Play Sleepers after over 30 infant fatalities were reported since the product’s 2009 introduction.17 Thirty deaths over a ten-year period triggered the recall of 4.7 million units.

In May 2021, the CPSC announced the recall of approximately 125,000 Peloton Tread+ treadmills after one child death and more than 70 reported incidents, including injuries to children and pets pulled beneath the rear of the treadmill.18 One death and 70 incidents triggered the recall of 125,000 units.

The pattern is consistent. The CPSC initiates corrective action when a product causes injury or death at rates that are, by any actuarial standard, vanishingly small compared to the total units in distribution. A product causing 30 deaths over ten years is intolerable. A product causing 48,308 deaths in a single year has never been the subject of a staff briefing.

If the CPSC applied the Samsung Galaxy Note7 recall ratio—92 incidents of overheating per 1 million units triggering a full recall—to gravity, the incident-to-unit ratio would require the recall of every unit of gravity currently in circulation. There are approximately 336 million units of gravity in active consumer use in the United States, one per resident. The product’s incident rate is not 92 per million. It is 143,690 per million, based on the 48,308 deaths alone, without counting the millions of nonfatal injuries. This is 1,562 times the ratio that triggered the Note7 recall.

V. The Section 15(b) Reporting Failure

Under 15 U.S.C. § 2064(b), a manufacturer, distributor, or retailer who obtains information that reasonably supports the conclusion that its product contains a defect creating a substantial risk of injury to the public must immediately inform the Commission. The implementing regulations at 16 CFR § 1115.14 require this notification within 24 hours.19

The hazardous properties of gravity have been known with scientific precision since July 5, 1687, when Isaac Newton published the Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica, in which he described the universal law of gravitation and the constant acceleration of falling bodies.20 Newton did not file a Section 15(b) report. He received a knighthood.

Prior to Newton, Galileo Galilei published quantitative measurements of gravitational acceleration in his Discorsi e Dimostrazioni Matematiche (1638), demonstrating that all objects fall at the same rate regardless of mass.21 Galileo did not report this finding to a consumer safety agency. He reported it to the Medici court, which lacked jurisdiction.

As of April 21, 2026, 338 years and 290 days have elapsed since the publication of the Principia. No Section 15(b) report has been filed. The 24-hour reporting deadline has been exceeded by a factor of approximately 2,978,160. This is the longest continuous reporting violation in the history of consumer product safety regulation, though it is not without precedent in the broader universe of gravity-related delays.

The manufacturer of record—Earth—has not retained legal counsel. It has not designated a United States agent for service of process. It has not registered with the CPSC under 15 U.S.C. § 2070. The reporting obligation, which attaches to any party in the distribution chain, remains unfulfilled by every entity with knowledge of the hazard, which at this point includes the entire scientific community, every medical examiner who has certified a fall death, and the Commission itself.

VI. The Corrective Action Impossibility

When the CPSC determines that a product presents a substantial product hazard, Section 15(d) of the CPSA authorizes the Commission to order the manufacturer, distributor, or retailer to undertake one or more of the following corrective actions: (1) bring the product into conformity with applicable safety standards; (2) replace the product with a conforming product; or (3) refund the purchase price of the product.22

Repair. Gravity operates at a fixed output of 9.80665 m/s². There is no adjustment mechanism. The product has no firmware, no software, no modular components. Its output is determined by the mass of the Earth and the inverse square of the distance from its center. Reducing the hazard would require either reducing the Earth’s mass or increasing the consumer’s distance from the center of gravity. The former would require the removal of approximately 1.2 × 1024 kilograms of material, or roughly 20 percent of the planet, to reduce surface gravity to a level where the median fall from standing height would not produce a traumatic brain injury.23 The latter would require relocating all consumers to an altitude of approximately 1,200 kilometers, which presents independent habitability challenges. Neither option constitutes a practical repair within the meaning of the statute.

Replacement. The CPSC may order replacement of the hazardous product with a conforming product. There is no conforming replacement for gravity. Electromagnetic force is too strong and selective. The strong nuclear force operates only at subatomic distances. The weak nuclear force is involved in radioactive decay and is not suitable for holding consumers to the surface of a planet. No alternative fundamental interaction of nature is available at retail or through the manufacturer.

Refund. Consumers have never paid a purchase price for gravity. It is distributed without charge. However, consumers have incurred substantial costs arising from its use. In 2015, the estimated medical costs attributable to fatal and nonfatal falls among adults aged 65 and older alone was approximately $50 billion, with Medicare paying $28.9 billion, Medicaid $8.7 billion, and private and other payers covering $12.0 billion.24 Adjusted for medical cost inflation and the 21 percent increase in the fall death rate since 2018, the current annual cost exceeds $65 billion for this age group alone. A refund of the costs imposed by this product would constitute the largest consumer remedy in the history of American commerce. The manufacturer has no known revenue stream from which to fund it.

There is no conforming replacement for gravity. Electromagnetic force is too strong. The weak nuclear force is involved in radioactive decay and is not suitable for holding consumers to the surface of a planet.

VII. The Labeling Deficiency

Under 15 U.S.C. § 2063, consumer products must comply with all applicable labeling and certification requirements. The Federal Hazardous Substances Act (FHSA), 15 U.S.C. § 1261 et seq., requires that hazardous substances bear cautionary labeling that is “adequate for the protection of the public health and safety,” including the common or chemical name of the hazardous substance, an affirmative statement of the principal hazard or hazards, precautionary measures to follow, instructions for first-aid treatment, and the statement “Keep out of the reach of children.”25

Gravity bears no label. It carries no warnings. It provides no instructions for safe use. It does not identify itself by name, chemical formula, or trade designation. It does not state its principal hazard, which is the involuntary acceleration of consumers toward rigid surfaces at velocities sufficient to cause death. It provides no first-aid instructions. It does not include the statement “Keep out of the reach of children,” despite the fact that falls are the leading cause of nonfatal injury among children aged 0 to 19 in the United States.26

The product is equally available to newborn infants and 95-year-old consumers with osteoporosis. It makes no accommodation for the vulnerability of the user. It does not reduce its output for consumers with known risk factors, including advanced age, impaired balance, medication use, or visual impairment. A consumer using a Samsung Galaxy Note7 was warned, prior to the recall, that the battery might overheat. A consumer using gravity receives no warning that a loss of balance at standing height can produce a hip fracture with a one-year mortality rate of 21 to 30 percent.27

The labeling failure is total. The product has been distributed for 4.54 billion years without a single cautionary notice. The nearest equivalent in consumer product history would be if every bottle of bleach in every American household had been sold without a label since the Hadean Eon.

VIII. The Manufacturer Problem

Under the CPSA, corrective action orders are directed to “any manufacturer, distributor, or retailer” of the hazardous product. The manufacturer of gravity is the Earth. The Earth has a mass of 5.972 × 1024 kg, an age of approximately 4.54 billion years, and no legal personality.28

The Earth is not incorporated in any U.S. state. It is not registered as a foreign entity doing business in the United States. It does not maintain a registered agent. It has never filed a Form 8(a) product report with the CPSC. It has no CEO, no board of directors, no general counsel, and no customer service department. It does not respond to subpoenas. Previous attempts to serve legal process on geological formations have not been successful.29

The CPSC’s enforcement toolkit assumes a manufacturer that is, at minimum, a legal person. Section 15 corrective action orders, Section 17 judicial enforcement proceedings, and Section 20 civil penalty assessments all require a respondent capable of receiving notice and complying with legal obligations. The Earth meets none of these procedural requirements. It is not in contempt of court. It is not even in court.

The distributors of gravity are equally unidentifiable. Unlike conventional supply chains, where distribution can be traced from factory to warehouse to retail outlet, gravity is distributed instantaneously and uniformly across the entire surface of the manufacturer. There is no intermediary. There is no logistics network. There is no last-mile delivery problem, because delivery occurs at the speed of light and has never once been late.30

The practical consequence is that the CPSC cannot identify a respondent to whom it can direct a corrective action order. The product is hazardous. The statute authorizes a remedy. The remedy requires a manufacturer. The manufacturer is a planet. The enforcement gap is not bureaucratic. It is geological.

IX. The Economic Exposure

The economic costs of gravity-related injuries are staggering by any consumer product standard. A 2018 study published in the Journal of the American Geriatrics Society by researchers at the CDC’s National Center for Injury Prevention and Control estimated the total medical cost of fall injuries among older adults at approximately $50 billion per year in 2015 dollars.31 This figure covers only adults aged 65 and older and only direct medical expenditures. It does not include lost productivity, long-term care costs, caregiver burden, or quality-of-life impairment.

When injuries across all age groups are included—workplace falls, childhood falls, recreational falls, and falls in institutional settings—the National Safety Council estimates that the total cost of fall injuries exceeds $170 billion annually in combined medical costs, wage and productivity losses, and administrative expenses.32

For context, the CPSC assessed $51.6 million in civil penalties across all enforcement actions in fiscal year 2023, covering the full universe of hazardous consumer products under its jurisdiction.33 The annual economic damage attributable to gravity exceeds the agency’s total annual penalty assessments by a factor of approximately 3,300. The agency’s entire annual budget—approximately $176 million in fiscal year 2025—would cover the medical costs of gravity-related injuries for approximately 23 hours.34

Under 15 U.S.C. § 2069, the CPSC may impose civil penalties of up to $100,000 per violation, with a maximum of $15 million for any related series of violations. If each of the 48,308 deaths in 2024 constitutes a separate violation of the reporting requirement, the aggregate penalty would be $4.83 billion. This amount is approximately 27 times the agency’s annual budget and approximately 7 percent of the annual medical costs the product inflicts. The penalty structure was not designed for a product that kills at this scale. No penalty structure was.

X. The International Dimension

Gravity does not respect national borders. It operates with identical force in every jurisdiction on Earth, subject to minor local variations of less than 0.5 percent attributable to latitude, altitude, and geological density.35 It is simultaneously a domestic product (produced within U.S. territory) and a foreign product (produced by the entire planet, including portions lying outside U.S. sovereign jurisdiction). It cannot be subjected to an import ban, because it does not cross a border. It is already present in every customs district before any border is drawn.

The World Health Organization estimates that approximately 684,000 people die from falls globally each year, making falls the second leading cause of unintentional injury death worldwide after road traffic injuries.36 No nation has initiated a product safety enforcement action against gravity. No international treaty addresses the regulation of fundamental physical forces. The United Nations Convention on the Safety of Consumer Products does not exist, though if it did, gravity would be the first item on the agenda.

The absence of international cooperation on this matter is not surprising. Gravity’s manufacturer has never attended a bilateral trade negotiation. It does not participate in standards-setting bodies. It does not comply with ISO, ANSI, ASTM, or any other voluntary consensus standard. It operates under its own standard, which has not been updated since the formation of the planet and is not open for public comment.

XI. The Agency Capacity Problem

The CPSC issued 312 recalls in fiscal year 2023, a 20 percent increase over the prior year and a record for the agency.37 The agency employs approximately 560 full-time equivalent staff across its headquarters and field offices.38 This workforce manages the safety of an estimated 15,000 types of consumer products in commerce in the United States.

Adding gravity to the agency’s product portfolio would increase the mortality attributable to CPSC-jurisdictional products by a factor that the agency’s current staffing model cannot accommodate. The 312 recalls issued in FY2023 addressed products collectively responsible for fewer than 500 reported deaths. Gravity alone would increase the agency’s mortality caseload by approximately 9,600 percent.

Enforcement would require inspectors with access to every square foot of U.S. territory, because the product is in use everywhere. A compliance inspection of gravity would require measuring its output at each consumer-accessible location—a task that is, in fairness, already performed by the National Geodetic Survey, which maintains a gravity reference network of approximately 1.4 million stations.39 The data confirms that gravity is operating within its normal parameters everywhere in the country. The problem is not that it is malfunctioning. The problem is that its normal parameters are lethal.

This is, under the statute, irrelevant. A product need not malfunction to be defective. Under Section 15(a)(2), a “product defect” includes any defect that “creates a substantial risk of injury to the public.” The CPSC’s own interpretive guidance notes that a product operating as designed may still be defective if the design itself creates an unreasonable risk of injury.40 Gravity operates exactly as designed. Its design kills 48,308 Americans per year. The design is the defect.

XII. Conclusion

The Consumer Product Safety Act defines a consumer product as any article produced or distributed for use by a consumer. Gravity is produced by the Earth. It is distributed to every household, school, and recreational facility in the United States. It is used by every consumer in the country, continuously, without interruption, 24 hours per day, 365 days per year.

The CPSA defines a substantial product hazard as a product defect that creates a substantial risk of injury to the public. Gravity causes 48,308 deaths per year—more than motor vehicle traffic fatalities, more than all consumer products ever recalled by the CPSC combined, and more than any single product in the history of American commerce. The medical costs exceed $50 billion annually for older adults alone. The total economic burden exceeds $170 billion.

The CPSC recalls products that cause single-digit deaths. It recalls products that cause zero deaths and 26 burn injuries. It recalls products that overheat. Gravity does not overheat. It does not malfunction. It functions with the same lethal precision it has maintained for 4.54 billion years. It has never been recalled, suspended, modified, labeled, or warned about by any consumer safety agency on Earth.

The manufacturer cannot be identified, served, or compelled. The product cannot be repaired, replaced, or refunded. The labeling cannot be applied, because there is no surface on which to affix it that is not itself subject to the product’s force. The international community has not addressed it. The regulatory framework does not contemplate it. The penalty structure cannot contain it.

The most dangerous consumer product in American history is not a sleeper, a smartphone, or a treadmill. It is a fundamental force of nature operating at 9.8 meters per second squared, available without restriction to every man, woman, and child in the United States, with a perfect 4.54-billion-year safety record of causing harm and a perfect 4.54-billion-year regulatory record of avoiding accountability.
