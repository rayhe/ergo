# 7-Critic Panel Evaluation: "Dairy Cows as Unpermitted Title V Major Sources"

## 1. General Editor — 9.0/10
The article follows the established Ergo format precisely: absurd-yet-rigorous mega-headline, scholarly deck, Roman-numeral sections building the case from statute to source to plume to scale to absurd-result to cow-tax to Supreme Court reproof to appropriations rider to penalty, genuine footnotes with a Sources section, and the deadpan "Ergo." close. Structure escalates with real dramatic logic: the arithmetic is introduced, confirmed by the USDA's own 2008 analysis (25.9 cows, rounded to 25), conceded by the EPA's Tailoring Rule, rebuked by the Supreme Court, and then mooted by Congress — each institution declining to do what the statute's text appears to demand. The satellite plume in Section III gives the piece a cinematic exhibit the genre needs. Minor weakness: Section VIII's rider discussion and Section IX's penalty math slightly overlap in the "Congress forbids it" beat, and the piece leans on the reader to hold the 100 vs. 100,000 ton contrast across four sections.

## 2. Voice Coach — 9.5/10
Dead-serious investigative tone throughout, matching the mosquito and beaver pieces in the administrative-law register. Humor emerges exclusively from deadpan precision, never from jokes. Standout lines: "The monitoring system is the nose of anyone downwind," "The plume is measured in tons per hour. The permit file is measured in pages: zero," "The agency did not amend the statute. It could not," "Congress barred the agency from spending a dollar to permit water vapor from livestock, a substance no one proposed to permit, out of what can only be described as an abundance of caution," "The violators are ruminants. The statute does not know this. The statute knows only tons." Excellent voice match; the piece never winks.

## 3. Ethics — 9.0/10
All statutes and regulations cited are real: 42 U.S.C. § 7661a (Title V permit requirement), 42 U.S.C. § 7661(2) (major source, 100 tpy), the EPA's Tailoring Rule with its 75,000/100,000 tpy CO2e thresholds and 6.1-million-source concession, *Utility Air Regulatory Group v. EPA*, 573 U.S. 302 (2014), with the "no power to tailor" holding quoted verbatim, the FY2010 appropriations rider (P.L. 111-88, §424) and its annual renewal barring Title V permits for livestock biological-process emissions, and the 42 U.S.C. § 7413(b) penalty schedule inflation-adjusted to $124,426/day. Scientific facts verified: IPCC Tier 1 default of 138 kg CH4/head/year for North American dairy cattle, methane GWP of 28 (AR5), ~95% of enteric methane via eructation, 150–600 liters/day (UNL), the GHGSat feedlot detection (977–1,472 lbs/hr), USDA's 87.2 million head / 9.36 million milk cows, EPA's 192.6 MMT CO2e enteric fermentation figure for 2022, and the 2022 Census of Agriculture dairy consolidation numbers. The piece is honest about the definitional escape hatch: the Supreme Court itself held the Act neither compels nor permits GHG-only Title V permitting, and Congress affirmatively bars the permits — the deliberately wrong conclusion (cows must be permitted) is the disclosed conceit, stated openly in Sections VII and VIII. No ethical concerns.

## 4. Social/Shareability — 9.5/10
"Cows need Clean Air Act permits" is immediately graspable and the cow-tax history gives it political bite: the USDA agreed with the math (25 cows), the Farm Bureau priced it ($175/cow), and Congress banned the permits for sixteen straight years. "The satellite can pinpoint the facility. The permitting authority cannot produce the permit" and the water-vapor rider aside are strong shareable moments. The 95%-burps-not-farts correction is evergreen share bait. Higher share potential than the mosquito piece because the premise requires no regulatory setup — everyone knows what a permit is, and the 100 vs. 100,000 ton contrast fits in a headline.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 9.0/10
The Clean Air Act citations are accurate: Title V at 42 U.S.C. § 7661a, the 100 tpy major-source definition, the PSD 100/250 tpy thresholds, the Tailoring Rule's phased 75,000/100,000 tpy CO2e thresholds, and the *UARG* holding quoted from the slip opinion (the "stand on the dock and wave goodbye" passage and the "no power to tailor" principle). The rider's text — "in this or any other Act," covering CO2, N2O, water vapor, and methane from livestock biological processes — is quoted from the CRS summaries of the enacted appropriations language. The § 7413(b) inflation-adjusted penalty ($124,426, January 2025 rule) is taken from the Federal Register table. One standard Ergo deduction: the piece's core inference (a dairy operation is a "stationary source" whose "potential to emit" counts enteric fermentation) is the creative leap the genre requires, and the piece discloses the counter-authorities (UARG's GHG-only holding, the rider) rather than hiding them.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0/10
Strong sourcing across independent authorities: govinfo/CRS for the appropriations rider, law.cornell.edu for the *UARG* slip opinion, EPA's own RIA and inventory documents for the 6-million-source concession and the 192.6 MMT figure, the IPCC 2019 refinement (via peer-reviewed literature) for the 138 kg factor, EPA's methane overview for GWP 28, UNL and the Greenhouse Gas Management Institute for the eructation mechanics, GHGSat via Modern Farmer for the satellite detection, USDA NASS for the herd inventory, the Census of Agriculture via farmdoc for consolidation, and Senate sources for the cow-tax history. The per-cow math (138 kg × 28 = 3.86 t; 100 ÷ 3.86 = 25.9) is shown, not asserted, and independently matches the USDA's 2008 "more than 25 dairy cows" figure — the strongest internal consistency check in the piece. Minor weakness: the IPCC factor comes via a secondary (MDPI) paper restating the Tier 1 default rather than the IPCC guidelines themselves.

## 7. Data Presentation — 9.0/10
Numbers are presented with consistent precision and always sourced: 138 kg/head/year, GWP 28, 3.86 t CO2e/cow/year, 25.9 cows to the threshold, 150–600 liters/day, 95% eructation, 977–1,472 lbs/hr satellite plume (5,116 t/yr), 87.2 million head, 9.36 million milk cows, 192.6 MMT CO2e (2022), 36.6% of U.S. methane, 9.4% of total GHG, 24,094 dairy farms, 2,013 with 1,000+ cows (66% of sales), 834 with 2,500+ head (~9,660 t/yr each), 6.1 million vs. 14,700 permit applications, 75,000/100,000 tpy tailored thresholds, $175/$87.50/$20 per-head permit costs, $13,000–$22,000 per medium dairy, $124,426/day penalties, $45M+/year theoretical exposure per large dairy. The comparison framework — the statute's number against every institution's refusal to enforce it — is devastating and verifiable. Clear, consistent, sourced.
