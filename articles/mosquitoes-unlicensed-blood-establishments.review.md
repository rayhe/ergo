# 7-Critic Panel Evaluation: "Mosquitoes as Unlicensed Blood Establishments"

## 1. General Editor — 9.0/10
The article follows the established Ergo format precisely: absurd-yet-rigorous headline, scholarly deck, Roman-numeral sections building a systematic case from statute to operator to method to scale to penalty, genuine footnotes with a Sources section, and the deadpan "Ergo." close. Structure escalates naturally: the registration framework, the operator's biology, the pooling mechanism, the eligibility and testing regimes the operator ignores, the scale of the unregistered operation, the labeling and deviation-reporting failures, the penalty schedule, and the conclusion. The West Nile irony in Section V, that the FDA screens the blood supply against a pathogen the unregistered operator distributes, is the piece's load-bearing beam and it holds. Minor weakness: Section VIII's penalty discussion leans on the statutory schedule without a human comparator case the way the beaver piece had the Alaska landowner; the asymmetry lands slightly softer than the genre's best closers.

## 2. Voice Coach — 9.5/10
Dead-serious investigative tone throughout, matching the fluoridation and cat-purr device articles in the FDA-law register. The humor emerges exclusively from deadpan precision, never from jokes. Standout lines: "The word is *all*," "The deferral rate is zero. The notification rate is zero," "The mosquito's donors are frequently asleep at the time of collection," "The one required statement the product arguably satisfies: 'This product may transmit infectious agents,'" "The enforcement file contains no warning letter, no consent decree, and no inspection, because the establishment has never registered and therefore does not exist in the agency's records." The regulation provides no classification for the involuntary donor, the sleeping donor, or the donor who was never asked. Excellent voice match; the piece never winks.

## 3. Ethics — 9.0/10
All statutes and regulations cited are real: 21 CFR 607.7 registration requirement ("All owners or operators..."), the FDA's 5-day registration and June/December listing update schedule, 21 CFR 630.10 donor eligibility and consent provisions, 21 CFR 610.40 testing tiers (HIV, HBV, HCV; HTLV, syphilis, West Nile virus, Chagas), 21 CFR 606.121 pooling traceability and donor-classification labeling, the 45-day biological product deviation reporting rule, and 21 U.S.C. § 333 penalty schedules, all verified against govinfo.gov, law.cornell.edu, and fda.gov. Biological facts verified: female-only blood feeding and salivary anticoagulant proteins (phys.org/Kanazawa University), 0.001–0.01 mL blood meals (Coachella Valley MVCD), double and triple blood meals within a gonotrophic cycle (PLOS NTDs), WHO 249 million cases and 608,000 deaths in 2022, the 725,000 annual deaths figure (Smithsonian/Gates), WHO's 100–400 million dengue infections, and CDC's 2023 locally acquired U.S. malaria cases. The article clearly serves Ergo's educational mission: the deliberately wrong conclusion, that mosquitoes are blood establishments, is the disclosed conceit, and the piece is honest about the definitional escape hatch, an insect is not an establishment, in the same way the cicada piece disclosed its own. No ethical concerns.

## 4. Social/Shareability — 9.0/10
"Mosquitoes are running an unlicensed blood bank" is immediately graspable and viscerally funny; the pooling detail, that up to 42 percent of mosquitoes carry double blood meals, gives readers a concrete horror to share. The West Nile irony is social media gold: the FDA tests every donated unit for a virus the mosquito injects directly. "The donors are frequently asleep" and the paid/volunteer/involuntary donor classification bit create strong shareable moments. Slightly less universally graspable than "the Moon is unlicensed" because the premise requires one paragraph of FDA registration setup, the deck does that work, but the headline carries the whole burden alone. Still very high share potential.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 8.5/10
The 21 CFR citations are accurate: 607.7's "all owners or operators" language, the 5-day registration and November 15–December 31 annual renewal with June and December listing updates, 607.65 exemptions, 630.10's eligibility, consent, and withdrawal provisions including 630.10(h)'s prohibition on collecting from ineligible donors, 610.40's testing tiers with West Nile virus correctly placed in the second tier, 606.121(c)(3)'s pooling traceability language quoted verbatim, the paid/volunteer donor classification statement, and the 45-day Form FDA-3486 deviation reporting window. 21 U.S.C. § 333(a)'s one-year/$1,000 and three-year/$10,000 schedules are correct. The article's deliberately wrong conclusion, that a mosquito is an "establishment that manufactures blood products," is the genre's disclosed conceit, and the piece never pretends the CFR defines insects as establishments. Passes threshold, with the standard Ergo deduction for creative inference from regulatory text.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0/10
Strong sourcing across independent authorities: govinfo.gov for the CFR and U.S. Code, law.cornell.edu for the e-CFR text, fda.gov for the registration guidance, peer-reviewed entomology (PLOS Neglected Tropical Diseases on multiple feeding; Scientific Reports via phys.org on salivary proteins), a county mosquito-control agency for species counts and bite mechanics, the Coachella Valley MVCD for blood-meal volume, WHO for malaria and dengue burden, Smithsonian for the Gates mortality figures, and CDC for domestic malaria surveillance. Minor weakness shared with the cicada piece: the key biological evidence on pooling comes from field studies with wide ranges (0–32% double meals in Thailand), which the article reports as ranges rather than overclaiming point estimates. The article is transparent about its sources throughout.

## 7. Data Presentation — 9.0/10
Numbers are presented with consistent precision and always sourced: 0.001–0.01 mL per meal against the bathtub equivalent, up to 42% double and 5% triple blood meals, 3,000+ species with 176 in the U.S., 249 million cases and 608,000 deaths in 2022 with 94%/95% African share and ~80% under-five mortality share, 725,000 annual deaths against 475,000 murders and fewer than 10 shark fatalities, 100–400 million dengue infections, 10 locally acquired U.S. malaria cases in 2023, 5-day registration, 45-day deviation reporting, and the one-year/$1,000 and three-year/$10,000 penalty tiers. The comparison framework, registered establishments versus the unregistered operator on every metric, is devastating and verifiable. Clear, consistent, sourced.
