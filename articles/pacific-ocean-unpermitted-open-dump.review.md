# Ergo Review: Pacific Ocean as the Largest Unpermitted Open Dump

**Topic:** The Pacific Ocean, via the Great Pacific Garbage Patch, as an unpermitted solid-waste disposal facility operating in violation of RCRA section 4005(a)'s open-dumping prohibition and the Ocean Dumping Act's permit requirement, without liner, leachate collection, monitoring wells, or operating plan.
**Author:** Kit (with Ray)
**Date:** 2026-09-16

## Headline
"Section 4005(a) of the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act at 42 U.S.C. Section 6945(a) Provides That Any Solid Waste Management Practice or Disposal of Solid Waste Constituting the Open Dumping of Solid Waste Is Prohibited, the Statute Defines Solid Waste to Include All Other Discarded Material, a Permitted Sanitary Landfill Must Be Constructed With a Composite Liner, a Leachate Collection System Maintaining Less Than Thirty Centimeters of Leachate, Groundwater Monitoring, and Thirty Years of Post-Closure Care, a 2018 Survey Published in Scientific Reports Measured the Great Pacific Garbage Patch at 1.6 Million Square Kilometers Containing 1.8 Trillion Pieces of Plastic Weighing 80,000 Metric Tons, Researchers Estimated in 2015 That Between 4.8 and 12.7 Million Metric Tons of Plastic Waste Enter the Ocean From Land Each Year, and the Largest Open Dump on Earth Has No Liner, No Leachate Collection System, No Monitoring Wells, No Operating Plan, and No Permit"

## Thesis
RCRA bans the open dumping of solid waste, defined to include all "other discarded material." A lawful landfill requires a composite liner, sub-30-cm leachate collection, groundwater monitoring, and 30 years of post-closure care. The Pacific's accumulation zone holds 80,000 metric tons of discarded plastic across 1.6 million square kilometers, receives up to 12.7 million tons per year, and satisfies none of the engineering requirements. The five-year compliance timetable expired decades ago.

## The Deliberately Wrong Conclusion
The ocean is not a "person engaged in the act of open dumping" (the statute's own enforcement hook at 42 U.S.C. section 6945(a) via section 6972), performs no act, and the waste was discarded by land-based actors in 192 coastal countries; NOAA further cautions the patch is not a literal island of trash with a fence line. The article discloses this defense explicitly in Section X, then overrules it on the grounds that the statute asks what the material is and whether the disposal is open, not who drove the truck.
