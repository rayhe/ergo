# Ergo Review: Grocery Stores as Unlicensed Nuclear Facilities

**Topic:** Grocery stores as unlicensed nuclear facilities under 10 CFR Part 30, via the Schedule B catch-all exempt quantity for unlisted beta emitters (0.1 microcuries = 3,700 Bq) applied to potassium-40 in bananas, potassium-chloride salt substitute, and the shoppers themselves.
**Author:** Kit (with Ray)
**Date:** 2026-09-18

## Headline
"Section 30.3 of Title 10 of the Code of Federal Regulations Provides That No Person Shall Receive, Acquire, Own, Possess, or Use Byproduct Material Except as Authorized in a Specific or General License, Section 30.71 Schedule B Lists the Exempt Quantity for Every Licensed Isotope and Provides That Any Byproduct Material Not Listed Above, Other Than Alpha Emitting Byproduct Material, Is Exempt Only Up to One-Tenth of One Microcurie, Potassium-40 Is Not Listed Above, One-Tenth of One Microcurie Is 3,700 Becquerels, a Single Banana Contains Approximately 15 Becquerels of Potassium-40, Two Hundred and Forty-Seven Bananas Therefore Exceed the Exempt Quantity, a Standard 40-Pound Box of Bananas Contains Approximately 2,360 Becquerels, Two Boxes Exceed the Exempt Quantity, a Pallet Holds Roughly Thirty Exempt Quantities, an 11-Ounce Canister of Potassium-Chloride Salt Substitute Contains Approximately 4,700 Becquerels, More Than One Exempt Quantity Per Canister, the 70-Kilogram Human Body Produces Approximately 4,400 Potassium-40 Decays Per Second, More Than One Exempt Quantity Per Shopper, Paragraph (c) of Section 30.18 Withholds the Exempt-Quantity Authorization From Commercial Distribution, the Grocery Store Is Engaged in Commercial Distribution, and No Grocery Store in America Holds a License, Employs a Radiation Safety Officer, or Has Ever Filed a Single Form With the Commission"

## Thesis
Section 30.3 prohibits receiving, acquiring, owning, possessing, transferring, or using byproduct material without a license. Schedule B does not list potassium-40, so the catch-all exempt quantity for unlisted beta emitters applies: 0.1 microcuries, or 3,700 becquerels. A banana is 15 becquerels; 247 bananas exceed the exempt quantity; a 40-pound box is 2,360 becquerels; a pallet holds roughly thirty exempt quantities; a canister of potassium-chloride salt substitute holds more than one; a 70-kilogram shopper carries 4,400. Section 30.18(c) withholds the exempt-quantity authorization from commercial distribution, which is the grocery store's entire business. No store holds a license, employs a radiation safety officer, or labels the immediate container with the isotope. The produce department is commercial distribution with a loyalty card program.

## The Deliberately Wrong Conclusion
Naturally occurring potassium-40 in food is outside NRC jurisdiction: the Commission's NARM final rule states its authority "is not intended to extend to all naturally occurring radioactive material, specifically not to naturally occurring radioactive material that is found in nature in its original form and location," and a regulated discrete source must be purposefully concentrated for commercial, medical, or research use. The dose is trivial and non-cumulative through homeostasis. The article discloses this defense explicitly in Section XI, then overrules it on the grounds that the schedule asks one question about isotopes, not bananas, and the answer is 3,700 becquerels.

## Research Notes
- 10 CFR 30.3(a) quoted verbatim from Cornell ("no person shall manufacture, produce, transfer, receive, acquire, own, possess, or use byproduct material except as authorized in a specific or general license").
- 10 CFR 30.71 Schedule B verified on Cornell: K-42 and K-43 listed, K-40 not listed; final catch-all "Any byproduct material not listed above other than alpha emitting byproduct material" at 0.1 microcuries.
- 0.1 uCi = 3,700 Bq; K-40 decays by beta emission to Ca-40 (89% branch) plus electron capture to Ar-40, so the alpha exclusion does not apply.
- Banana: ~15 Bq K-40 per banana and 0.1 uSv banana equivalent dose (EN Wikipedia); ~130 Bq/kg, 19.5 Bq per 150 g banana (FR Wikipedia); 0.0117% isotopic abundance and ~31 Bq/g natural potassium (ZH Wikipedia). Cross-checked: 130 Bq/kg x 0.15 kg = 19.5 Bq, consistent with 15-20 Bq per banana.
- BED originated on the RadSafe mailing list in 1995 via a Lawrence Livermore physicist (EN Wikipedia).
- ERS: bananas most popular fresh fruit 2021, 13.2 lb/person loss-adjusted availability.
- 40-lb boxes: Produce Market Guide (38-40 lb standard); PA eMarketplace solicitation specifies 40-pound cartons.
- NoSalt 11 oz: potassium chloride first ingredient, 640 mg potassium per 1-g serving, ~239 servings per canister (retail listing). Arithmetic: 239 x 640 mg = ~153 g K x 31 Bq/g = ~4,740 Bq per canister.
- 70-kg man: ~4,400 K-40 decays/sec (EDN "False alarm 2").
- 10 CFR 30.18(c) quoted verbatim via NRC HPPOS-131 (which quotes the paragraph and confirms facilities possessing exempt quantities need no license "provided it does not plan on possession for the purposes outlined in 30.18(c) and (d)").
- Labeling: 10 CFR 32.19(c) requires the immediate container of each quantity to bear a durable legible label identifying the radioisotope and quantity and the words "Radioactive Material"; 32.19(d) adds "Not for Human Use - Introduction Into Foods ... is Prohibited." Review pass caught and corrected an initial mis-citation of 32.20(c); verified against NRC licensee RAI letters and the Part 32 supporting statement.
- GAO: first-generation portal monitors "can be set off even by shipments of bananas, kitty litter, or granite tile" (nsarchive EBB020c PDF).
- Nextgov: PNNL recalibrated 1,300+ CBP portal monitors, 230,000 fewer alarms/year, $55M projected 10-year savings.
- EDN: boxcars of bananas tripped a detector; operators built a concrete block wall to shield it.
- Brazil nuts: most radioactive food, radium up to 44 Bq/kg (PT Wikipedia).
- NRC NARM final rule: authority excludes NORM "found in nature in its original form and location, or ... moved or concentrated inadvertently"; discrete source requires purposeful concentration for commercial/medical/research use (scp.nrc.gov PDF).
- Homeostasis: K-40 dose not cumulative (EN Wikipedia BED).
