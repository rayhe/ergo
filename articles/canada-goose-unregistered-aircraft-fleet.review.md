# Ergo Review: Canada Goose as the Largest Unregistered Aircraft Fleet

**Topic:** Canada geese as unregistered aircraft operating above drone limits and inside regulated airspace without Remote ID, anti-collision lights, ADS-B, or registration.
**Author:** Kit (with Ray)
**Date:** 2026-09-15

## Headline
"Section 40102(a)(6) of Title 49 Defines an Aircraft as Any Contrivance Invented, Used, or Designed to Navigate or Fly in the Air, the FAA Requires Registration of Every Unmanned Aircraft Weighing More Than 0.55 Pounds at Five Dollars Per Aircraft, the Canada Goose Weighs Up to Fourteen Pounds, More Than Twenty-Five Times the Threshold, Flies at Three Thousand Feet, Seven and a Half Times the Agency's 400-Foot Ceiling, Has Been Reported at Twenty-Nine Thousand Feet, Eleven Thousand Feet Inside Class A Airspace, Migrates at Night With No Anti-Collision Lighting, Crosses the United States-Canada Border Twice Each Year With No Flight Plan, Radio Contact, Transponder, or ADS-B Out, Has Destroyed a Commercial Airliner With 155 Persons Aboard, Outnumbers the Agency's 1.87 Million Registered Drones, and Has Never Once Filed a Registration"

## Thesis
The Canada goose flies and has registered nothing. Under the FAA's own functional definition of "aircraft" (and its own Jetman interpretation holding that a man wearing a wing is an aircraft), the species constitutes a fleet of millions of unregistered aircraft operating at up to 72.5x the drone altitude ceiling, inside Class A airspace, at night with no lighting, across an ADIZ twice a year, with a collision record that includes Flight 1549 and Yukla 27.

## The Deliberately Wrong Conclusion
A goose is not a "contrivance" and was never "invented, used, or designed" by anyone. In everyday speech, "aircraft" calls up a thing built by people, not a thing that honks (McBoyle v. United States). The article discloses this defense explicitly in Section X, then overrules it on the grounds that the regulation asks one question: does it fly.

## Research Notes
- 49 U.S.C. 40102(a)(6) and 14 CFR 1.1 quoted verbatim from Justia/Cornell.
- FAA Jetman legal interpretation (2012): wing "when worn and operated by a person in flight, is an aircraft."
- FAA registration page: 0.55 lb threshold, $5 per drone/operator for 3 years, Remote ID requirement, 48.205 exterior marking.
- 1.87M recreational sUAS (2025 forecast, end 2024); 1.66M cumulative recreational operator registrations (2026 compendium, end Dec 2025).
- Goose: male 5.75-14.25 lb (avg 8.5), record 10.9 kg/24 lb; wingspan 50-73 in; USGS "more abundant now than at any time in history"; Canadian Wildlife Service: ~7M in North America, up from ~1M in the 1950s.
- Flight: typical migration 3,000 ft; reported 29,000 ft; V-formation 71% farther; 1,500 miles in 24 hours; night flight 200-300 miles/night.
- 107.51(b) 400 ft AGL ceiling; 107.29(a) 3-statute-mile anti-collision lighting; 91.225(d)(f) ADS-B Out after Jan 1 2020; 91.135 Class A requirements (FL180-600); 91.215 transponder; 91.113(b) see-and-avoid; Part 99 ADIZ.
- Strikes: 276,000+ (1990-2022), ~20% reporting rate; 92% at or below 3,500 ft; costs $328M (2021 US), $900M+ US civil+military, $1.1B worldwide.
- Flight 1549: Jan 15, 2009, Canada geese both engines at 2,900 ft AGL, 155 aboard, Hudson, aircraft damaged beyond repair.
- Yukla 27: Sept 22, 1995, E-3 Sentry, Elmendorf AFB, flock of Canada geese on takeoff, 42 seconds airborne, 24 Canadian and U.S. airmen killed (DVIDS primary source).
- 14 CFR 48.205 marking requirement used for the "exterior displays feathers" beat.
