# Review: clouds-unreported-weather-modification.html
**Date:** 2026-09-10
**Reviewer panel:** 7 critics
**Draft reviewed:** full article + hero image + metadata

## Critic scores

### 1. Legal Precisionist — 9.0/10
The §908.1(c), §908.2, §908.3(a)/(b)/(c), and §908.10 quotations track the govinfo CFR text verbatim, as does the §330d penalty quotation from the U.S. Code. The 10-day initial report, 45-day interim/final report schedule matches 15 CFR §§ 908.4–908.6 via the GAO and legalclarity corroboration. The §330a duty is paraphrased rather than quoted, which is honest but slightly less rigorous than the regulation block quotations. Deduction: the headline says reports are due "at least ten days before acting"; the regulation says the initial report must be received at least 10 days before commencement, which the headline matches, so the deduction is small and limited to the paraphrase point.

### 2. Arithmetic Auditor — 9.0/10
Verified all computations: 500,000,000 grams = 500,000 kg = 1.1 million pounds. 1,025 reports over 25 years ≈ 41 per year, supporting "roughly forty." 50,000× the cumulus water volume = 25,000,000,000 kg = 25 million tonnes, consistent with the cited figure. $10,000 × 19,710 days (Dec. 18, 1971 to Sept. 10, 2026) ≈ $197.1 million, supporting "roughly $197 million." 403,500 + 116,500 = 520,000 km³, matching "some 520,000 cubic kilometers." Deduction: the "19,700 missed final reports" is satirical arithmetic layered over a real per-activity deadline; it is framed as deadpan rather than fact, but the auditor notes the seam.

### 3. Source Verifier — 9.5/10
All 18 source notes map to URLs copied verbatim from fresh search results (Sept 10, 2026): govinfo CFR PDFs for the regulation text, the NOAA library FAQ, the GAO-26-108013 PDF, the Scientific Data / PMC paper, the CPR interview with the GAO director, the TDLR fact sheet, the Texas Agriculture Code on Justia, the bill status page, NASA, USGS-via-EarthDate, and the water-budget reporting. Each factual claim maps to a numbered source. Deduction: two claims lean on secondary reporting (zmescience, EarthDate) where the primary work exists; both identify the primary researchers by name (LeMone/NCAR, USGS), so traceability is preserved, and the nature.com URL carries search-result query parameters while the PMC copy is used in parallel.

### 4. Tone & Deadpan Editor — 9.5/10
Consistently deadpan investigative voice throughout. Best lines: "A rain dance is exempt. Rain is not," "The two emptinesses are related," and "Congress exempted the intention and left the weather." No em dashes anywhere in the file. No AI slop phrases. Deduction: none substantive; the Section IV "self-executing instance of the regulated activity" paragraph rides the edge of the register but stays inside it.

### 5. Structure & Format Checker — 9.0/10
Matches the established Ergo template: giant evidence-stacking H1, dek, author byline, JSON-LD NewsArticle, responsive hero with srcset, Roman-numeral sections, three pull quotes, numbered source notes, deadpan "Ergo." close. Meta description and OG tags present. Reading time 14 min is consistent with ~1,900 words of body text. Deduction: the hero image is 1200×600 while the convention is 1200×630; the markup declares the actual dimensions, so nothing breaks, but the ratio drifts from the house standard.

### 6. Ethics & Honesty Critic — 9.5/10
Every factual claim is real and verifiable against the cited sources. The deliberately wrong conclusion (clouds must file weather modification reports) is presented with its own refutation in Section IX: the statute requires intention, a cloud has no intention, §908.2 applies to persons, and there is no operator or sponsor to name on the form. The piece never conceals the jurisdictional failure; it states it plainly, exactly as the sun piece disclosed the personhood problem. Texas SB1154 is described accurately as dead in committee, not as law. Deduction: none; this is the genre executed correctly.

### 7. Reader Advocate — 8.5/10
The argument is clear from the headline through the conclusion, and the numbers are translated into vivid comparisons (100 elephants, 910,000-pound 747, two-thirds of the planet). The disclosures arrive before the conclusion, so the reader is never misled. Deduction: Sections I and II are dense with regulation; a general reader may need to slow down at the eight-item §908.3(a) enumeration, but the genre's audience expects this.
