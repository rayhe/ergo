## Critic scores

### 1. Legal Precisionist — 9.5/10
The §2131 paraphrase tracks the govregs text faithfully ("any person within the United States ... except under and in accordance with a license issued by the Commission"). The 10 CFR citations (20.1301, 50.47, 50.75, Part 30) match the CFR sources. The April 13, 2023 SRM-SECY-23-0001 date is correct per the NRC fusion page. The Price-Anderson figures ($450M primary, $131.1M retrospective, ~$12.9B total) match the NRC's 2021 report. The $372,240/day figure matches the FY2025 rule and the FY2026 cancellation notice. The "person within the United States" disclosure in Section VIII is the load-bearing legal honesty of the piece. Deduction: the headline says the 2023 decision placed "every fusion machine" under Part 30; the SRM approved limited-scope rulemaking, which is slightly narrower. The body states it correctly. Minor headline overreach.

### 2. Arithmetic Auditor — 9.0/10
Verified all computations: 3.828e26/1e9 = 3.828e17 ≈ 383 quadrillion one-gigawatt reactors. 94 reactors at ~1 GW = 9.4e10 W; ratio 4.07e15 ≈ four quadrillion. 3.828e26/4.5e9 (Vogtle) = 8.5e16 ≈ 85 quadrillion. $372,240 × 365.25 = $135.96M/year ≈ $136M. ×4.6e9 years = 6.25e17 ≈ $625 quadrillion. $12.9B/$600B (Lloyd's low end) = 2.15e-5 ≈ 0.002%. All arithmetic correct. Deduction: the "four quadrillion" fleet comparison rests on an unstated ~1 GW average reactor size; the article implies rather than states the assumption. Acceptable in the genre but noted.
