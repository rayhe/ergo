## Critic scores

### 1. Legal Precisionist — 9.0/10
The MMPA framing tracks the sources: October 21, 1972 enactment as Public Law 92-522, the section 101(a) take moratorium in U.S. waters (territorial sea plus 200-nautical-mile EEZ), and the take/person definitions quoted from the NOAA General Counsel's MMPA guidance (16 U.S.C. sections 1362(13), 1362(10), 1362(15)). The Level B harassment paraphrase tracks 16 U.S.C. section 1362(18)(B), including the full behavioral-pattern list. The section 101(a)(5)(D) mechanism is correctly stated: upon request, incidental (not intentional) take by harassment of small numbers by U.S. citizens engaged in a specified activity other than commercial fishing, with a negligible-impact finding, implemented at 50 CFR 216.101. Deduction: the draft states IHAs run "up to one year" without citing the one-year cap to a specific source line; the CFR excerpt's "1-year period" language supports it, but the citation could have been pinned.

### 2. Arithmetic Auditor — 9.0/10
Verified all computations: 236 minus 160 equals 76 dB; 10^7.6 is approximately 39.8 million, correctly stated as "roughly forty million times the acoustic intensity." 86,400 seconds times 0.8 equals 69,120 foraging seconds per day; at roughly one click per second that is on the order of 69,000 clicks; times $10,000 per violation equals roughly $690 million per whale per day, correctly presented as a theoretical ceiling with the conditional "were each click counted as a take." Deduction: the 76 dB subtraction places a maximum source level beside an rms received-level threshold, which is the face-value comparison the piece adopts; footnote 8's Nielsen and Mohl rms figure (236 dB re 1 uPa rms on-axis) narrows the metric gap, but the draft could have said so in the body rather than leaving it to the notes.
