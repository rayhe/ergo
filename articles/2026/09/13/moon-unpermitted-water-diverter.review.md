**Date:** 2026-09-13

## Critic scores

### 1. Legal Precisionist — 9.0/10
The Water Commission Act framing tracks the Board's own account: approved by 1914 referendum, established the permit process, created the agency that evolved into the State Water Board, effective December 19, 1914. The three-right taxonomy (riparian, pre-1914, post-1914) and the priority-dates-to-application-filing rule are correctly stated. The §1052 paraphrase tracks the statute: unauthorized diversion or use as trespass, $500/day ordinary, $1,000/day plus $2,500 per acre-foot in critically dry years or declared drought emergencies, liability imposable by court or Board. Deduction: the draft compresses the Water Commission Act's 1913 passage versus 1914 voter approval and effective date into "approved in 1914"; the sources support the 1914 approval and the December 19, 1914 effective date, so the compression is fair, but a footnote on the 1913/1914 legislative history would have been cleaner.

### 2. Arithmetic Auditor — 9.5/10
Verified all computations: December 19, 1914 to September 13, 2026 = 40,811 days; 40,811 x $500 = $20,405,500, correctly presented as a ceiling. 8 x 10^9 m3 / 1,233.48 = 6,485,715 acre-feet/day; x $2,500 = $16,214,287,500, correctly rounded to "more than sixteen billion dollars." 8,000/8,446 = 0.947, correctly stated as roughly ninety-five percent. The drought-schedule figure is presented conditionally ("were the drought schedule applied to a single day's operation"), which is the correct treatment. Deduction: the draft counts every calendar day as a trespass day; section 1052 penalizes days "in which the trespass occurs," and the tide occurs every day, so the reading holds, but the draft could note that the continuous-operation framing is doing the work here.

Deduction: the "no application from the Moon" claim in the paper trail is stated as a search result rather than a verified database query; within the genre this is acceptable, but the draft is honest about it being a public-record search.
