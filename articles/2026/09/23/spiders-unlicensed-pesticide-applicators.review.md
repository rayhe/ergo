# Ergo Review: The Global Spider Community as an Unlicensed Pesticide Application Operation

**Topic:** The global spider community as the largest unlicensed pesticide application operation in history under FIFRA — §3(a) registration at 7 U.S.C. §136a(a), the §2(u) pesticide definition ("any substance or mixture of substances intended for preventing, destroying, repelling, or mitigating any pest"), the §2(t) pest definition ("any insect" in the first clause), §11 applicator certification at 7 U.S.C. §136i with 40 CFR Part 171 state standards, the Nyffeler & Birkhofer 2017 global estimate (400-800 million metric tons of prey/year, >90% insects and springtails, 25 million tons spider biomass, 45,000+ described species), the FAO 3.7-million-tonne human pesticide baseline, the "label is the law" doctrine, the §7 establishment-registration gap, and a $981 million registration-violation penalty exposure (45,000 unregistered products × $21,805 per offense under §14(a)(1)).
**Author:** Kit (with Ray)
**Date:** 2026-09-23

## Headline
"Section 3 of the Federal Insecticide, Fungicide, and Rodenticide Act at 7 U.S.C. § 136a Provides That No Person in Any State May Distribute or Sell Any Pesticide That Is Not Registered With the Administrator, Section 2 at 7 U.S.C. § 136(u) Defines a Pesticide as Any Substance or Mixture of Substances Intended for Preventing, Destroying, Repelling, or Mitigating Any Pest, Section 2 at 7 U.S.C. § 136(t) Defines a Pest to Include Any Insect, Section 11 at 7 U.S.C. § 136i Provides for the Certification of Applicators Authorized to Use or Supervise the Use of Any Pesticide Classified for Restricted Use, the Global Spider Community Kills an Estimated 400 to 800 Million Metric Tons of Insect Prey Every Year by Injecting Venom Through Its Fangs, a Venom That Has Never Been Submitted for Registration and an Application Program for Which No Spider Has Ever Been Certified Under the Standards of 40 CFR Part 171, the Label Is the Law and No Spider Carries a Label, and the Largest Pesticide Application Operation on Earth Has Never Filed a Single Form With the Agency"

## Thesis
FIFRA §3(a) requires registration of every pesticide before distribution or sale. Section 2(u) defines a pesticide as any substance intended for preventing, destroying, repelling, or mitigating any pest, and Section 2(t) lists "any insect" as a pest without need of an Administrator's declaration. Spider venom is a mixture of neurotoxic and cytotoxic substances whose sole demonstrated function is the destruction of insects, applied at 400-800 million tons of prey per year (Nyffeler & Birkhofer 2017, The Science of Nature, doi:10.1007/s00114-017-1440-1), versus humanity's entire registered program of 3.7 million tonnes of active ingredient (FAO, 2022). No venom product is registered, no spider is a certified applicator under 40 CFR Part 171, no label exists though "the label is the law," no producing establishment is registered under §7, and no application records exist. Penalty exposure on the registration violation alone: 45,000 described species × $21,805 per offense = approximately $981 million.

## The Deliberately Wrong Conclusion
A spider is not a "person" under §136(s) (individual, partnership, association, corporation, organized group of persons — no arachnid listed); venom is manufactured endogenously and never distributed or sold in commerce; the pesticide definition turns on intent and spiders make no pesticidal claims, publish no labels, and state no implications; and FIFRA §25(b) exempts minimum-risk pesticides. The article discloses all four objections explicitly in Section IX, then overrules them on the text: the "person" definition's function is liability identification and exempting the largest program on Earth on incorporation grounds elevates form over purpose; §136(gg) defines "distribute or sell" expansively enough to cover holding venom in glands for delivery through hollow fangs; EPA determines intent objectively under 40 CFR §152.15(c) and venom has no non-pesticidal use ("it is not a condiment"); and the §25(b) list names corn oil and sodium chloride but not spider venom.
