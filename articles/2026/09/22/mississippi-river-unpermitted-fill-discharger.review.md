# Ergo Review: The Mississippi River as an Unpermitted Fill Discharger

**Topic:** The Mississippi River as an unpermitted discharger of fill material under Clean Water Act §404 (33 U.S.C. § 1344) — ~150 million metric tons/year of rock, sand, soil, and clay into the Gulf of Mexico, the 40 CFR § 232.2 effects-based fill definition, the 30,000 km² delta as Exhibit A, the §1344(f)(2) recapture provision ("bringing an area of the navigable waters into a use to which it was not previously subject"), the blocked practicable alternative (Old River Control Structure holding the river in its channel against the Atchafalaya), no ENG Form 4345 ever filed, and a §1319(d) penalty accruing at $25,000/day since October 18, 1972 ($492,425,000).
**Author:** Kit (with Ray)
**Date:** 2026-09-22

## Headline
"Section 404 of the Clean Water Act at 33 U.S.C. § 1344 Provides That the Secretary of the Army May Issue Permits for the Discharge of Dredged or Fill Material Into the Navigable Waters at Specified Disposal Sites, Section 301 at 33 U.S.C. § 1311 Declares the Discharge of Any Pollutant by Any Person Unlawful Except in Compliance With Such a Permit, the Mississippi River Discharges Approximately 150 Million Metric Tons of Rock, Sand, Soil, and Clay Into the Gulf of Mexico Every Year, the Regulation at 40 CFR § 232.2 Defines Fill Material as Any Material Placed in Waters of the United States Having the Effect of Replacing Any Portion of Such Waters With Dry Land or Changing the Bottom Elevation, the River Has Constructed a Delta Plain of More Than 30,000 Square Kilometers by Exactly That Means, the Recapture Provision at 33 U.S.C. § 1344(f)(2) Requires a Permit for Any Discharge Incidental to an Activity Having as Its Purpose Bringing an Area of the Navigable Waters Into a Use to Which It Was Not Previously Subject, No Individual Permit Application Has Ever Been Filed, the Corps Operates a Control Structure to Hold the River in Its Present Channel While the Unpermitted Discharge Continues, and the Largest Unpermitted Fill Operation in America Runs Twenty-Four Hours a Day Under the Supervision of the Agency That Issues the Permits"

## Thesis
Section 404 requires a permit from the Secretary of the Army before any discharge of dredged or fill material into the navigable waters. The Mississippi discharges ~150 million metric tons per year of exactly the materials the regulation lists as examples (rock, sand, soil, clay), into the territorial seas (which §1362(7) defines as navigable waters), with the precise effect the regulation defines (replacing water with dry land, changing bottom elevation). The §1344(f)(2) recapture provision — "bringing an area of the navigable waters into a use to which it was not previously subject" — describes delta-building with photographic precision. The practicable alternative exists (the Atchafalaya) and the permitting agency blocks it with the Old River Control Structure. No ENG Form 4345 has ever been filed, no notice published, no public-interest review conducted. Penalty at §1319(d)'s $25,000/day for the 19,697 days since October 18, 1972: $492,425,000.

## The Deliberately Wrong Conclusion
A river is not a "person" under §1362(5) (individual, corporation, partnership, association, State, municipality, commission, political subdivision, interstate body — no river listed); "discharge of a pollutant" under §1362(12) requires an addition "from any point source," and the Mississippi is itself a water of the United States, not a point source; the Supreme Court in Rapanos described fill material as "solids that do not readily wash downstream," which the river's fill plainly does, showing the statute was written for dump trucks, not watersheds; and the discharge predates the statute by millennia. The article discloses all four objections explicitly in Section VIII, then overrules them on the text: the "person" definition is qualified by "except as otherwise specifically provided," the fill definition is effects-based and purpose-free, the recapture provision reads as though drafted for the bird-foot delta, and the exemption list contains no river.
