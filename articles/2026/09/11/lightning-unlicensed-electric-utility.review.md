## Critic scores

### 1. Legal Precisionist — 9.0/10
The §205 (16 U.S.C. §824d) paraphrase tracks the statute: just and reasonable rates, filed schedules, no undue preference/prejudice under §205(b), Commission rate-setting under §206. The §824(e) "public utility" definition is quoted via CRS R41193 corroboration. The $1,544,521 per violation per day penalty figure matches the Federal Register inflation adjustment (Jan. 11, 2024, Doc. 2024-00425), which this draft cites instead of the older $1 million statutory base, a precision upgrade. Section 215 / 16 U.S.C. §824o mandatory reliability standards via IF11411. The §796(4) "person" definition is cited via EveryCRSReport RS20774. Deduction: the headline asserts Commission jurisdiction "over the transmission of electric energy in interstate commerce" without the statutory reservation that FPA regulation extends only to matters not subject to state regulation; Section VII supplies the reservation, but the headline elides it.

### 2. Arithmetic Auditor — 9.0/10
Verified all computations: 300,000,000 V × 30,000 A = 9×10¹² W = 9 terawatts, "on the order of a terawatt" is conservative and correct. 9 TW / 1.280799 TW ≈ 7.03, supporting "roughly seven times." 25,000,000 × 1,000,000,000 J = 2.5×10¹⁶ J = 6,944,444 MWh ≈ 6.9 TWh, supporting "on the order of seven terawatt-hours." 25,000,000 / 365 ≈ 68,493, supporting "roughly 68,000 deliveries a day." 100 W × 2,160 h (90 days) = 216 kWh ≈ 778 MJ, within an order of magnitude of one billion joules, consistent with the NWS "100-watt bulb for more than three months" citation. Deduction: "five times hotter than the surface of the sun" depends on 5,800°F vs 50,000°F (~8.6×); the source's "five times" uses 10,000°C (~18,000°F) phrasing from the cited weather.com page, so the draft inherits the source's convention, but the auditor notes the temperature-comparison convention is loose.

### 3. Source Verifier — 9.0/10
All 11 source notes map to URLs copied verbatim from fresh search results (Sept 11, 2026): the FPA text via powermag PDF, CRS R41193 and IF11411, the Federal Register penalty adjustment, NWS/NLDN figures via weather.gov and Fox Weather, the UNMC/NWS specialist piece for voltage/current/joules, the Weather Network lightning facts, weather.com lightning facts, EIA capacity, NOAA ORR for deaths, NIFC for fires, the University of Arizona ATMO336 lecture page for the 30%-of-outages figure, Cullman Times/NWS Jensenius for Florida density, and NCSL for energy-theft criminalization. Each factual claim maps to a numbered source. Deduction: the Florida "33 strikes per square mile" figure reaches the draft via a Cullman Times opinion column quoting NWS lightning specialist John Jensenius; traceability is preserved (Jensenius is named in the source), but the primary NWS page was not captured. The theweathernetwork URL carries search-result query parameters, flagged for monitoring in the next run.

Best lines: "It has no queue position because it does not wait in the queue," "The enforcement record is a matter of public record in its absence," and "A customer in Florida has, through no fault of his own, been placed in a rate class of one, and the class pays in roof repairs." ### 5. Structure & Format Checker — 9.0/10

### 6. Ethics & Honesty Critic — 9.5/10
Every factual claim is real and verifiable against the cited sources. The deliberately wrong conclusion (lightning must file FERC rate schedules) is presented with its own refutation in Section VII: the statute requires a person, §796(4) does not include weather, §205 regulates rates "made, demanded, or received" and lightning charges nothing, and the interstate-commerce hook fails because a bolt's transmission occurs within a single state. The piece never conceals the jurisdictional failure; it states it plainly, exactly as the clouds piece disclosed the intention element and the sun piece disclosed the personhood problem. Deduction: none; this is the genre executed correctly.

### 7. Reader Advocate — 9.0/10
The argument is clear from the headline through the conclusion, and the numbers are translated into vivid comparisons (seven times the grid's capacity, a 100-watt bulb for three months, a rate class of one in Florida). The disclosures arrive in Section VII, before the conclusion, so the reader is never misled. Deduction: Sections I and V are dense with statutory paraphrase; a general reader may need to slow down at the §205(b) undue-preference framing, but the genre's audience expects this.
