## Critic scores

### 1. Legal Precisionist — 9.0/10
The §10 (33 U.S.C. §403) paraphrase tracks the statute: creation of any obstruction not affirmatively authorized by Congress prohibited; no building of dams/dikes and no altering of the course, location, condition, or capacity of a navigable channel except on plans recommended by the Chief of Engineers and authorized by the Secretary of the Army prior to beginning. The Secretary-of-the-Army successor note (1947 reorganization replacing the Secretary of War) is correct. The §403a penalty structure is quoted accurately: each week's continuance a separate offense, every person and every corporation, misdemeanor, fine not exceeding $5,000, imprisonment not exceeding one year for a natural person, district-court injunction at the Attorney General's direction. Deduction: the draft describes §403a as "carried over from the 1890 Act," which is accurate but compresses the codification history; the citation itself is exact, so this is a style note, not an error.

### 2. Arithmetic Auditor — 9.0/10
Verified all computations: 22 weeks × $5,000 = $110,000; 6 weeks × $5,000 = $30,000; combined exposure $140,000, all correctly stated as "up to." 105,000 m³/s × 35.3147 = 3,708,044 cfs, supporting "roughly 3.7 million cubic feet per second" against the Army's figure. 83 ft = 25.3 m, consistent with the published 25.5 m. Dam-width growth 450 m to 600 m, lake rise 0.6 ft/day, inflow 16,500 cfs all transcribed correctly. Deduction: the 1986 closure is dated only "May 1986" in the USGS source, so "roughly twenty-two weeks" carries a few weeks of uncertainty; the draft hedges with "roughly" and the arithmetic is presented as an exposure ceiling, which is the correct treatment.
