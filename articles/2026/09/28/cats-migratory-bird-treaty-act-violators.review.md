# Ergo Review: Cats as Migratory Bird Treaty Act Violators

**Topic:** The domestic cat as an ongoing federal wildlife crime under the Migratory Bird Treaty Act, via 16 U.S.C. § 703 ("by any means or in any manner"), § 707(a) ($15,000/6 months per violation, strict liability), and 18 U.S.C. § 2 (the feeder as aider/abettor and the cat as the "innocent agent or instrumentality"). Median take 2.4 billion birds/year → $36 trillion maximum annual misdemeanor exposure.

## Headline
"Section 703 of the Migratory Bird Treaty Act at 16 U.S.C. § 703 Provides That It Shall Be Unlawful at Any Time, by Any Means or in Any Manner, to Pursue, Hunt, Take, Capture, Kill, Attempt to Take, Capture, or Kill Any Migratory Bird, Section 707 at 16 U.S.C. § 707 Punishes Each Misdemeanor Violation With a Fine of Not More Than $15,000 and Imprisonment of Not More Than Six Months, the Courts Apply Strict Liability to Misdemeanor Violations So the Government Need Not Prove That the Defendant Intended to Kill Anything, Section 2 of Title 18 at 18 U.S.C. § 2 Provides That Whoever Aids, Abets, Counsels, Commands, Induces or Procures the Commission of an Offense Against the United States Is Punishable as a Principal, and That One Who Causes the Commission of an Offense by an Innocent Agent or Instrumentality Is Guilty as a Principal, Peer-Reviewed Research Published in Nature Communications Estimates That Free-Ranging Domestic Cats Kill Between 1.3 and 4.0 Billion Birds in the United States Every Year, With a Median of 2.4 Billion, and Concludes That Cats Are Likely the Single Greatest Source of Anthropogenic Mortality for American Birds, Approximately 69 Percent of the Killing Is Done by Un-Owned Cats, Feeding the Cats Has No Measurable Effect on the Predation Rate, the Statute Contains No Exception for Cats, No Cat Has Ever Been Prosecuted, No Feeder Has Ever Been Charged, and the Largest Ongoing Federal Wildlife Crime in American History Is Committed by an Animal That Cannot Be Indicted"

## Thesis
Section 703 prohibits killing migratory birds "by any means or in any manner." Section 707(a) punishes each misdemeanor violation with up to $15,000 and six months, under strict liability. Section 2 of Title 18 punishes as a principal anyone who puts an "innocent agent or instrumentality" in motion. America's cats kill a median of 2.4 billion birds a year, and the person who feeds, shelters, and opens the door for the cat has put the instrumentality in motion. Maximum annual exposure: $36 trillion (median), $60 trillion (upper bound), $19.5 trillion (lower bound). The government fines the tank (ExxonMobil, $600,000 for 85 birds) and ignores the cat (zero prosecutions for 2.4 billion birds a year).

## The Deliberately Wrong Conclusion
The MBTA punishes "any person, association, partnership, or corporation"; a cat is not a person, aiding and abetting requires a principal offense, and no court would charge a feeder (the incidental-take circuit split and enforcement discretion make this doubly certain). The article discloses all of this explicitly in Section X, then overrules it on the grounds that the statute asks one question about means, not offenders: ExxonMobil did not personally drown 85 birds, its reserve pits did, and it pleaded guilty anyway because the pits were its instrumentality. The cat is an instrumentality with a name.
