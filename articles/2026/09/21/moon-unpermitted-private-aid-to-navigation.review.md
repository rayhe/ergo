# Ergo Review: The Moon as an Unpermitted Private Aid to Navigation

**Topic:** The moon as an unpermitted private aid to maritime navigation under 33 CFR Part 66 — no CG-2554 ever filed, no Light List entry, ~12 unauthorized discontinuances per year, lunar eclipses without Notice to Mariners, and a § 542 penalty accruing at $1,500 per new offense per day for 4.5 billion years.

## Headline

## Thesis
The moon aids maritime navigation and has never been permitted to do so. Under 33 CFR § 66.01-1, no person may establish and maintain any aid to maritime navigation without the Commandant's permission; under 14 U.S.C. § 542, unauthorized aids are a misdemeanor at $1,500 per offense per continuing day. The moon's service record (Polynesian wayfinding, the lunar-distance method from the 1767 Nautical Almanac, USNO's still-published lunar tables, USNA's restored celestial-navigation curriculum) makes it the oldest continuously operating aid known — and it has filed nothing, appears in no Light List volume, conforms to no IALA characteristic, and discontinues itself a dozen times a year without the required thirty days' notice.

## The Deliberately Wrong Conclusion
A celestial body is not "established" or "maintained" by any person; it sits in no navigable water and no Coast Guard district; it reflects rather than emits; § 542 itself reaches only aids "in or adjacent to" U.S. waters or on the high seas established by a person subject to U.S. jurisdiction. The article discloses all five objections explicitly in Section VIII, then overrules them on the text: "any aid to maritime navigation" is unqualified, § 66.01-40's exemptions are exclusive (bridges and OCS structures only), and a wreck buoy marks a hazard it did not create yet remains an aid.
