# Ergo Review: The Autumn Equinox as an Unlawful Deceptive Trade Practice

**Topic:** The autumn equinox as a deceptive act or practice in commerce under Section 5 of the FTC Act, 15 U.S.C. § 45 — the name means "equal night," the day exceeds the night everywhere on Earth on the equinox date, and the sellers have printed the corrective data in their own tables all along.
**Author:** Kit (with Ray)

## Headline
"Section 5 of the Federal Trade Commission Act at 15 U.S.C. § 45 Declares Unlawful Unfair or Deceptive Acts or Practices in or Affecting Commerce, the Word Equinox Is Formed From the Latin Aequus Meaning Equal and Nox Meaning Night, the September Equinox of 2026 Occurs at 00:05 Coordinated Universal Time on September 23, Which Is 5:05 PM Pacific Daylight Time on September 22, on That Date the Day Exceeds the Night by Approximately Fourteen Minutes at the Equator, Approximately Eight Minutes at Mid-Temperate Latitudes, and a Full Twenty-Four Hours at the South Pole Where No Night Occurs at All, Sunrise Is Defined as the Moment the Sun's Upper Limb Appears Though Its Center Is Fifty Arcminutes Below the Geometric Horizon, the Sunset Tables Every Almanac Publishes Assume Thirty-Four Arcminutes of Atmospheric Refraction and a Sixteen-Arcminute Solar Semidiameter, the Date of Actually Equal Day and Night, the Equilux, Falls Several Days Later and Within Two Degrees of the Equator Never Falls at All, and the Product Sold to the Public Under the Name Equal Night Has Contained No Equal Night for as Long as Anyone Has Been Selling It"

## Thesis
The FTC Act declares unlawful deceptive acts or practices in or affecting commerce. The equinox is sold under a name that means equal night. On the September equinox of 2026 (00:05 UTC September 23, 5:05 PM PDT September 22), the day exceeds the night by about fourteen minutes at the equator, about eight minutes at mid-temperate latitudes, and by the entire night at the South Pole. The thirty-four arcminutes of refraction and sixteen-arcminute solar semidiameter that cause the shortfall are the working assumptions of the very almanacs that advertise the equal night. The genuinely equal date, the equilux, falls several days later and within two degrees of the equator never occurs at all.

## The Deliberately Wrong Conclusion
Astronomers define the equinox geometrically (the sun's center crossing the celestial equator), refraction is physics not fraud, no consumer was harmed by eight extra minutes of daylight, and the name is seven hundred years old. The article discloses all five objections explicitly in Section VIII, then overrules them on the text: the statute asks about the reasonable consumer, not the astronomer; the deception is in the product's name and the dictionary entry; and the statute contains no grandfather clause for Latin.
