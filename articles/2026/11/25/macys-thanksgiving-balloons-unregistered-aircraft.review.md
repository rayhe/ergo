# 7-Critic Panel Evaluation: "Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade Balloons as Unregistered Aircraft"

The article follows the established Ergo format precisely: absurd-yet-rigorous headline, scholarly deck, Roman-numeral sections building a systematic case, genuine footnotes with a Sources section. No ethical concerns.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 8.5/10
The regulatory citations are accurate: 14 CFR 1.1 aircraft and balloon definitions, 49 U.S.C. 44101 registration requirement and its three exceptions, 14 CFR 91.203 airworthiness and registration certificates aboard, 14 CFR 101.13 operating limitations including the shielded-operation exception, 101.15 notice, 101.17 marking, 101.19 rapid deflation device, and 101.7 hazardous operations. The $75,000 civil penalty figure is correctly attributed to the registration-penalty source. The article's deliberately wrong conclusion, that moored parade balloons are unregistered civil aircraft, is the genre's disclosed conceit, and the piece states the Part 101 counter-reading honestly. Passes threshold, with the standard Ergo deduction for creative inference from regulatory text, and a further small deduction because the 61.3 and 46306 points are cited to this publication's own earlier birds investigation rather than to primary text.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0/10
Strong sourcing across primary and independent outlets: eCFR and govinfo for the regulations, Cornell LII for 91.203, AFAR and The Travel for the 2026 centennial figures, NPR Illinois for the wind rules, Wikipedia and Weather.com for 1997, Collider and Grunge for 2005, Blimpinfo and Untapped Cities for 1932, Mental Floss and People for balloon specifications. Minor weakness: the 1932 incident rests on secondary retellings rather than the original Times reporting, and the claim that the balloons fly below 150 feet is asserted from general parade reporting rather than a measured source. The article is transparent about its sources, and the arithmetic assumptions are stated with the figures.

## 7. Data Presentation — 9.0/10
Numbers are presented with consistent precision and always sourced: 12,000 cubic feet per giant balloon times 26 character balloons equals 312,000 cubic feet, inside the reported 300,000 to 400,000 parade total; 746 pounds of lift per balloon times 26 equals nearly 19,400 pounds; 3.5 million spectators divided by 100 balloons equals 35,000 people per aircraft; $75,000 per balloon times 100 equals $7.5 million per parade, times 96 balloon parades equals $720 million; $5 per registration times 100 equals $500. All arithmetic is correct, and the 96-parade count (1927 to 1941, plus 1945 to 2025) is stated and verifiable. Clear, devastating, verifiable.
