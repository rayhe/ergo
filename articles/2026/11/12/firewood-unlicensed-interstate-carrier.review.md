# 7-Critic Panel Evaluation: "Firewood Is an Unlicensed Interstate Carrier"

## 1. General Editor — 9.0/10
The article follows the established Ergo format precisely: absurd-yet-rigorous mega-headline, scholarly deck, Roman-numeral sections building the case from cargo to passenger to route to fellow passengers to law to scale to defense to prescription, genuine footnotes with a Sources section, and the deadpan "Ergo." close. Structure escalates with real dramatic logic: the cargo is counted in cords and cubic feet, the passenger is identified by species and body count, the route is mapped onto the American weekend, the fellow passengers widen the indictment, the law is specified by statute and section number, the scale is priced in billions, the defense concedes the escape hatches, and the prescription demands a compliance agreement per log. The Lake George roadside stop gives the piece a human exhibit the genre needs, and the per-log beetle arithmetic in Section VI turns the entomology into logistics. Minor weakness: Section IV carries three distinct pests in sequence, the longhorned beetle, the lanternfly, and the moth, which slightly crowds the section, though each earns its place on a distinct vector (inside the wood, on the wood, on the furniture).

## 2. Voice Coach — 9.0/10
Dead-serious investigative tone throughout, matching the hail and avalanche pieces in the regulatory register. Humor emerges exclusively from deadpan precision, never from jokes. Standout lines: "The camper is a weekend," "The seal is the license. The log without the seal is the violation," "The beetle did not walk there. It rode," "The wood does not volunteer," and "because it is a log." Excellent voice match; the piece never winks.

## 3. Ethics — 9.0/10
All statutes, regulations, and study results cited are real: the Energy Information Administration household counts and cord figures, the emerald ash borer discovery and biology from the USDA Agricultural Research Service and Iowa State Extension, the Kovacs cost projection, the Plant Protection Act penalties at 7 U.S.C. 7734, the New York 50-mile rule and $250 fine with $100 civil penalty and 15 days in jail, the Illinois 50-mile rule, the Wisconsin dealer certification, the T314-a and T314-c heat treatment standards, the Asian longhorned beetle counts from Penn State Extension, the spotted lanternfly spread and $554 million Pennsylvania estimate, and the December 15, 2020 APHIS final rule lifting the federal quarantine. The deliberately wrong conclusion, that every log requires a compliance agreement and a heat treatment certificate, is the disclosed conceit; the piece is honest about the escape hatch, that firewood is not a person and cannot sign an agreement, in Section VII, the same way prior pieces disclosed theirs. The computed figures (25 million cords, 3.2 billion cubic feet, 11,500 beetles per log, more than 4 million beetles per cord) are derived from cited inputs and shown, not asserted. No ethical concerns.

## 4. Social/Shareability — 9.0/10
"Firewood needs a license" is immediately graspable and funny at the headline level alone, and it carries three hooks most Ergo premises lack: one infected log and one female borer can destroy an entire forest, two logs held more than 23,000 beetles, and a seven dollar bundle bought locally costs less than the $350 penalty. "The beetle flies half a mile on its own wings. It rides hundreds of miles in a trunk" and "The seal is the license" are strong pull-quote material. The hero image, an inspector with a Firewood Compliance Agreement clipboard beside a quarantine checkpoint sign and a green beetle on a log, is built for the thumbnail. High share potential.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 9.0/10
The quarantine framework is accurately summarized: the Plant Protection Act criminal penalties of up to one year generally and up to five years for knowing movement for distribution or sale, the civil penalty ladder of $50,000 for an individual with the $1,000 cap for an initial non-commercial violation, $250,000 per violation for any other person, $500,000 and $1,000,000 per proceeding caps, and twice the gross gain or loss; New York's 2009 regulation, 50-mile linear limit, ban on out-of-state untreated imports, 71 degrees Celsius for 75 minutes, $250 fine plus $100 civil penalty and up to 15 days in jail, and confiscation; Illinois's 50-mile rule with original packaging and labeling; Wisconsin's $50 dealer certification and $200 re-inspection fee; and the T314-a 60 degrees for 60 minutes and T314-c 71.1 degrees for 75 minutes heat treatment standards, with kiln drying and seasoning correctly distinguished from heat treatment. The deliberately wrong conclusion, that logs must hold compliance agreements, is the genre's disclosed conceit, and the piece never pretends any compliance agreement has a line for a log. Passes threshold, with the standard Ergo deduction for creative inference from statutory text.

## 6. Research Rigor — 8.5/10
Strong sourcing across independent authorities: the Energy Information Administration for household counts and consumption, the USDA Agricultural Research Service and Iowa State Extension for the borer, the Forest Service for the Kovacs cost projection, the United States Code for the Plant Protection Act, the New York Department of Environmental Conservation for the 50-mile rule, the Illinois Administrative Code and Wisconsin Administrative Code for the state rules, the National Plant Board for the heat treatment standards, Penn State Extension for the longhorned beetle counts, and a peer-reviewed agent-based modeling paper for the lanternfly spread. The per-cord beetle arithmetic is computed from the cited per-log count and the volume of a cord, with inputs stated. Minor weakness: the 4.3 cords per household figure is carried by a secondary explainer of the Energy Information Administration survey rather than the survey table itself, and the $300 million federal expenditure figure comes via a book rather than the agency's own accounting. The disputed question of whether the camper or the log is the violator is handled by quoting the statute's own $1,000 cap for the non-commercial first offender, which is the cleaner reading of the conceit.

## 7. Data Presentation — 9.0/10
Numbers are presented with consistent precision and always sourced: 2.25 million primary and 8.83 million secondary wood-burning households, 4.3 cords a year, 128 cubic feet per cord, 500 trillion British thermal units, about 25 million cords, about 3.2 billion cubic feet, half an inch of beetle, 16 native ash species, more than 8 billion ash trees threatened, hundreds of millions killed, more than 85 percent mortality, 35 states and the District of Columbia, half a mile of flight against hundreds of miles of hitchhiking, 23,000 beetles in two logs, 11,500 per log, more than 4 million per cord, 130 counties and 13 states for the lanternfly, $554 million in Pennsylvania, $40 billion a year in invasive pest losses, $10 billion and double with the suburbs, $1.8 to $7.6 billion in Ohio, more than $300 million in federal funds, 50 miles, 60 degrees for 60 minutes, 71.1 degrees for 75 minutes, $50,000, $250,000, $500,000, $1,000,000, and one year against five years. The comparison framework, the beetle's flight against the trunk's mileage and the cord's volume against the beetle count, is devastating and verifiable. Clear, consistent, sourced.
