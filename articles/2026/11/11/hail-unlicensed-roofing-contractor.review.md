# 7-Critic Panel Evaluation: "Hail Is an Unlicensed Roofing Contractor"

## 1. General Editor — 9.0/10
The article follows the established Ergo format precisely: absurd-yet-rigorous mega-headline, scholarly deck, Roman-numeral sections building the case from trade to record to scale to work to license to defense to prescription, genuine footnotes with a Sources section, and the deadpan "Ergo." close. Structure escalates with real dramatic logic: the physics of the stone is introduced, the record stone is certified, the national scale is counted in billions, the work is priced per roof, the license is specified by number, the defense concedes the escape hatches, and the prescription demands a permit per stone. The Vivian daiquiri anecdote gives the piece a human exhibit the genre needs, and the kinetic-energy ladder in Section IV turns the meteorology into ballistics. Minor weakness: Section III carries three distinct counts in sequence, the event totals, the annual losses, and the costliest single storms, which slightly crowds the section, though each earns its place on a distinct metric (how often, how much, how bad at worst).

## 2. Voice Coach — 9.0/10
Dead-serious investigative tone throughout, matching the avalanche and iceberg pieces in the severe-weather register. Humor emerges exclusively from deadpan precision, never from jokes. Standout lines: "A hailstone does not drift down. It arrives," "The roof is not struck by weather. It is struck by a volley of small-caliber rounds made of ice, fired from a cloud, with no one holding the weapon," "The exemption is written for the homeowner with a tube of sealant and a Saturday afternoon," "Hail has never performed a minor repair," and "because it melts." Excellent voice match; the piece never winks.

## 3. Ethics — 9.0/10
All statutes, regulations, and study results cited are real: the NOAA definition of hail and the National Severe Storms Laboratory fall speeds and updraft requirements, the National Weather Service one-inch severe threshold and its January 5, 2010 raising from three quarters of an inch, the Vivian record certified by NOAA's National Climate Extremes Committee, the Bangladesh world record from the World Meteorological Organization, the Mayfest injuries and losses, the Gallagher Re severe convective storm loss totals, the Insurance Information Institute event counts and claim averages, the Aon 60 to 80 percent hail share, the International Residential Code permit requirement and R105.2 ordinary-repair exemption, the California C-39 classification and Business and Professions Code Section 7028, the Louisiana roofing license threshold, the Maryland Home Improvement Commission rules, and the Cost vs. Value and Verisk roof replacement costs. The deliberately wrong conclusion, that every hailstone requires a roofing license and a permit per stone, is the disclosed conceit; the piece is honest about the escape hatch, that hail is not a person and cannot hold a license, in Section VI, the same way prior pieces disclosed theirs. The computed figures (kinetic energies, the $31 to $41 billion hail share, the 1.8 to 2.3 million roofs) are derived from cited inputs and shown, not asserted. No ethical concerns.

## 4. Social/Shareability — 9.0/10
"Hail needs a roofing license" is immediately graspable and funny at the headline level alone, and it carries two hooks most Ergo premises lack: the Vivian stone's finder planned to make daiquiris out of it before a power outage melted part of the record, and the kinetic-energy ladder ends with a hailstone carrying twice the muzzle energy of a 9mm round. "A hailstone does not drift down. It arrives" and "Hail has never performed a minor repair" are strong pull-quote material. The hero image, an inspector with a Roofing Permit clipboard beside a softball-sized stone embedded in a shattered roof, is built for the thumbnail. High share potential.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 9.0/10
The building-code framework is accurately summarized: the International Residential Code requires a permit for work that alters, repairs, or replaces structural components, full roof replacement requires a permit in virtually all United States jurisdictions, minor repairs are exempt as ordinary repairs under IRC R105.2, and permits typically expire 180 days after issuance if no work has started. The licensing framework is accurately summarized: California C-39 roofing under 16 CCR 832.39, required for roofing work over $500, unlicensed work a misdemeanor under Business and Professions Code Section 7028, four years of journey-level experience, two examinations, a $25,000 bond, and workers' compensation insurance even with no employees; Louisiana's $7,500 residential roofing license threshold effective January 1, 2026; Jefferson Parish's re-roofing permit for 50 percent or more replacement; Maryland's MHIC license for home improvement over $500, unpermitted work a misdemeanor punishable by up to one year in jail, two years of experience, examinations, and $50,000 liability insurance; Florida's requirement that the licensed roofing contractor pull the permit. The deliberately wrong conclusion, that hailstones must hold licenses and file permits, is the genre's disclosed conceit, and the piece never pretends any permit application has a line for a hailstone. Passes threshold, with the standard Ergo deduction for creative inference from statutory text.

## 6. Research Rigor — 8.5/10
Strong sourcing across independent authorities: the National Weather Service and Guinness World Records for the Vivian stone, the World Meteorological Organization for the Bangladesh record, the billion-dollar disaster database for the costliest storms, Climate Central for the $10 billion annual figure and the 7,000-event average, the Insurance Information Institute and Gallagher Re for the event counts and loss totals, Aon for the hail share of storm losses, the California Contractors State License Board for the C-39 classification, Jefferson Parish for the Louisiana re-roofing permit, and Zonda and Verisk for the roof replacement costs. The per-stone kinetic energies are computed from the laboratory's fall speeds and the density of ice, with inputs stated. Minor weakness: the Gallagher Re and Insurance Information Institute figures are carried by a secondary roofing-statistics aggregator rather than the primary releases, and the National Severe Storms Laboratory fall speeds and updraft requirements come via a restatement rather than the laboratory's own page. The disputed question of whether damage is work is avoided by treating the loss total as the bill for the work, which is the cleaner reading of the conceit.
