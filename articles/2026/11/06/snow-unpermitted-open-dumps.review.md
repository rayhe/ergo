# 7-Critic Panel Evaluation: "Snow as Unpermitted Open Dumps"

## 1. General Editor: 9.0/10
The article follows the Ergo canon precisely: long factual headline, scholarly deck, Roman-numeral sections building a systematic case from facility to scale to process to discharge to standard to defense to prescription, genuine footnotes with a Sources section, and the deadpan "Ergo." close. The arc escalates cleanly, and the load-bearing beam is the unit rate against the fleet: 2,715 gallons of water per acre-inch of snow, a 75-foot pile that stood for five months, against a permitting system that has permitted zero snow piles. The defense section concedes the real escape hatches (precipitation is not discarded material, the pile is temporary, meltwater is stormwater under MS4 permits, missing form, government operator) before overruling them, which is the genre's honesty mechanism. Minor weakness: Section II carries two arithmetics in one paragraph, the per-acre figures and the Harvard tonnage conversion, which slightly crowd each other, though both are flagged as arithmetic in footnotes 6 and 9.

## 2. Voice Coach: 9.0/10
Deadpan investigative register throughout, matching the oysters and chimney pieces, with the humor emerging exclusively from bureaucratic precision rather than jokes. Standout lines: "The permitted system takes in the household trash. The unpermitted system takes in the winter," "a farm is a place where material is placed on land and left there, and so is a snow farm," "The definition fits the way a glove fits a hand, provided the hand is a snowbank," and "waiting for a form that does not exist." The piece never winks; the closest it comes is the hero image's clipboard reading "Turbidity: HIGH," which lands as straight-faced procedural evidence. Excellent voice match.

## 3. Ethics: 9.0/10
All statutes, guidelines, and study results cited are real and quoted or paraphrased accurately where quoted: the 2,715 gallon per acre-inch figure, the Orchard Park 77-inch storm total, the Boston 110.6-inch seasonal record, the Harvard 300,000-ton snow farm, the 24 million ton road salt figure, the Minnesota impairment counts, the EPA 230 milligram per liter chloride criterion, the one gallon of oil to one million gallons of water figure, the RCRA section 1004(27) definition, the section 4005 open dump definition, the 40 CFR 258 liner and leachate standards, and the MassDEP guidance provisions. The deliberately wrong conclusion, that a snow pile is an open dump requiring a Subtitle D permit, is the disclosed conceit; the piece is honest about the definitional escape hatch, that snow is precipitation rather than discarded material and meltwater is regulated as stormwater, in Section VI, the same way prior pieces disclosed theirs. The per-acre, meltwater, and chloride figures are explicitly flagged in footnotes 6 and 9 as arithmetic, not separately measured loads, and footnote 3 discloses that the State Climate Extremes Committee declined to certify the 24-hour Orchard Park figure while the storm total stands. No ethical concerns.

## 4. Social/Shareability: 9.0/10
"Your snow pile is an illegal dump" is immediately graspable and funny at the headline level alone, and it carries a second hook most Ergo premises lack: the reader's own city built the unpermitted facility, with their tax dollars, and called it a snow farm. The 75-foot pile that survived until July 14 is the single most shareable image; "276 tons of trash, including manhole covers, parking meters, and half-eaten yogurts" and "the unpermitted system takes in the winter" are strong pull-quote material. The hero image, an inspector in a suit and hard hat sampling meltwater beside a grime-streaked pile, is built for the thumbnail. High share potential.

## 5. Legal Accuracy: 8.5/10
The RCRA framework is accurately summarized: section 1004(27) defines solid waste to include other discarded material resulting from community activities; section 4005(a) defines an open dump as a facility where solid waste is disposed of that is not a sanitary landfill meeting the section 4004 criteria; 40 CFR 257.1 treats facilities failing the criteria as open dumps prohibited under section 4005; 40 CFR Part 258 requires composite liners, leachate collection maintaining less than 30 centimeters over the liner, and groundwater monitoring; about 1,200 permitted municipal solid waste landfills operate in the United States. The MassDEP guidance provisions are accurately summarized, including the 75-foot well setback, the silt fence, the 50-foot buffer, and the May 15 debris deadline. The deliberately wrong conclusion, that a snow pile is an open dump, is the genre's disclosed conceit, and the piece never pretends any permit application has a line for tons of snow. Passes threshold, with the standard Ergo deduction for creative inference from regulatory text.

## 6. Research Rigor: 9.0/10
Strong sourcing across independent authorities: USGS for the snow water equivalent, the National Weather Service via the Associated Press for the Orchard Park storm, Harvard Magazine for the Allston snow farm, the USGS via a New Hampshire news roundup for the road salt tonnage, the Minnesota Pollution Control Agency via East Metro Water for the impairment counts, the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences via the Chesapeake Bay Program for the stream chloride exceedances, the EPA via NEIWPCC for the oil contamination figure, EPA training slides for the solid waste definition, the eCFR for the open dump criteria, EPA for the Subtitle D standards, and MassDEP for the snow disposal guidance. Minor weakness: the 24 million ton road salt figure is USGS data carried by a local news roundup rather than a USGS publication directly, and the 292 million ton municipal waste figure comes from a 2025 journal paper rather than an EPA release; the article handles both by linking the carrying sources transparently. The 10-to-1 snow ratio is named as a rule of thumb that varies with wet or powdery snow.

## 7. Data Presentation: 9.0/10
Numbers are presented with consistent precision and always sourced: 2,715 gallons per acre-inch of snow, 27,154 gallons per acre-inch of rain, 209,055 gallons per acre for Orchard Park, about 300,000 gallons per acre for Boston, 300,000 tons and 72 million gallons for the Harvard snow farm, 1,700 truckloads in a day, a 75-foot pile lasting until July 14, more than 24 million tons of road salt, about 14.6 million tons of chloride, 276.17 tons of trash, 50 impaired and 75 high-risk Minnesota water bodies, 42 percent of Minnesota chloride from road salt, 730 million pounds of salt per Minnesota winter, 230 milligrams per liter against five to ten times that in urban streams, one gallon of oil to one million gallons of water, and about 1,200 permitted landfills. The comparison framework, unit water equivalent versus fleet tonnage versus the permitted system on the same tonnage scale, is devastating and verifiable. Clear, consistent, sourced.
