# 7-Critic Panel Evaluation: "Chimneys Are Unpermitted Stationary Sources"

Article: `chimneys-unpermitted-stationary-sources.html` by Josiah Merriweather, November 3, 2026. Category: Clean Air Law & Hearth Science. Thesis: every chimney is a stationary source under the Clean Air Act, the fireplace is exempt from federal heater standards precisely because it heats poorly, the Bay Area already treats chimneys as regulated stacks, and therefore every chimney should hold a Title V operating permit. One review round; passes on all seven.

## 1. General Editor — 8.8/10
Canon-adherent: absurd-yet-rigorous headline, scholarly deck, byline/date/read-time meta, seven Roman-numeral sections (I-VII), two pullquotes, hero image with descriptive alt text, 14 sequential footnotes with matching bidirectional refs, JSON-LD matching on-page copy, deadpan "Ergo." close. The arc is the strength: the stack, the tonnage, the statute, the exemption, the local precedent, a defense that concedes everything and is overruled in four words, then a prescription that embraces the absurdity. Minor deductions: 14 footnotes is thinner than the recent 23-27 footnote articles, though every load-bearing number is sourced; the prescription section's roofline sentence was awkward in draft ("where the inspector already stands in the Bay Area's enforcement photographs, or rather where the inspector would stand") and was simplified before publication.

## 2. Voice Coach — 9.0/10
Dead-serious investigative register held throughout. Zero em dashes, zero exclamation points in copy, no jokes, winks, slang, or AI-slop phrases. Structural humor, never signaled. Standout lines: "It is a smokestack with a mantelpiece attached," "The worse the heater, the freer the stack," "The defense rests. It is overruled." Minor: "Read that exemption again, slowly, because it is the whole story" is the closest the piece comes to addressing the reader directly, but it stays in the bureaucratic register.

## 3. Ethics — 9.1/10
The deliberately wrong conclusion (a Title V permit for every chimney) is the genre's disclosed conceit, and the defense names the honest mechanisms: area sources are defined as too small and numerous to inventory individually, NSPS applies only to new heaters, and a fireplace is not a heater by the agency's own determination. No person or company is harmed; the Environmental Protection Agency and the Bay Area Air District are described by their own published words. The mortality figure (about 8,600 premature deaths annually) is presented with its confidence interval and as modeled association, not as a body count caused by any individual fireplace.

## 4. Research Rigor — 8.8/10
Load-bearing numbers verified against sources consulted for this article: Science Advances 2026 (2 percent primary fuel, about 28 percent of wintertime PM2.5 emissions per the 2023 National Emissions Inventory, 21.9 percent population-weighted winter PM2.5 at 2.43 micrograms per cubic meter, about 8,600 premature deaths, CI 6,500-9,600); ACS EST Air paper (11 million homes, 8.9 percent, up to 98 percent of residential-heating PM2.5, about 485,000 tons per year); CRS report on the 2015 NSPS (March 16, 2015 publication, fireplaces excluded because "fireplaces are not effective heaters"); EPA fact sheet (Step 1 4.5 grams per hour, Step 2 2.0/2.5 grams per hour, standards apply only to new heaters); Bay Area Air District pages (November to February season, more than one-third of winter particulate pollution, up to 90 percent on some days and areas, 20 percent opacity rule, new-building prohibition) and its November 15, 2018 release ($100 ticket class option, sole-source-heat registration, open-hearth fireplace no longer qualifying). Minor: the 485,000-ton figure and the 11-million-home figure come via a peer-reviewed secondary synthesis of EPA estimates rather than the EPA inventory itself; the arithmetic built on them (485,000 divided by 100 equals 4,850) was recomputed and is correct.

## 6. Satire Calibration — 9.1/10
The wrongness lives exactly where the genre requires: the prescription (permitting millions of chimneys as Title V sources), never the evidence table. The defense is the honesty engine, not a strawman: it concedes every number and raises the four genuine objections (inventory absurdity, new-source-only standards, the not-a-heater determination, the house-versus-chimney question). A careful reader cannot mistake the piece for advocacy; the piece itself quotes the regulation that defeats its own prescription. The Bay Area material keeps the satire grounded: a real district already bans the burning, limits the smoke, and prohibits the device in new buildings.

## 7. Legal Precision — 8.7/10
Statutory quotations check out: 42 U.S.C. 7602(z) stationary source ("any building, structure, facility, or installation which emits or may emit any air pollutant"), 7602(j) major stationary source (100 tons per year), 7412 major source (10 tons single / 25 tons combined hazardous air pollutants) and area source (any stationary source that is not a major source), 40 CFR 51.491 area sources as too small or numerous to inventory individually. The summary of section 112 area-source strategy is fair. Minor deductions: the article elides the Title V applicability nuance (Title V covers major sources and certain area sources subject to standards, not area sources generally), which is precisely the leap the prescription depends on and is therefore genre-correct but legally incomplete; the "house is the source, not the chimney" defense exhibit is the strongest legal objection and gets only one paragraph before being overruled, which is the joke but also the thinnest rebuttal in the piece.
