# 7-Critic Panel Evaluation: "Pollen Is the Largest Unpermitted Air Emission in the United States"

## 1. General Editor — 9.0/10
The article follows the established Ergo format precisely: absurd-yet-rigorous mega-headline, scholarly deck, Roman-numeral sections building the case from emission to grain to route to fellow emitters to law to scale to defense to prescription, genuine footnotes with a Sources section, and the deadpan "Ergo." close. Structure escalates with real dramatic logic: the emission is counted in grains and tons, the grain is measured against the PM10 cutoff it evades, the route is mapped onto the wind, the fellow emitters widen the indictment, the law is specified by statute and threshold, the scale is priced in people and dollars, the defense concedes the escape hatches, and the prescription demands a Title V permit per plant. The goldenrod scapegoat gives the piece a human exhibit the genre needs, and the 6.25 square miles equals 100 tons arithmetic in Section V turns the botany into permitting. Minor weakness: Section IV carries three distinct emitters in sequence, trees, mold, and goldenrod, which slightly crowds the section, though each earns its place on a distinct vector (the season opener, the spore count, the false suspect).

## 2. Voice Coach — 9.0/10
Dead-serious investigative tone throughout, matching the firewood and rain pieces in the regulatory register. Humor emerges exclusively from deadpan precision, never from jokes. Standout lines: "One plant. One billion grains. No permit.", "The grain is twice the size of the largest particle the PM10 standard counts.", "The wind does not file a permit application.", "The invisible emitter is never charged. The visible bystander is convicted every fall.", and "because it is a plant." Excellent voice match; the piece never winks.

## 3. Ethics — 9.0/10
All statutes, regulations, and study results cited are real: the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention prevalence figures from the 2021 National Health Interview Survey, the ragweed billion-grain figure from Purdue Extension and the Brooklyn Botanic Garden, the 400 mile transport distance, the Anderegg PNAS findings on season length and pollen concentrations, the six criteria pollutants and the NAAQS table from the Environmental Protection Agency, the 188 hazardous air pollutants under Section 112, the Title V major source thresholds at 100 tons per year and 10 and 25 tons per year for hazardous air pollutants, the 40 CFR 70.2 definition of major source, the National Allergy Bureau counting thresholds, and the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality cost estimates. The deliberately wrong conclusion, that every ragweed plant requires a Title V operating permit and pollen should become a seventh criteria pollutant, is the disclosed conceit; the piece is honest about the escape hatch, that a plant is not a person and cannot sign a permit, in Section VII, the same way prior pieces disclosed theirs. The computed figures (6.25 square miles of ragweed equals the 100 ton threshold) are derived from cited inputs and shown, not asserted. No ethical concerns.

## 4. Social/Shareability — 9.0/10
"Pollen needs a permit" is immediately graspable and funny at the headline level alone, and it carries three hooks most Ergo premises lack: one plant releases a billion grains, the grains travel 400 miles, and a quarter of American adults have a seasonal allergy. "The grain is twice the size of the largest particle the PM10 standard counts" and "The wind does not file a permit application" are strong pull-quote material, and the goldenrod scapegoat is the kind of counterintuitive fact that travels on its own. The hero image, an inspector with a Title V Operating Permit Application clipboard standing in a ragweed field under a haze of pollen, is built for the thumbnail. High share potential.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 9.0/10
The Clean Air Act framework is accurately summarized: the six criteria pollutants under Sections 108 and 109, 42 U.S.C. 7408 to 7409, and the NAAQS in 40 CFR Part 50, with the PM10 24 hour standard of 150 micrograms per cubic meter and the PM2.5 annual standard of 9.0 micrograms per cubic meter; the 188 hazardous air pollutants under Section 112, 42 U.S.C. 7412, with the 189 to 188 history correctly stated; the Title V major source thresholds of 100 tons per year for any regulated pollutant and 10 and 25 tons per year for hazardous air pollutants, with the 40 CFR 70.2 definition of major source and the five year permit term correctly stated; and the PM10 and PM2.5 size cutoffs of 10 and 2.5 microns correctly applied to the 18 to 22 micron ragweed grain. The deliberately wrong conclusion, that plants must hold Title V permits, is the genre's disclosed conceit, and the piece never pretends any plant has applied for one. Passes threshold, with the standard Ergo deduction for creative inference from statutory text.

## 6. Research Rigor — 8.5/10
Strong sourcing across independent authorities: the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention for prevalence, Purdue Extension and the Brooklyn Botanic Garden for ragweed biology, the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences for the season lengthening study, the Environmental Protection Agency for the criteria pollutants, the NAAQS table, the hazardous air pollutant list, and the Title V thresholds, the National Allergy Bureau record via a peer reviewed dataset paper for the station counts, and the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality for the cost estimates. The per-square-mile tonnage arithmetic is computed from the cited 16 tons per square mile figure and the 100 ton threshold, with inputs stated. Minor weakness: the 16 tons per square mile figure reaches the piece through a secondary encyclopedia citation of an older consumer health reference rather than a primary agronomic source, and the 2021 study's season lengthening is reported as eight days where some press coverage of the same paper says ten, a discrepancy the article resolves by following the paper's own framing but does not flag. The disputed question of whether the plant or the person is the violator is handled by quoting the statute's own stationary source definition, which is the cleaner reading of the conceit.

## 7. Data Presentation — 9.0/10
Numbers are presented with consistent precision and always sourced: one billion grains per plant, 16 tons per square mile, 6.25 square miles to 100 tons, 400 miles of transport, two miles of altitude, 18 to 22 microns against the 10 micron PM10 cutoff, 0.5 to 5 micron fragments against the 2.5 micron PM2.5 cutoff, 25.7 percent of adults and 18.9 percent of children in 2021, 25.2 and 20.6 percent in 2024, more than 50 million Americans, 4.1 million physician office visits, 1.16 to 4.5 billion dollars direct and 7.7 billion with indirect costs, 35 million people and 19 million employed adults, more than 3.0 billion dollars for antihistamines, 20 days earlier, eight days longer, 21 percent more pollen, 50 percent and 8 percent climate attributions, 125 stations historically and 42 currently active against 4,821 air quality stations, 100 tons per year, 10 and 25 tons per year, 188 hazardous air pollutants, and 150 and 9.0 micrograms per cubic meter. The comparison framework, the grain's size against the monitor's inlet and the volunteer pollen network against the regulatory monitoring network, is devastating and verifiable. Clear, consistent, sourced.
