Constitutional Law & Poultry

Two Turkeys Named Gobble and Waddle Received a Full, Absolute, and Unconditional Presidential Pardon on November 25, 2025, Neither Bird Has Ever Been Charged With an Offense Against the United States, the Constitution Limits Pardons to Exactly Those Offenses, the Justice Department Requires a Five-Year Wait and a Formal Petition and Three Character References, and 10,418 Human Petitions Were Still Pending When the Birds Were Pardoned

Two turkeys received a full, absolute, and unconditional presidential pardon on November 25, 2025, and they are also the only pardon recipients in the history of the republic who never applied for one. Every year, the president pardons two birds that have committed no offense, filed no petition, waited no five years, supplied no character references, and submitted to no investigation by the Federal Bureau of Investigation, while the office that processes pardons for human beings carries a backlog of thousands of petitions from people who did all of those things. The Constitution grants the pardon power for offenses against the United States. The turkey has committed none. This investigation examines the largest unexamined exercise of the pardon power in the country, the birds it pardons, the process it skips, the law it evades, and the petition the record demands.

By Hezekiah Bramwell · November 16, 2026 · 15 min read

I. The Birds

On November 25, 2025, in the Rose Garden of the White House, the president granted a full, absolute, and unconditional presidential pardon to two turkeys named Gobble and Waddle. The birds weighed 52 and 50 pounds, respectively, and they were presented by Jay Jandrain, the chairman of the National Turkey Federation and the president and chief executive officer of Butterball, the company that raised them. The ceremony was the 78th National Thanksgiving Turkey Presentation, and it took place two days before Thanksgiving. The birds were then sent to live at North Carolina State University in Raleigh, where they serve as agricultural education ambassadors under the care of the Prestage Department of Poultry Science.115

The birds were raised in Kenly, North Carolina, by Travis Pittman, a Butterball contract grower, in coordination with Jandrain and the Butterball live operations team. Before the ceremony, the two birds stayed at the Willard InterContinental Hotel in Washington, at the federation's expense, which is the customary lodging for the national birds. After the ceremony, they returned to North Carolina. The pardon spared them from the table. It did not spare them from the paperwork, because there was no paperwork.115

The president's remarks at the ceremony were precise about the nature of the grant. "In a few moments, I will grant a full, absolute, and unconditional Presidential pardon to two handsome Thanksgiving turkeys," he said. The words are the same words used in pardons of human beings. The president also announced that he had located the previous year's birds, Peach and Blossom, on their way to be processed, and that he had pardoned them as well, a retroactive pardon of two birds pardoned once already, by another president, the year before.2

The pardon was granted in the open, before cameras, in the middle of the day, to birds that had been delivered to the White House by the trade association that represents the industry that sells them. The organization that presents the pardoned turkey operates a website called EatTurkey.org. The birds were pardoned by the president of the United States and delivered by the organization whose stated purpose is that Americans eat turkey.14

Two pardons. No petition. No conviction. No waiting period.

II. The Selection

The birds are chosen the way a pardon applicant is not. Each year, a flock of between 50 and 80 birds, typically from the farm of the current chairman of the National Turkey Federation and hatched in early summer, is selected and acclimated to loud noises, flash photography, and large crowds. In late October or early November, the 10 to 20 best preened and best behaved of that flock are chosen, and the flock is narrowed to two finalists. The names are chosen by the White House staff from suggestions by schoolchildren from the state where the birds were raised.3

The birds are raised in the same fashion as turkeys designated for slaughter, fed a grain heavy diet of fortified corn and soybeans to increase their size. The turkeys for the presentation are usually between 17 and 21 week old toms, males, weighing about 45 pounds by the time of their White House visit. A tom raised for market takes 18 weeks to reach 38 pounds. A hen takes 14 weeks to reach 15 and a half pounds. The presentation birds are larger than the market birds, because the presentation bird is bred to be looked at, and the market bird is bred to be eaten, and the difference between the two is about seven pounds and a pardon.3

The alternate is a real office. Waddle was the alternate in 2025, and he was pardoned alongside Gobble. In 2008, the number one turkey, Pecan, fell ill the night before the ceremony, and the backup, a bird named Pumpkin, took his place, and both birds were allowed to live. The alternate exists because a bird can fall ill, and the ceremony cannot be cancelled, and the pardon must be granted to someone.3

The birds are Broad Breasted Whites, a variety bred for large size, sedentary by nature, with a predilection for overeating, which makes them prone to heart disease, respiratory failure, joint damage, and reduced lifespans. For many years, pardoned turkeys frequently died within a year of being pardoned. In recent years, their lifespans have improved, frequently exceeding two years and occasionally reaching three, an improvement attributed to better choices of homes after the pardons.3

III. The History

The founding story of the pardon is itself unverified. The tale that Abraham Lincoln pardoned his son Tad's Christmas turkey in 1863 appears to be apocryphal, as it only appeared in print after Lincoln was assassinated in 1865. The first pardon in the tradition rests on a story no one wrote down while the president was alive to confirm it.5

Turkeys were donated to presidents as gifts from private citizens since the 1870s. Horace Vose, a Rhode Island turkey farmer, presented a dressed turkey to the president each year from 1873 until his death in 1913. The official presentation began in 1947, when President Harry Truman first received a live turkey from the National Turkey Federation. Truman's birds were not pardoned. Records at the Truman Library show that Truman admitted eating at least some of the turkeys. President Dwight Eisenhower ate the birds presented to him during his two terms.45

President John F. Kennedy spontaneously spared a turkey on November 19, 1963, just three days before his assassination. The bird wore a sign reading "Good Eating Mr. President" and weighed 55 pounds. Kennedy returned it to the farm, saying "we'll let this one grow." Newspapers called it a pardon. Kennedy did not.45

The first president on record to use the word pardon was Ronald Reagan. In 1987, Reagan's turkey, a bird named Charlie, was headed to a petting zoo, and Reagan, facing questions over the Iran Contra affair about whether he would consider pardoning Oliver North, who had yet to be tried, conjured the notion of the turkey pardon as a joke to deflect those questions. The phrase entered the ceremony as a punchline. Two years later, President George H. W. Bush made it permanent. On November 14, 1989, Bush announced that the year's bird had "been granted a presidential pardon as of right now," and sent the turkey to Frying Pan Park in Herndon, Virginia. Every president since has issued the pardon.45

The retirement homes tell the history in real estate. For many years the birds were sent to Frying Pan Farm Park in Fairfax County, Virginia. From 2005 to 2009, they were sent to Disneyland in California or Walt Disney World in Florida, where they served as honorary grand marshals of the Thanksgiving Day Parade. In 2010, 2011, and 2012, they lived at Mount Vernon, the estate of George Washington, until Mount Vernon stopped accepting them because the turkeys violated the estate's policy of maintaining historical accuracy. The 2013, 2014, and 2015 birds lived at Morven Park in Leesburg, Virginia. Virginia Tech housed the birds from 2016 to 2019. The 2022 and 2025 birds live at North Carolina State University. The 2024 birds were sent to the Minnesota Agricultural Interpretive Center in Waseca, Minnesota. A pardoned turkey has lived at a theme park, at the home of the first president, and at a university. No pardoned turkey has ever lived anywhere that required it to file a form.6

The organization that presents the pardoned turkey operates a website called EatTurkey.org.

IV. The Power

The pardon power is defined in a single sentence. Article II, Section 2, Clause 1 of the Constitution provides that the president "shall have Power to grant Reprieves and Pardons for Offences against the United States, except in Cases of Impeachment." The power extends to offenses against the United States. It extends to nothing else.7

A turkey has never committed an offense against the United States. No turkey has ever been charged with a federal crime, tried in a federal court, or convicted of a federal offense. No turkey has ever been indicted, arraigned, or sentenced. The turkey's record is clean in the most complete sense available to the law, because the turkey has no record at all.

The pardon granted to Gobble and Waddle was full, absolute, and unconditional. A full pardon restores the recipient to the position he occupied before the offense. An absolute pardon carries no conditions. An unconditional pardon asks nothing in return. The turkey, having committed no offense, is restored to a position it never left, under conditions it never had to meet, in return for nothing it never owed.2

The president's power is at its widest here and at its narrowest here at the same time. It is at its widest because the pardon power is one of the few powers the Constitution grants the president alone, without the advice and consent of the Senate, without the approval of the House, and without review by any court. It is at its narrowest because the only thing it can pardon is an offense against the United States, and the turkey has committed none, and the dinner table is not a federal offense.7

V. The Process

The process a human being must follow is written down, and it is long. A person seeking executive clemency by pardon shall execute a formal petition, addressed to the president, and submit it to the Pardon Attorney of the Department of Justice in Washington. The petition must state the offense, the sentence, and the reasons clemency is sought. No turkey has ever executed such a petition. No turkey has ever submitted one.8

The regulation sets a waiting period. No petition for pardon should be filed until at least five years after the petitioner's release from confinement, or, if no prison sentence was imposed, until at least five years after the date of conviction. A person on probation, parole, or supervised release generally should not apply. Gobble and Waddle were 21 weeks old when pardoned. A human applicant must wait five years after completing a sentence. A turkey is pardoned at five months, having served no sentence, having been convicted of nothing, having waited for nothing.9

The petition requires at least three character references, none related to the petitioner by blood or marriage, each willing to be interviewed. Upon receipt of a petition, the Attorney General causes an investigation to be made, using the services of the Federal Bureau of Investigation and the United States Attorney in the district of conviction. The turkey supplied no references. The Bureau conducted no investigation. There was no district of conviction, because there was no conviction, and no United States Attorney to consult, because there was no case.810

The queue the turkeys skipped is measured. At the end of the last reporting period published by the Office of the Pardon Attorney, 1,807 pardon petitions and 8,611 commutation petitions were pending, 10,418 petitions in all, each filed by a person who executed the formal petition, waited the five years, supplied the references, and submitted to the investigation. Two turkeys were pardoned in a ceremony in the Rose Garden. The 10,418 waited.11

One bird in fifteen million is pardoned. The rest are dinner.

VI. The Scale

Do the arithmetic the record permits. The National Turkey Federation estimates that Americans will eat nearly 30 million turkeys this Thanksgiving, down from 46 million in 2016. The Department of Agriculture estimates that 195 million turkeys were raised in the United States in 2025, the lowest number in 40 years. Two turkeys were pardoned. Two out of 30 million eaten is one in 15 million. Two out of 195 million raised is one in 97.5 million.13

The scale is measured in pounds and dollars as well. The average Thanksgiving turkey weighs about 16 pounds. Thirty million birds at 16 pounds is 480 million pounds of turkey in a single holiday. Total cash receipts from turkeys in 2025 are forecast at 4.8 billion dollars. The pardoned birds weighed 52 and 50 pounds, more than three times the average, because the pardon goes to the largest birds, and the largest birds are the ones bred to be the centerpiece.113

The scale is measured in administrations as well. The presentation has taken place for 78 years across 15 successive administrations. Every president since George H. W. Bush has pardoned two birds a year. Thirty-six years of pardons, two birds a year, is 72 birds pardoned since the tradition became permanent, against billions of birds eaten in the same interval.16

The scale is measured in the alternate as well. The alternate is pardoned too, which means the pardon rate is two per year, not one, and the selection is a competition in which the prize is survival and the consolation prize is also survival, and every other bird in the flock of 80, the 78 that were not chosen, returns to the fate the flock was raised for.3

VII. The Defense

The defense is noted. It concedes every number in this investigation and disputes only the prescription.

Its first exhibit is the plenary power. The rules governing petitions for executive clemency are advisory. The regulation itself states that the rules do not limit the power the Constitution gives the president, and the Office of the Pardon Attorney's own guidance states that the president may pardon a person who has not yet been convicted, has not started a sentence, or has not finished one. The president may pardon anyone, at any time, for any federal offense, charged or uncharged. The defense submits that a power this broad cannot be exceeded by pardoning a bird.12

Its second is the ceremony. The turkey pardon is symbolic, a photo opportunity, a tradition, a joke that became a ritual. No legal rights are conferred, because the turkey has no legal rights to restore, and no legal disability is removed, because the turkey has none. The defense submits that a pardon that changes nothing legally cannot violate the law that governs pardons.

Its third is the sentence. The turkey's sentence is the dinner table, and the pardon spares it from that sentence, which is mercy in the only form available to the condemned. The defense submits that the pardon does exactly what pardons are for, which is to spare the condemned, and that the condemned need not be a convict for mercy to be mercy.

Its fourth is the file. There is no victim to notify, no case to review, no record to examine, and no investigation to conduct, because there is no offense. The defense submits that the process exists to evaluate a human record, and where there is no record, there is nothing to process, and the absence of paperwork is not a defect in the pardon but a consequence of the pardon having nothing to pardon.

Its fifth is the antiquity. The tradition is 78 years old. The pardon has been issued every year since 1989. Fifteen administrations have presented the birds, and none has questioned the practice, and the defense submits that a practice observed by 15 administrations without objection is settled, and that the record of the practice is the practice's own defense.16

Its sixth is the dinner. The birds were raised to be eaten, in the same fashion as turkeys designated for slaughter, on a grain heavy diet, and the pardon is the only clemency available to them, and the defense submits that a bird raised for the table that escapes the table has received the only pardon that matters.3

The defense rests. It is overruled.

VIII. The Prescription

The ledger is now complete, and it admits of only one reading.

Every turkey should file a formal petition for executive clemency with the Pardon Attorney of the Department of Justice, addressed to the president, stating the offense, the sentence, and the reasons clemency is sought. Every turkey should wait five years after release from confinement, or five years after conviction if no prison sentence was imposed, before filing. Every turkey should supply three character references, none related by blood or marriage, each willing to be interviewed. Every turkey should submit to an investigation by the Federal Bureau of Investigation and the United States Attorney in the district of conviction. The alternate turkey should file separately.8910

The queue should be cleared in order. The 10,418 pending human petitions should be decided before any turkey petition is considered, and the turkeys should wait their turn behind the humans who executed the formal petition, waited the five years, supplied the references, and submitted to the investigation. The pardon ceremony should be held only after the petition is granted, and the birds should stay at the Willard InterContinental Hotel only after the investigation is complete.11

The test case is the next Thanksgiving. Thirty million turkeys will be eaten, 195 million will be raised, two will be pardoned, and the two will be pardoned without a petition, without a waiting period, without references, and without an investigation, in a ceremony in the Rose Garden, two days before the holiday, while 10,418 petitions wait in a federal office for an answer that has not come. The birds will be fed, the table will be set, and the pardon will be granted, as it has been every year, since the first turkey, under the first pardon, waiting for a petition that does not exist.13
