# 7-Critic Panel Evaluation: "Avalanches as Unlicensed Blasting Operations"

## 1. General Editor: 9.0/10
The article follows the Ergo canon precisely: long factual headline, scholarly deck, Roman-numeral sections building a systematic case from operation to scale to process to discharge to standard to defense to prescription, genuine footnotes with a Sources section, and the deadpan "Ergo." close. The arc escalates cleanly, and the load-bearing beam is the lawful charge limit against the unlicensed delivery: a hand charge capped at 4.5 pounds of TNT equivalent against a million-ton avalanche carrying the kinetic energy of roughly 150 tons of TNT, sixty-seven thousand times the limit, delivered 274 times a day. The defense section concedes the real escape hatches (explosives law regulates persons and explosives, not snow; there is no operator to license; the lawful blast is deliberate and planned while the natural slide is neither; the forecasting and control system already works) before overruling them, which is the genre's honesty mechanism, and it is unusually candid in admitting that the system regulates the fuse and the avalanche has no fuse. Minor weakness: Section II carries three distinct counts in sequence, the national estimate, the Colorado reporting gap, and the licensed shot count, which slightly crowds the section, though each earns its place on a distinct metric (how many slides, how many are seen, and how many are licensed).

## 2. Voice Coach: 9.0/10
Deadpan investigative register throughout, matching the rain and wildfire pieces, with the humor emerging exclusively from bureaucratic precision rather than jokes. Standout lines: "The difference between the licensed operation and the unlicensed one is not the force. It is the paperwork," "No charge was capped at 4.5 pounds, because the charge was the mountain itself, and the mountain does not weigh its charges," "The system regulates the fuse. The avalanche has no fuse," and "waiting for a form that does not exist." The piece never winks; the closest it comes is the hero image's inspector in a suit and hard hat holding a clipboard and a stick of dynamite beside a running avalanche, which lands as straight-faced procedural evidence. Excellent voice match.

## 3. Ethics: 9.0/10
All statutes, regulations, and study results cited are real and quoted or paraphrased accurately where quoted: the Washington explosives licensing requirement and its avalanche control provision, the Colorado Explosives Program permit types and its naming of avalanche control, the California avalanche blasting rule and its 4.5 pound hand-charge cap, daylight requirement, and written safety plan, the Washington administrative code on blasting crews and avalaunchers, the Forest Service estimate of about 100,000 avalanches a year, the Colorado Avalanche Information Center fatality statistics of 23 deaths a winter over the last ten winters and 37 in 2020-21, the 2,700 documented Colorado avalanches a year and the 5,555 recorded in 2023-24, the avalanche speeds of 80 miles per hour average and up to 200 for powder avalanches, the million-ton masses, the 48-times demolition force figure, the asphyxiation and trauma percentages, the Wellington 96 deaths and the Tunnel Creek 3 deaths, and the Colorado Department of Transportation figures for monitored paths, blaster shots, howitzers, closure costs, and the Vail Narrows fence. The deliberately wrong conclusion, that an avalanche is a blasting operation requiring a permit, is the disclosed conceit; the piece is honest about the escape hatch, that explosives law regulates persons and explosives and snow is neither, in Section VI, the same way prior pieces disclosed theirs. The kinetic energy conversion is explicitly flagged in footnote 14 as arithmetic, not a separately measured energy. No ethical concerns.

## 4. Social/Shareability: 9.0/10
"Your avalanche never filed for a blasting plan" is immediately graspable and funny at the headline level alone, and it carries a second hook most Ergo premises lack: the reader's own highway department already fires licensed shots 200 times a year and spends 325 million dollars on one pass to hold the snow in place, and then the mountain releases the same work for free. The 4.5 pounds against 150 tons is the single most shareable image; "A million-ton avalanche at 80 miles per hour carries the kinetic energy of roughly 150 tons of TNT" and "Two hundred licensed shots a year against one hundred thousand unlicensed releases" are strong pull-quote material. The hero image, an inspector with a clipboard and a stick of dynamite beside a running avalanche, is built for the thumbnail. High share potential.

## 5. Legal Accuracy: 8.5/10
The explosives licensing framework is accurately summarized: federal law makes it unlawful to engage in the business of importing, manufacturing, or dealing in explosive materials without a federal explosives license, Washington requires a license to manufacture, purchase, sell, use, possess, transport, or store explosives and addresses avalanche control expressly, Colorado issues Type I, Type II, and Type III explosives permits and names avalanche control among the regulated uses, California Title 8 Section 5357 requires a licensed avalanche blaster in charge, daylight blasting, a 4.5 pound hand-charge cap, and a written safety plan filed with the Division before blasting, and Washington Administrative Code 296-52-807 requires a licensed blaster and a trained assistant on every crew and Federal Aviation Administration approval for aerial blasting. The deliberately wrong conclusion, that an avalanche is a blasting operation, is the genre's disclosed conceit, and the piece never pretends any blasting permit application has a line for a discharge of 100,000 slides a year. Passes threshold, with the standard Ergo deduction for creative inference from regulatory text.

## 6. Research Rigor: 9.0/10
Strong sourcing across independent authorities: the Washington and California codes for the licensing rules, the Colorado Department of Public Safety for the permit types, the Colorado Avalanche Information Center for the fatality statistics and the accident archive, the United States Forest Service via SnoWest for the national avalanche count and the forecasting centers, the International Snow Science Workshop proceedings for the Colorado documentation rate and the cause-of-death review, the Northwest Avalanche Center for the Tunnel Creek incident report, the Colorado Department of Transportation's own capital request and safety program presentation for the highway paths, blasters, howitzers, and fence costs, and the Denver Post for the closure economics and the program history. Minor weakness: the characterization of avalanche debris setting up like concrete is stated without a direct citation, the 100,000-a-year national figure is a Forest Service specialist's estimate carried by a magazine rather than an agency publication, and the ten-times unreported estimate for Colorado comes from a practitioner blog rather than the Center itself. The disputed storm-total figures for Tunnel Creek are avoided entirely, and the piece uses the undisputed two-days-of-storm phrasing instead.

## 7. Data Presentation: 9.0/10
Numbers are presented with consistent precision and always sourced: about 100,000 avalanches a year against about 274 a day, 2,700 documented in Colorado a year against 5,555 recorded in 2023-24, 938 human-triggered against 117 caught and 6 fully buried, 200 licensed shots a year against 7 howitzers with a 7-mile range, 23 deaths a winter over the last ten winters against 37 in 2020-21, 328 Colorado deaths since 1950-51 against 6 a year, 80 miles per hour average against 190 for powder avalanches, a million tons against 300,000 cubic meters at Montroc, 48 times the force needed to demolish a frame house, 65 percent asphyxiation against 22 percent trauma, 96 dead at Wellington against 3 at Tunnel Creek, 278 of 522 paths monitored, 30 hours of Interstate 70 closure against 1 million to 1.6 million dollars an hour, and 4.5 pounds of TNT equivalent against roughly 150 tons. The comparison framework, lawful charge limit versus unlicensed delivery on the same TNT scale, is devastating and verifiable. Clear, consistent, sourced.
