## Round 2 — 8.57/10

## 1. General Editor — 8.6/10
Structure mirrors the exemplar cleanly: eight sections, Invention to Filing to Bounty to Ratification to Ledger to Arithmetic to Defense to Prescription, with the long declarative headline, deck, pullquotes, and "Ergo." close. The arc builds steadily from a factual oddity to an absurd prescription, and the deadpan register holds throughout. The Round 1 fixes land. Sixteen years / seventeen observances is now consistent; the bounty arithmetic is exact ($5M + $2.5M extension = $7.5M; 1:2,933 against the $22B single-year figure); the trademark is properly sourced to Justia (Serial 85155778, Reg. 3974211, Registered and Renewed); SBA figures use the single March 2023 vintage; and the $241B vs $220B gap is reconciled in the footnotes as Amex cumulative since 2010 versus SBA's surveys-since-2012 aggregate. The 73x and ~18% calculations also check out. Remaining weaknesses are editorial, not structural: the Section III pullquote ("Five million dollars bought the first observance") now contradicts the revised $7.5M full launch price beside it; "observed… by a hundred million people" generalizes the 2011-only 103M figure; fn8 still leans on an Instagram reel for a claim Justia already proves; and the Defense's opt-out paragraph repeats itself. Publishable, with the pullquote fix worth making first.

## 2. Voice Coach — 9.2/10
Fix verification: both winking flourishes are gone, neither "the day now observes itself" nor "the honest description of what happened" appears anywhere in the draft. "Nobody is compelled to queue" is gone; the opt-out paragraph is rewritten around compulsion to observe, open, and shop. One residual echo remains: "the day is voluntary for the seller as well as the shopper" is the exemplar's "the sale is voluntary for the seller as well as the shopper" with one word swapped. Cadence in sections V and VI is substantially fixed. Two long polysyndeton hammers survive (the 2024-survey sentence in V, the "Sixteen years have passed…" closer in VI), but both now read as the house rhythm rather than overuse. The deadpan register holds end to end; not one joke is told, every laugh comes from bureaucratic precision ("the small raised R that denotes private ownership of a public day," "the day falls today… on a Saturday the federal calendar does not contain"). All six pullquotes land, and the best two — "The holiday was filed before it was held" and "Two hundred and forty-one billion dollars, and not one receipt" — are pure precision, no wink.

## 3. Ethics — 8.0/10
Fix verification: the $241B / since-2012 point is fixed in substance. Section V now calls the total "the sum of surveys… conducted since 2012," and fn15 discloses the SBA's lower $220B+ aggregate "covering surveys since 2012." The "since the first one in 2010" phrasing survives only as an attributed Amex claim, immediately corrected. Elapsed years are now right throughout: 16 years since 2010, 17 observances, 15 years since the 2011 resolution and SBA co-sponsorship. Trademark sourcing and scope are fixed: fn7 is now the Justia USPTO record (Serial 85155778, Reg. 3974211, renewed Dec. 1, 2021, SMALL BUSINESS disclaimed), and the body and Section VII defense both state the mark covers only the combination as a brand, not the words. Unsourced absolutes are mostly fixed: "renewed" is now sourced; "only measurement" softened to "the measurement the company publishes." The 103M figure was not fully fixed: the deck no longer carries it, and Section IV correctly scopes 103 million to 2011, but Section II still asserted the day is "observed as a holiday, nationally, annually, by a hundred million people." That is the exact Round 1 error, relocated from deck to body rather than corrected. Residual: fn8 (Instagram reel) still cites the filing claim, though Justia now carries it; harmless but untidy.

## 4. Social/Shareability — 8.7/10
The opening clause — "American Express Invented a Holiday in 2010" — lands instantly and is the shareable hook; the rest of the house-style headline is unquotable in full and will truncate on social, though the trimmed title tag helps previews. Readers will share the first clause, not the whole sentence. Pull-quotes are excellent, and the draft's strongest asset. Six are ready-made for screenshots and captions: "A company founded to carry parcels by horse now owns a day of the week," "The holiday was filed before it was held," "Five million dollars bought the first observance of a national holiday," and best of all, "Two hundred and forty-one billion dollars, and not one receipt." "One day is worth seventy-three" is the sleeper. The hero image is apt and witty — quiet main street, calendar with Saturday circled and an ® beside it — but quieter and less thumb-stopping than yesterday's surging Black Friday crowd. Publishing on Small Business Saturday itself is the point, and the pairing with yesterday's "unpermitted mass gathering" piece creates a clean two-part Thanksgiving-weekend arc ("unpermitted" Friday, "unlicensed" Saturday) that invites reading and sharing both together.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 9.0/10
All three Round 1 fixes check out against the law. Trademark scope is fixed: Section II now limits the right to confusing use "as a brand in commerce" for "its own competing shopping event," states the registration disclaims SMALL BUSINESS, and says "the company owns the combination, not the words, and the words remain free." Section VII's Defense correctly adds the descriptive and nominative point: towns and newspapers may describe shops as small businesses on a Saturday. That tracks 15 U.S.C. section 1115(b)(4) fair use and likelihood-of-confusion doctrine. Intent-to-use is fixed: "The name was claimed before the day existed" replaces "owned," and Section II correctly identifies the Oct. 19, 2010 filing as intent-to-use under 15 U.S.C. section 1051(b), with first use in commerce Nov. 8, 2010. Federal holidays are fixed: Section VIII now says the holidays "are listed by statute, under 5 U.S.C. section 6103, which names eleven of them" — literally true of the section 6103(a) list. The overbroad "requires a statute" is gone. Residual nits: Section II still says holidays are created "by statute" without the executive-order qualifier, and Section I's "owns a day of the week" rhetoric could mislead a lay reader, though Section VII corrects it. S. Res. 320 correctly treated as non-law.

## 6. Research Rigor — 7.5/10
Fixed and verified against the live sources: Trademark — Justia record confirms Serial 85155778, filed Oct. 19, 2010, Registration 3974211 on June 7, 2011, renewed Dec. 1, 2021, disclaimer of "SMALL BUSINESS," first use Nov. 8, 2010. S. Res. 320 — Congress.gov Congressional Record confirms Nov. 10, 2011 unanimous consent, designation of Nov. 26, 2011, and the $68-per-$100 preamble. 5 U.S.C. section 6103 now cites Cornell LII. The 88% survey now cites the Amex newsroom release, verified live: 88%, highest since 2012, Sept. 9–11, 2026, n=1,008. SBA figures are now consistent: 33,185,550 / 33.2M, March 2023 FAQ in fn19–20; no 36.2M remains. Internal arithmetic checks out: 39 days, $5M + $2.5M = $7.5M, 1:2,933, 73x, ~18% decline. Not fully fixed: fn8 still cites the Instagram reel for the trademark, five times, alongside Justia. No govinfo link appears for S. Res. 320, and the womensbusinessdaily blog remains bundled in fn9. Most seriously, fn13 claims the $37.4B discount-revenue figure is "according to the company's 2025 annual report" but links only to legalclarity.org — a secondary breakdown, not Amex's report. Headline numbers ($241B/$22B/$18B) still rest on Inc., Rakuten, and DaysOfObservance roundups; SBA's own release says "over $220B" since 2012, a tension only partly explained by differing start years. Real improvement over Round 1's 6.0, but two claimed fixes are overstated and the headline ledger remains second-hand.

## 7. Data Presentation — 9.0/10
Round 2 verification — all five Round 1 flags confirmed fixed. Years: 2010 to 2026 is 16 years, 2011 to 2026 is 15, used correctly throughout; 17 appears only as the observance count (2010–2026 inclusive), and "seventeenth observance" for Nov 28, 2026 is correct. Launch cost: 200,000 × $25 = $5M day-of, plus 100,000 × $25 = $2.5M extension = $7.5M total. Ratio $22B ÷ $7.5M = 2,933.3, so 1:2,933 is correct. Cumulative total: $241B (Amex, since 2010) vs "over $220B" (SBA) is explicitly reconciled in fn15 — the SBA aggregate covers surveys since 2012 only. Body uses $241B consistently; fn2 ("north of $240B") and fn17 ("more than $220B") match their respective sources. SBA vintage: single March 2023 FAQ throughout (fn19, fn20) — 33,185,550 to "33.2 million," 61.7M, 46.4%, 43.5%, 99.9%, 81.7% all match body and footnotes. Date spans: Oct 19 to Nov 27, 2010 is 39 days; Nov 8 to Nov 27 is 19 days — both correct, "thirty-nine days" used consistently. Other arithmetic spot-checked: (22−18)/22 = 18.2% ≈ "about eighteen percent"; 1/365 = 0.27% and 20% ÷ 0.27% = 73x; 11 holidays listed under 5 U.S.C. section 6103; survey n=2,601 and n=1,008 consistent between body and footnotes. Two small nits: the Section III pullquote ("Five million dollars bought the first observance") restates the day-of figure without the extension a skimmer might miss; and "observed seventeen times since" in Section III is ambiguous against the correct "seventeenth observance" elsewhere. Neither is an arithmetic error.
