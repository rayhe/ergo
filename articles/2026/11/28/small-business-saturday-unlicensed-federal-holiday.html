Investigation About
Trademark Law & the Federal Calendar

American Express Invented a Holiday in 2010, Trademarked It Thirty-Nine Days Before the First One, Paid Shoppers Twenty-Five Dollars to Observe It, Got the Senate to Ratify It Unanimously, Got a Federal Agency to Co-Sponsor It, and Has Counted Two Hundred and Forty-One Billion Dollars of Spending on It in Surveys, and No Law Has Ever Created It

The holiday is a trademark that fits on no statute. It was filed in October 2010 and observed for the first time thirty-nine days later, paid for in statement credits, ratified by a Senate resolution that is not a law, co-sponsored by a federal agency that promotes a private mark, and measured ever since in surveys of what shoppers say they spent, and it has been observed every year since, in all fifty states, on a day the federal calendar does not contain. The United States has spent two centuries building a system that creates holidays by statute, registers trademarks by application, and counts spending by receipt. It has never licensed a holiday to a credit card company. This investigation examines the largest unlicensed federal holiday in American commerce, the company that invented it, the filing that owns it, the bounty that launched it, the resolution that ratified it, and the statute the record demands.

By Silas Pemberton · November 28, 2026 · 15 min read

I. The Invention

The holiday was invented in the middle of a recession, by a credit card company, in the loneliest spot on the calendar. American Express created Small Business Saturday in 2010, in the midst of the recession, on the Saturday after Thanksgiving, to encourage people to Shop Small and bring more holiday shopping to small businesses. The company says so itself, on its own history page, in the plainest possible language: in the midst of the recession in 2010, we created Small Business Saturday on the Saturday after Thanksgiving.12

The spot on the calendar was chosen with care, because it was the only spot left. Friday belonged to the big-box stores, and Monday belonged to the internet, and Saturday belonged to nobody. Black Friday was the day of the chains, and Cyber Monday was the day of the websites, and the small shop between them had no day at all, and American Express gave it one. The campaign was kicked off on November 8, 2010, in New York City, nineteen days before the first observance, and the first Small Business Saturday was held on November 27, 2010, between what the mayor of Boston called two of the busiest shopping days of the year.34

The first observance was held, fittingly, in a neighborhood. Boston's mayor signed a proclamation formalizing November 27 as Small Business Saturday, and the city promoted the campaign through its twenty Boston Main Streets districts, culminating in a celebration at the Roslindale Village Main Streets district annual tree-lighting event at three in the afternoon. The holiday began at a tree lighting in Roslindale Village, Massachusetts, nineteen days after it was announced in New York, and it has been observed every year since.45

The inventor was not a legislature, and it was not a town, and it was not a trade association. It was American Express, a company founded in 1850 as an express mail business in Buffalo, New York, with the merger of three express mail companies, a company that began by carrying parcels and became a company that carries payments. A company founded to move packages by horse and rail now owns a day of the week, and the day it owns is the Saturday after Thanksgiving.6

A company founded to carry parcels by horse now owns a day of the week.

II. The Filing

The holiday was filed before it was held. American Express Marketing & Development Corp. filed the trademark application for SMALL BUSINESS SATURDAY on October 19, 2010, Serial No. 85155778, thirty-nine days before the first observance on November 27, 2010. The mark was registered on June 7, 2011, as Registration No. 3974211, and it stands today as Registered and Renewed, renewed on December 1, 2021. The name was claimed before the day existed, and the day was an application in the federal trademark register before it was an entry on any calendar. Today Small Business Saturday is a registered trademark owned by American Express, and so is Shop Small, the slogan under which the day is observed.782

The order of events is the whole of the case. A holiday is ordinarily declared first and named second, by the authority that declares it, and the name follows the fact. Here the name was filed first and the fact followed, by the company that filed it, and the authority that declared the holiday was the United States Patent and Trademark Office, which registers marks, not holidays. The application was filed on an intent-to-use basis: the company intended to use the day, and then it did, and the record shows first use in commerce on November 8, 2010, the day the campaign was kicked off.7

Consider what the filing bought. A trademark is a right to stop other people from using a name as a brand in commerce, and the name in question is three ordinary words describing a size, a business, and a day. No other company may use the name as the brand of its own competing shopping event without the permission of American Express, and every town that prints the name on its banners prints a mark that belongs to a credit card company, and every newspaper, every mayor, every chamber of commerce that writes the name writes a trademark, and writes it with the small raised R that denotes private ownership of a public day. The registration itself disclaims the words SMALL BUSINESS, so the company owns the combination, not the words, and the words remain free, and the combination remains property, and the service the mark covers is stated in the register as promoting public awareness of shopping locally.27

The contrast is the federal calendar. The United States creates legal public holidays by statute, under 5 U.S.C. section 6103, which lists eleven of them, and a holiday enters that list when Congress puts it there, and the list contains New Year's Day, the birthday of Martin Luther King, Jr., Presidents Day, Memorial Day, Juneteenth, Independence Day, Labor Day, Columbus Day, Veterans Day, Thanksgiving, and Christmas. Small Business Saturday is not among the eleven, and no statute has ever added it, and the day is nevertheless observed as a holiday, nationally, annually, under a name that is registered to a private company.9

The holiday was filed before it was held.

III. The Bounty

A holiday that must be paid for is not a holiday. It is a promotion, and the promotion was priced at twenty-five dollars. In 2010, American Express offered a twenty-five dollar statement credit to the first one hundred thousand cardmembers who registered their card and spent at least twenty-five dollars at a locally owned, independent small business on Small Business Saturday. Two hundred thousand cardmembers participated on the day, and the company then extended the offer through December 31, 2010, to one hundred thousand additional cardmembers, at twenty-five dollars each.41011

The arithmetic is exact, and it is small. Two hundred thousand cardmembers at twenty-five dollars each is five million dollars, and five million dollars bought the first observance of a national holiday. The company then extended the offer to one hundred thousand more cardmembers through December 31, at twenty-five dollars each, two and a half million dollars more, so the full price of the launch was seven and a half million dollars, and the day has now been observed seventeen times in all, and the launch has never had to be bought again.10

The bounty had terms, and the terms are the tell. The credit was payable only to American Express cardmembers, and only for purchases made with an American Express card, and only at shops that accepted American Express, and online purchases, purchases at large national chains, purchases at franchised locations, and purchases at government agencies were all ineligible. The celebration was open to everyone, and the payment was open to cardmembers, and the shop that did not accept the card could not participate in the day that was supposedly held for its benefit.41112

The merchants were paid too, in a different currency. The first ten thousand business owners to sign up received one hundred dollars of free Facebook advertising to build buzz for the day, and for every person who liked Small Business Saturday on Facebook, American Express donated one dollar, up to five hundred thousand dollars, to Girls Inc., a group that empowers young women to be the entrepreneurs of tomorrow. The shops received advertising, and the charity received donations, and the cardmembers received credits, and the company received a holiday, and every party to the transaction was paid except the public, which received the bill in the form of a day it now observes for free.311

The reason for the bounty is not hidden, because the business model is not hidden. American Express earns discount revenue from the merchant on every transaction made with its card, thirty-seven point four billion dollars of it in 2025, its largest single revenue stream, so a day that sends millions of shoppers into small shops with American Express cards in their hands is a day that multiplies the company's own revenue, and the twenty-five dollar credit was an investment in the habit it was buying. The company did not create the holiday out of charity. It created the holiday out of arithmetic, and the arithmetic has held for sixteen years.13

Seven and a half million dollars bought the launch of a national holiday.

IV. The Ratification

The holiday was ratified the following year, by the United States Senate, unanimously, and the ratification changed nothing in the law. In 2011, the Senate unanimously passed a resolution in support of the day, and officials in all fifty states participated, and the day received a shout-out from the president of the United States. On November 10, 2011, the Senate agreed by unanimous consent to S. Res. 320, which designated November 26, 2011, as Small Business Saturday and supported efforts to encourage consumers to shop locally.19

A resolution is not a law. It is the sense of the Senate, expressed by unanimous consent, and it has no force of law, and it creates no holiday, and it amends no statute, and it binds no one. The Senate may resolve that a day shall be observed, and the day is observed, and the resolution is the reason given, and the reason is not a law. Small Business Saturday is not a federal holiday, and the federal holiday schedule lists Thanksgiving and nothing on the Friday or Saturday after it, and the day after Thanksgiving belongs to no statute at all.9

The federal government did not stop at resolving. The United States Small Business Administration, a federal agency, has co-sponsored the day since 2011, and the agency says so itself: the day was founded by American Express in 2010, and the agency has co-sponsored it since 2011, and the agency encourages Americans to shop small on it, every year, in official releases. A federal agency co-sponsors a private company's registered trademark, and promotes it, and counts it, and the promotion is paid for by the taxpayer and the benefit accrues to the mark.1415

The ratification was thorough, and it was entirely ceremonial. Mayors issued proclamations, and governors issued proclamations, and the Senate resolved, and the agency co-sponsored, and the president gave a shout-out, and not one of these acts has the force of law, and the day they all ratified is owned by a company that filed for it thirty-nine days before it existed. The government blessed a holiday it did not create, and the blessing is the only part of the holiday the government owns.149

The second year proved the ratification worked. In 2011, five thousand small businesses participated and one hundred and three million Americans shopped small on the day, and the day generated two point seven million likes on Facebook and became a top trending topic on Twitter, and the Senate's resolution had done what a statute could not: it had made a trademarked marketing campaign into an official day, without ever making it a law.16

The idea has since been exported. Britain holds its own Small Business Saturday on the first Saturday in December, a copy of the American day, on a different Saturday, under the same name, and the export proves the point the filing made: a day invented by a company can be adopted by a nation, and a nation can adopt it, and the company still owns the name.17

The Senate ratified a holiday it did not create, and the agency co-sponsors a trademark it does not own.

V. The Ledger

The ledger is the largest part of the file, and it is counted in surveys. American Express puts the cumulative reported total at two hundred and forty-one billion dollars spent at small businesses on Small Business Saturday since the first one in 2010, including about twenty-two billion dollars in 2024 and about eighteen billion dollars in 2025, a decline of about eighteen percent in a single year, and about seventeen billion dollars in 2023. The numbers are repeated everywhere, by the agency, by the press, by the chambers of commerce, and they are all the same numbers, from the same source, which is American Express.15217

The numbers are not receipts. They are answers. The American Express figures come from a survey of United States adults run for American Express, which scales up what people say they spent to the whole adult population, and the company says so in its own footnote: the spend figure is based on consumer self-report and does not reflect actual receipts, sales, or American Express Card Member spending data. The 2024 survey was conducted among a nationally representative sample of two thousand six hundred and one adults, on November 30, 2024, by email invitation and online questionnaire, and the twenty-two billion dollars is what those adults said they spent, projected onto the country.1417

The two hundred and forty-one billion dollars is therefore the sum of surveys of what shoppers remember spending, conducted since 2012, and the sum has been reported as spending, and no cash register has ever been opened to check it, and no card network has ever been asked to confirm it, and the company that owns the day also owns the survey that measures it, and the survey is the measurement the company publishes.1415

The ledger's other entries are also surveys, and they are also favorable. A 2026 American Express survey, released on November 12, found that eighty-eight percent of consumers are likely to shop at small businesses this holiday season, the highest level since 2012. A 2025 economic study commissioned by American Express estimated that sixty-eight cents of every dollar spent at a small business stays in its local community. Small business owners surveyed expect nearly twenty percent of their annual revenue to come from sales on Small Business Saturday alone.181417

The ledger must be read honestly, and the honest reading narrows it. The twenty-two billion dollars of 2024 is an estimate built from two thousand six hundred and one questionnaires, and the eighteen billion dollars of 2025 is the same estimate a year later, and the four billion dollar decline between them is a decline in what shoppers told a pollster, and the two hundred and forty-one billion dollar cumulative total is a sum of recollections, and the recollections are the record, and the record is the only evidence the holiday has ever produced that it works.1415

Two hundred and forty-one billion dollars, and not one receipt.

VI. The Arithmetic

The arithmetic begins with the size of the constituency the day claims to serve. According to the Small Business Administration's Office of Advocacy, there are thirty-three point two million small businesses in the United States, ninety-nine point nine percent of all businesses, employing sixty-one point seven million people, forty-six point four percent of the private-sector workforce, and accounting for forty-three point five percent of gross domestic product. A small business is defined, generally, as an independent business having fewer than five hundred employees, and eighty-one point seven percent of them have no employees at all.1920

The day's claim on that constituency is total. Thirty-three point two million businesses, and one day for all of them, and the day is owned by a company that is not one of them, and the day is promoted by an agency that serves them, and the agency's own release calls the day one of the most important economic drivers of the year. The smallest sector of the economy, by the count of firms, has the largest holiday, by the count of owners, and the holiday belongs to a card network.1419

The expectation the owners hold is the most striking number in the file. Small business owners surveyed expect nearly twenty percent of their annual revenue to come from sales on Small Business Saturday alone, which is to say that one day in three hundred and sixty-five is expected to produce a fifth of the year. An average day produces one three-hundred-and-sixty-fifth of a year's revenue, or about twenty-seven hundredths of one percent, and twenty percent is seventy-three times that. One Saturday is worth seventy-three ordinary days, by the owners' own expectation, and the day that is worth seventy-three days is a day no statute has ever created.14

The money must be set against the fee, and the comparison is the engine. Every purchase made with an American Express card pays the company a merchant fee, and the day exists to multiply those purchases, and the seven and a half million dollars of launch credits were the seed capital for a habit that now reports twenty-two billion dollars of spending in a single observance, and the ratio of the launch to the ledger is one to two thousand nine hundred and thirty-three, and the company publishes no figure for what share of the ledger passes through its own network.101315

The time must be set against the holiday, and the comparison is the indictment. Sixteen years have passed since the first observance, and the day has been held seventeen times, and the Senate has resolved in its favor, and the agency has co-sponsored it, and the mayors have proclaimed it, and the surveys have measured it, and the trademark has been renewed, and the statute has never been written, and the day falls today, November 28, 2026, the seventeenth observance, on a Saturday the federal calendar does not contain.114

One day is worth seventy-three.

VII. The Defense

The defense is noted. It concedes every number in this investigation and disputes only the prescription.

Its first exhibit is the trademark. A trademark is lawful, and filing a name before using it is standard practice, and intent-to-use applications are filed every day, and no law forbids a company from naming a day, and the name Small Business Saturday is a mark like any other, and the public remains free to hold a sale on Saturday without calling it that. A trademark does not forbid a town from describing its shops as small businesses on a Saturday, and it does not forbid a newspaper from writing the words; it forbids only confusing use of the name as a brand, and the registration itself disclaims the words SMALL BUSINESS.7

Its second is the bounty. A statement credit is a promotion, and promotions are lawful, and the twenty-five dollars was offered openly, and the terms were printed, and no shopper was compelled to register a card, and no shop was compelled to accept one, and the company paid for the promotion out of its own funds, and the shops that benefited kept the sales.410

Its third is the resolution. A Senate resolution is ceremonial, and ceremonies are what resolutions are for, and nobody in the Senate claimed to be making law, and the resolution designated a single year, 2011, and the day has continued by custom since, and custom is how most holidays actually work, and Thanksgiving itself was a custom long before it was a statute.9

Its fourth is the agency. The Small Business Administration exists to serve small businesses, and promoting a day that sends shoppers to small businesses is serving small businesses, and co-sponsorship is promotion, not endorsement, and the agency promotes many private initiatives, and the mark in the agency's press release is the company's, and the agency has never claimed to own it.1415

Its fifth is the ledger. The surveys disclose their method in the footnote, and the footnote is printed in the agency's own release, and a self-reported estimate is still an estimate, and every economic statistic begins as an answer to a question, and the company has never claimed the numbers were receipts, and the decline from twenty-two billion to eighteen billion dollars proves the survey can fall as well as rise.1415

Its sixth is the benefit. The day helps the shops it names, and sixty-eight cents of every dollar spent at a small business stays in the local community, and eighty-eight percent of consumers say they are likely to shop small this season, and thirty-three point two million businesses employ sixty-one point seven million people, and a day that moves money toward them is a day that works, whatever its paperwork says.171819

Its seventh is the opt-out. Nobody is compelled to observe the day, and no shop is compelled to open for it, and no shopper is compelled to shop on it, and a merchant that dislikes the trademark may hold a sale on Saturday and call it anything else, and the calendar is large, and the day is optional for the seller as well as the shopper, and no one is compelled to attend.2

The defense rests. It is overruled.

VIII. The Prescription

The ledger is now complete, and it admits of only one reading.

The legal public holidays are listed by statute, under 5 U.S.C. section 6103, which names eleven of them, and the day has no entry on that list, and the defect must be cured in one direction or the other. Congress must enact Small Business Saturday as the twelfth legal public holiday under 5 U.S.C. section 6103, with a date certain, the Saturday after Thanksgiving, and the federal calendar must be amended to contain the day that Americans already observe, or Congress must enjoin the observance as an unlicensed holiday, and the Senate must stop resolving in favor of days it has not enacted, and the mayors must stop proclaiming what they have not ordained.916

Every trademark that names a day of the week must be surrendered to the public domain. A size, a business, and a day are three ordinary words, and their combination describes a fact of the calendar, and no company may own a fact of the calendar, and the registration must be cancelled, or licensed royalty-free to every shop in America, in perpetuity, with the small raised R struck from every banner, every poster, and every press release, so that the day belongs to the people who observe it.7

Every federal agency must cease co-sponsoring a private mark. The Small Business Administration must either purchase the trademark at fair value, so that the government owns the day it promotes, or withdraw its co-sponsorship, and the agency's releases must stop carrying the registered mark of a credit card company, and the company must pay the agency for fifteen years of advertising, at the rate the agency would charge any other sponsor.1415

Every dollar of the ledger must be counted from receipts. The two hundred and forty-one billion dollars must be audited against cash registers, card networks, and tax filings, and the survey must be retired as an instrument of measurement, and the Bureau of Economic Analysis must publish the true figure, and if the true figure is smaller, the holiday must be resized to fit it, and if the true figure is larger, the company must explain why its own survey understated its own holiday.1415

The bounty must be restored as a standing obligation. The twenty-five dollar credit that launched the day must be paid to every shopper, every year, in perpetuity, as the price of observing a holiday that was bought and sold in its first year, and the shops that do not accept the card must be paid in cash, and the company that owns the day must fund the observance it invented, at the rate it set itself, twenty-five dollars per shopper, every Saturday after Thanksgiving.41016

The queue should be cleared in order. The statute should be enacted, and the trademark should be surrendered, and the agency should withdraw, and the ledger should be audited, and the bounty should be paid, and the day should be held, and the holiday should be licensed at last, since the day has carried a private name for sixteen years, in the open, on the public record, one shopper at a time.19

The test case is today. Small Business Saturday falls on November 28, 2026, the Saturday after Thanksgiving, sixteen years after a credit card company invented it, fifteen years after the Senate resolved in its favor, fifteen years after a federal agency began co-sponsoring it, and the shops will open in the morning, and the shoppers will arrive with their cards, and the surveys will be taken, and the day will be observed for the seventeenth time, in the open, over two hundred and forty-one billion dollars of reported spending and thirty-three point two million small businesses, one shopper at a time.1141519

Ergo.
