# 7-Critic Panel Evaluation: "Fireflies as Unlicensed Nighttime Light Display"

## 1. General Editor — 9.0/10
The piece follows the Ergo template faithfully and holds together as a systematic case: an absurdly exhaustive headline and a deck that restates the whole indictment, eight Roman-numeral sections escalating cleanly from the insect itself, through the chemistry of the light, the history of the research, the lighting law, the synchronous display, the scale arithmetic, a genuinely conceded defense, and an impossible prescription ending in "Ergo." The escalation works because each section adds a new load-bearing fact, the 560-nanometer peak, the femme fatale's stolen steroids, the 1985 cloning of luciferase, the 91.209 position-light rule, the 960-pass lottery, the 13-million-flash count, rather than merely restating the joke. The six pullquotes are well placed and mostly sharp, and the footnotes and Sources section are complete and properly linked. The main drag is inheritance: Section IV leans on this publication's earlier birds investigation for the jurisdictional hook instead of re-proving it, which is honest cross-referencing but means the piece's load-bearing definition arrives secondhand, and the headline, while in-format, buries its punchline, "No firefly has ever held an airworthiness certificate," at the very end of a very long sentence.

## 2. Voice Coach — 9.0/10
The voice holds the Ergo register almost throughout: flat, procedural, and relentlessly literal, with the humor carried entirely by the precision of the measurements and the legal citations rather than by any aside or wink. The cadence is the article's strength, with short declarative sentences and parallel constructions, "The government licenses the audience. It has never licensed the performers," doing the satirical work that adjectives never do. The few slips are small but audible: "The smallest drone the rule excuses is heavier than the largest firefly it would excuse" is convoluted on first read, and the closing test-case paragraph restates the headline arithmetic in a long polysyndetic chain instead of landing a fresh image the way the turkeys piece does with its unfiled petition. One terminology slip was caught and fixed before publication: the prescription originally announced the season by "Notice to Airmen," but the FAA renamed NOTAMs to Notices to Air Missions in 2021. Still, the final "Ergo." lands because the preceding prescription has earned its flatness, and the piece never once steps out of character to admire its own joke.

## 3. Ethics — 8.5/10
Spot-checked claims against their sources: the species counts (more than 2,000 worldwide, about 170 in North America) match the cited sources; the luciferin-luciferase chemistry and the nearly 100 percent efficiency figure match the cited explainer; the femme fatale mimicry and lucibufagin transfer match Eisner and colleagues (PNAS 1997); the synchronous pattern (five to eight flashes, about eight seconds of darkness) and the lottery figures (960 reservations, 120 a night, May 20-27, 2026) match the Park Service and the park's own announcements; the 91.209, Part 107, DOE 45 lumens per watt, and 47 U.S.C. 227(e) citations are all correct, and the scale arithmetic is internally consistent. The defense section is the piece's ethical strong point: it fairly presents the dispositive escape hatches, above all that the FAA's rules are written for persons operating contrivances and no court has held a beetle to be an aircraft, plus the 250-gram registration threshold, the general-service-lamp definition, the telecommunications-service limit on the spoofing statute, and the protective direction of the Endangered Species Act, before the disclosed "overruled" joke. Two real factual problems were found and fixed before publication: the prescription used the pre-2021 term "Notice to Airmen" (corrected to Notice to Air Missions), and the scale section's rounding language muddled 12.6 million into "about 13 million" through a confusing aside (rewritten as a straight rounding). Minor nits: the 18,000-applicants figure rests on a 2018 secondary report and is honestly hedged as "an earlier year"; the Lynn Faust discovery story rests on a minor blog rather than a primary source, though the Park Service's own "since 1993" peak-date record corroborates the timeline; and the blue ghost fact is cited to a staging-domain history URL. Nothing is invented or misattributed in substance, the deliberately wrong conclusion is honestly fenced by a complete defense, and the piece serves the educational mission well.

## 4. Social/Shareability — 8.5/10
The premise is instantly graspable in one sentence, fireflies fly at night with lights and no license, and it lands harder than the cicadas or pollen premises because nearly everyone has watched the subject. The piece is rich in quotable, tweet-length lines and surprising-but-true facts that travel well, from the femme fatale who fakes a mating signal to eat her suitor, to the government licensing the audience but not the performers, to 13 million flashes a night in a single valley. Concrete anchors help too: the Park Service lottery that turns away nine of ten applicants, the 560-nanometer glow, and the luciferase gene cloned in 1985 that now lights up laboratories on the one continent with no wild fireflies. The headline, however, fails as a social hook: at roughly 70 words it cannot be quoted, posted, or remembered, so all sharing work falls to the deck and pullquotes instead. Still very high share potential, docked mainly for a headline that buries its own punchline.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 8.5/10
Every citation checked against the primary sources holds up: Section 40102(a)(6) and 14 CFR 1.1 are quoted verbatim; the Pirker holding is stated accurately; Section 91.209(a) and (b) are quoted or paraphrased essentially verbatim; Section 91.205(c) correctly lists the night visual-flight-rules equipment; the Part 107 registration thresholds (0.55 pounds recreational, any weight commercial) and the Remote ID requirement are correct; Sections 107.29 (anti-collision lighting visible three statute miles) and 107.35 (one pilot, one aircraft) are stated correctly; the DOE backstop (87 FR 27439, effective July 25, 2022, 10 CFR 430.32(dd), $560 per-lamp penalty) checks out; Section 227(e) is quoted essentially verbatim; and the Bethany Beach proposal (89 FR 79857, October 1, 2024) is accurately described, including that the final determination is still pending. The article is honest about the decisive escape hatches: Section VII correctly states that no court has applied the aircraft definition to a living thing, that the firefly falls below the 250-gram threshold, that the lamp rule reaches only manufactured general service lamps, and that Section 227(e) reaches only telecommunications services. Two legal nits were found and fixed before publication: the NOTAM terminology (see Voice Coach), and the prescription originally assigned the firefly lottery "120 an hour," which misstated the 120-per-night figure (corrected to 960 permits for the season). None of these changes the verdict, because the deliberately wrong conclusion, that every firefly requires registration and approved position lights, is the genre's disclosed conceit, stated only after the article itself has laid out the law that defeats it, and the "defense rests, it is overruled" turn is the joke rather than a misstatement of law.

## 6. Research Rigor — 8.5/10
All five spot-checked claims check out in substance: the 560-nanometer emission peak and the 1985 de Wet cloning match the KEGG enzyme record; the synchronous pattern and season match the Park Service's own page; the 2026 lottery figures (960 passes, 120 a night, May 20-27) match the park's announcements; the Bethany Beach proposal matches the Federal Register citation; and the 14 percent threatened figure matches Fallon and colleagues (PLoS ONE 2021). The sourcing behind those true claims is nevertheless weak for a load-bearing investigation in places, with the central efficiency figures routed through an obscure explainer blog rather than a primary or textbook source, the Faust discovery story resting on a minor blog, the lottery-application figure resting on a 2018 entertainment site, and the blue ghost fact resting on a staging-domain page. The 2,000-firefly working figure for the scale section is an assumption rather than a measurement, though it is explicitly flagged as a conservative working figure and the arithmetic built on it is labeled arithmetic rather than measurement. One detail went beyond its cited source and was fixed before publication: the prescription's lottery arithmetic misstated the nightly figure. No load-bearing claim proved false.

## 7. Data Presentation — 9.0/10
All of the listed arithmetic recomputes correctly within rounding: 60 seconds divided by an 11-second cycle is 5.45 cycles a minute, times a midpoint of 6.5 flashes is 35.4 flashes a minute, fairly stated as about 35; 35 flashes a minute for 180 minutes is 6,300, fairly stated as about 6,000; 2,000 fireflies at 6,300 flashes each is 12.6 million, fairly stated as about 13 million; and 13 million a night for fourteen nights is 182 million, fairly stated as about 180 million. Precision is consistent at two significant figures, every key figure is hedged with "about" and footnoted, and the summed 180-million figure is explicitly flagged as arithmetic rather than a measurement, with the article itself noting the assumptions it rests on. The only nits are rounding propagation, since the unrounded 6,300 flashes give 12.6 million a night rather than the stated 13 million, a discrepancy under four percent that disappears if the rounded 6,000 intermediate is used; and a small internal tension, since the 2026 lottery issued 960 passes while the test-case paragraph says 18,000 people will apply for 960 passes, mixing the 2026 pass count with an earlier year's applicant count, though both figures are individually sourced and the mixing is visible in the text. None of these is a material error, and the 9-of-10 rejection rate also checks out at 18,000 applicants for 1,800 passes.
