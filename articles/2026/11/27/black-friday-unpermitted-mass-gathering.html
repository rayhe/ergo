Investigation About
Consumer Economics & Black Friday

Black Friday Has Killed Seventeen People and Injured One Hundred and Twenty-Five Since 2006, Its Name Was Coined by the Philadelphia Police to Describe a Traffic Jam, Its Doorbusters Are Priced Below Cost in Quantities Too Small to Serve the Crowd They Summon, and the Fine for the Death Was Seven Thousand Dollars

The sale is a gathering that fits on no permit. It is advertised for a single hour, it summons thousands of people to a single doorway, it offers a handful of televisions priced below cost to a crowd numbered in the thousands, and it has killed seventeen people and injured one hundred and twenty-five since 2006, and the federal government has no standard that governs the crowd, and its own guidelines tell retailers to obtain permits and file crowd plans, and the guidelines are voluntary, and no law requires either. The United States has spent a century building a system that permits parades, licenses lotteries, and caps the occupancy of every room in which a crowd gathers. It has never permitted Black Friday. This investigation examines the largest unpermitted mass gathering in American retail, the name that warned of it, the bargain that summons it, the crowd it produces, the death it caused, and the permit the record demands.

I. The Name

The name has meant ruin twice before it meant shopping. On September 24, 1869, the financiers Jay Gould and Jim Fisk tried to corner the market in gold, buying up as much of the nation's gold as they could to drive up the price, and the scheme collapsed and the stock market crashed, and the day entered the language as Black Friday, a day of financial ruin. The name was a warning before it was a holiday.1

The name was used again in the 1950s, by factory managers, for a different kind of absence. So many workers called in sick on the day after Thanksgiving that managers referred to the day as black Friday, and the term described a workforce that did not show up. The day after Thanksgiving was already a day when the ordinary rules did not hold, and the name already described it.2

The name in its present sense was coined by the police. In the early 1960s, officers of the Philadelphia Police Department began using the phrase Black Friday to describe the chaos that resulted when large numbers of suburban tourists came into the city to begin their holiday shopping and, in some years, to attend the annual Army-Navy football game played in Philadelphia on the Saturday after Thanksgiving. The crowds created traffic jams, accidents, and shoplifting, and the officers worked longer shifts than usual, and the day was not a term of endearment to them. The name was a complaint filed by the people who had to manage the crowd.34

The merchants hated the name and tried to replace it. Philadelphia retailers attempted to put a prettier face on the day by calling it Big Friday, and the rebrand failed, and the police name stuck. The name first appeared in print in a 1961 issue of Public Relations News, which described the Philadelphia police using Black Friday and Black Saturday for the post-Thanksgiving chaos, and by 1966 a Philadelphia newspaper column was noting that officers considered it to be Black Friday and that it was not a term of endearment to them. The name stayed local for years, and it did not spread outside Philadelphia until about 1987, and it did not become accepted nationwide until the mid-1990s.345

The shopping itself is older than the name. The notion of consumers shopping the day after Thanksgiving has been pushed by retailers since the late nineteenth century, and the day after Thanksgiving was a shopping day for decades before anyone called it Black Friday. The name arrived in the 1960s, and the crowd it described had been gathering for generations.6

The explanation most Americans believe about the name is the newest part of the story, and it is the part the retailers invented. The story holds that the day is the point in the year when retailers move from operating at a loss, in the red, into profit, in the black, and that the day therefore marks the turn of the ledger. Retailers promoted that explanation in the 1980s, and by the late 1980s retailers nationwide had adopted it, and within a decade the police origin was so completely forgotten that most Americans assumed the profits story had always been true. The name that described overworked police officers and jammed streets had been rebranded as a win, and the rebrand worked so well that the warning was forgotten.145

The name was a complaint filed by the people who had to manage the crowd.

II. The Doorbuster

The sale is built around a single device, and the device is the doorbuster. A doorbuster is a deeply discounted item, stocked in limited quantity, advertised at a named hour, for the purpose of drawing a crowd through the door. It is a loss leader, which is to say it is priced at or below cost, and the retailer loses money on the item by design, and the store does not expect to profit on the item itself. The store expects to profit on the crowd the item summons, and the crowd is the product, and the television is the bait.727

The advertisement states the price and the hour, and it does not state the quantity. The quantity is described only as while supplies last, which is the entire inventory of the offer, and the advertisement does not say how many units that is. The shopper knows the price, and the shopper knows the hour, and the shopper does not know whether the store holds ten of the item or one hundred, and the only way to find out is to be at the door when it opens. The uncertainty is the mechanism, and the mechanism is the point.78

The mechanism has a record, and the record is a list of fights over a small number of cheap things. In 2011, shoppers at a Walmart in the Little Rock, Arkansas, area lost their composure over waffle makers priced at two dollars. In the same year, at a Walmart in Rome, New York, two shoppers were sent to the hospital with back injuries after being pushed to the ground when a brawl erupted near the cell phone display. In the same year, at a Walmart in Kinston, North Carolina, customers crowded around cell phones that had been discounted from approximately two hundred dollars to about thirty-five dollars, and an off-duty police officer working security discharged pepper spray into the crowd, reportedly affecting around twenty people. The items were a waffle maker, a telephone, and a telephone, and the injuries were real.8910

The most documented doorbuster fight of that year took place in California. Shortly after 10:20 p.m. on Thanksgiving night in 2011, at a Walmart in Porter Ranch, a woman used pepper spray on fellow shoppers as a crate of Xbox 360 video game units was being unwrapped, and about twenty people suffered minor injuries, and at least ten of the injuries were due to rapid crowd movement in the panic that followed. The woman finished her shopping and left the store unnoticed, and she turned herself in a day later, and the police did not have enough evidence to charge her, and she walked free. The store had summoned a crowd for a video game console, and the crowd had armed itself, and the law had nothing to say about either fact.119

The device works because the offer expires when the stock does, and the stock is small by design, and the shopper who arrives second loses. The deals retain their popularity because they capitalize on the fear of missing out, and the thrill of a limited-time offer and the potential to miss a bargain keep shoppers engaged year after year. The store sells the fear, and the television is only the occasion for it, and the fear is free, and the television is not.7

The store sells the fear, and the television is only the occasion for it.

III. The Crowd

The crowd is the hazard, and the hazard is measured in density, not in size. An individual occupies a floor space of about 30 by 60 centimeters, and at densities of one to two people per square meter, individuals can move freely without contact. At three or four people per square meter, the risk is low. At five people per square meter, it becomes more difficult for individuals to move. At six to seven people per square meter, individuals become pressed against each other and can be unable to move voluntarily, and the crowd begins to behave like a fluid, with shockwaves passing through it as pressures change. At eight to ten people per square meter, individuals are pressed against each other and may be swept along against their will, and the pressure on each individual can crush or asphyxiate them while they are still upright. The distance from a busy store to a lethal one is about four extra people in the space of a small dining table.12

The cause of death in these events is not what the newspapers report. Most crowd deaths are compressive asphyxia, which is external compression on the chest and abdomen that prevents the chest from expanding to draw breath, and the airway is usually clear, and the problem is that breathing requires the ribcage to move, and in a dense crowd the surrounding force prevents that movement. People do not die because they panic. They panic because they are dying. Trampling, which dominates the accounts, is secondary, and it typically occurs as a consequence of the primary mechanism, not as a cause in itself. The danger is the density, and the density is a decision made in advance, by whoever decided how many people could be in that space and by which route.1213

The critical factor is crowd density rather than crowd size, and a store entrance is a bottleneck engineered to produce density. The whole crowd must pass through a few meters of doorway at a single named hour, and the doorway is the narrowest point in the building, and the hour is the narrowest point in the day, and the advertisement has arranged for the maximum number of people to arrive at the narrowest place at the narrowest time. The National Fire Protection Association recommends one trained crowd manager for every 250 people in most assembly occupancies, which means a crowd of two thousand requires eight trained managers, and the store that summons two thousand people to one door has undertaken to manage a crowd that would fill a small theater, in a vestibule, at five in the morning.1214

The crowd is summoned, not spontaneous. The advertisement names the hour, and the shoppers gather in the predawn darkness, and the line forms hours before the doors open, and the store knows how many will come, because the store told them when to come. The crowd does not assemble by accident. It assembles by invitation, and the invitation is printed in the newspaper, and the invitation names a price.15

People do not die because they panic. They panic because they are dying.

IV. The Death

The death took place on November 28, 2008, at a Walmart in Valley Stream, New York, on Long Island. A crowd of about two thousand shoppers had gathered outside the store in the predawn darkness, waiting for the five o'clock opening of the annual sale, and at 4:55 a.m., five minutes before the store was set to open, the crowd crashed through the sliding-glass doors and rushed into the store toward the discounted merchandise, and the surge knocked down several employees who had been trying to hold back the throng. One of those employees was thirty-four-year-old Jdimytai Damour, a temporary worker from Queens, who was knocked onto the linoleum floor and killed as customers surged past him, and he died of asphyxiation, and shoppers stepped over his body to get to the electronics aisle.1516

About eleven other people were injured in the surge, among them a pregnant woman, and the store's managers, upon learning that an employee was fatally injured, attempted to close the store, and the customers kept shopping, determined to get what they came for. The store was not closed by the death. The store was closed by the managers, and the customers declined to leave, and the sale continued over the body of the man who had opened the doors.1617

The government investigated, and the investigation produced a fine of seven thousand dollars. The Occupational Safety and Health Administration cited Walmart for inadequate crowd management following the death, and its inspection found that the store's employees were exposed to being crushed by the crowd due to the store's failure to implement reasonable and effective crowd management principles, and the citation was issued under the general duty clause, because there exists no OSHA standard on crowd control, and the citation carried a proposed fine of seven thousand dollars, the maximum penalty allowed under the law for a serious violation. The maximum fine the law allowed for a death was seven thousand dollars.1618

The county settled separately, and the settlement was larger than the fine. The Nassau County District Attorney reached an agreement in which Walmart was spared criminal charges by agreeing to set up a four hundred thousand dollar victims' compensation fund, to donate one and a half million dollars to the community, and to provide fifty jobs annually to Nassau teenagers, and to improve post-Thanksgiving sale crowd control at its New York stores. The agreement was summarized as about two million dollars in various penalties, and the district attorney noted that a criminal charge would have accomplished much less, since the maximum criminal penalty for a corporation convicted of a felony is ten thousand dollars, and the maximum for a misdemeanor is five thousand dollars. The criminal law's largest possible fine for the death was ten thousand dollars, and the safety law's largest possible fine was seven thousand dollars, and the two maxima together were seventeen thousand dollars.1719

The company then spent years fighting the seven thousand dollar fine. Walmart battled the citation for years, arguing that the general duty clause was being read too expansively, and it spent millions of dollars fighting a fine of seven thousand dollars, and it ultimately gave up and paid the fine, dropping its challenge in 2015, seven years after the death. The fine was seven thousand dollars, and the fight over it cost millions, and the fight lasted seven years.1720

The maximum fine the law allowed for a death was seven thousand dollars.

V. The Ledger

The death was not the only entry in the ledger. Since 2006, the Black Friday Death Count project has tracked at least seventeen deaths and one hundred and twenty-five injuries tied to Black Friday shopping events across the country, and the project assumes many more have gone unreported. The count is kept by a private tracker, and the tracker assumes many more injuries have gone unreported.2021

The injuries have a geography, and the geography is the store. A 2019 analysis found that sixty-nine percent of Black Friday incidents occur inside retail stores, where shoppers compete for limited supplies of goods, and roughly twenty percent occur at the store entrance as crowds pour through the doors, and eleven percent take place outside, where customers race for parking spaces or drive on the roads tired after a long day of shopping. The same analysis found that Walmart accounted for seventy percent of all reported Black Friday incidents, while accounting for only three point four percent of Black Friday foot traffic in 2018, nearly double its next competitor, Target, at one point eight percent. The largest retailer in the country accounts for seven in ten incidents on three in one hundred shoppers.7

The ledger's other entries are a catalog of small catastrophes. In 1983, a crowd of one thousand turned violent outside a department store in Wilkes-Barre, Pennsylvania, over Cabbage Patch dolls, and a woman broke her leg and lost her doll, and the manager wielded a baseball bat to avoid facing a similar fate. In 2011, sixty-one-year-old Walter Vance collapsed while shopping for Christmas decorations at a Target in West Virginia on Black Friday, and other shoppers stepped over his body in their rush to grab deals, and a few nurses and a paramedic at the store attempted to resuscitate him, and he did not survive. In 2012, a store manager at a Walmart in Hampton, Virginia, was knocked to the ground and trampled by a rush of Black Friday shoppers flooding through the entrance. In 2012, at an Urban Outfitters in Santa Monica, California, roughly one hundred customers had gathered outside the store, and their eagerness to get inside caused a large glass door to shatter, and about five people suffered minor injuries. In 2013, an eleven-year-old girl and an elderly woman were trampled when Black Friday shoppers stampeded through the doors of a Walmart in New Boston, Ohio.7208

The ledger must be read honestly, and the honest reading narrows it. The seventeen deaths the tracker counts include shootings and stabbings in parking lots, and a man who collapsed of a heart condition while shopping, and one trampling. Damour is the single death by trampling in twenty years of tracking, and the rest are crimes and collapses that happened to take place on the same day, at the same sale. The count is real, and the count is seventeen, and the count is also a measure of everything that happens when two hundred million people are told to arrive at once, not only of what the crowd itself does.2021

The largest retailer in the country accounts for seven in ten incidents on three in one hundred shoppers.

VI. The Arithmetic

The money is the largest part of the file, and it is counted precisely. On Black Friday 2025, shoppers in the United States spent a record eleven point eight billion dollars online, up nine point one percent from 2024, according to Adobe, and Cyber Monday brought in fourteen point two five billion dollars, the biggest online shopping day ever recorded, and the five days from Thanksgiving to Cyber Monday brought in forty-four point two billion dollars online. A record two hundred two point nine million people shopped over the Thanksgiving weekend, up from one hundred ninety-seven million in 2024, according to the National Retail Federation, and one hundred sixty-two million of them shopped on Black Friday itself. The sale is the largest retail event in the country, and it grows every year, and online Black Friday sales have grown from seven point four billion dollars in 2019 to eleven point eight billion dollars in 2025, a rise of fifty-nine percent in six years.2223

The forecast for this year is larger still. Adobe forecasts that shoppers will spend twelve point nine billion dollars online on Black Friday, November 27, 2026, up nine point two percent from the record of 2025, and the National Retail Federation expects November and December retail sales to reach one point zero one three trillion dollars, up four point two percent. The doors open today, and the forecast is that more money will pass through them than ever before.23

The fine must be set against the money, and the comparison is the indictment. Seven thousand dollars is fifty-nine millionths of one percent of eleven point eight billion dollars, which is to say it is zero point zero zero zero zero five nine percent of a single day of online sales. It is the maximum the law allowed. The settlement the county extracted, about two million dollars, was about two hundred and eighty-six times the fine, and the settlement was still less than two hundredths of one percent of the day's online sales.161922

The deaths must be set against the shoppers, and the comparison is the defense. Seventeen deaths among two hundred two point nine million shoppers is one death for every eleven point nine million shoppers, and one hundred and twenty-five injuries among seventeen deaths is about seven injuries for every death, and seventeen deaths in twenty years of tracking is fewer than one death per year. The sale is the largest retail event in the country, and the arithmetic of the ledger is, by any measure, small.2022

The time must be set against the death, and the comparison is the shame. Eighteen years have passed since Damour died, and the sale has been held eighteen times since, and the tracker has counted seventeen deaths and one hundred and twenty-five injuries in the twenty years it has kept count, and the fine has not changed, and the standard has not been written, and the permit has not been required, and the doors open today at the same hour, for the same price, to the same crowd.1620

Seven thousand dollars is fifty-nine millionths of one percent of a single day of online sales.

VII. The Defense

The defense is noted. It concedes every number in this investigation and disputes only the prescription.

Its first exhibit is the name. A police complaint from 1961 does not make a sale illegal, and a name is not a permit, and the fact that officers dreaded the day is a fact about traffic, not a fact about retail. The name warned of congestion, and the congestion was managed, and the sale went on, and the name is now a brand worth billions.3

Its second is the doorbuster. A loss leader is lawful, and selling below cost to draw traffic is standard retail practice, and no law caps the discount, and no law requires the stock to match the crowd, and the shopper who arrives late loses nothing but a bargain. The pepper spray was a crime by a shopper, not a policy of the store, and the store did not hand her the can.711

Its third is the guidelines. The federal government did respond to the death, and in November 2009 the Occupational Safety and Health Administration published its Crowd Management Safety Guidelines for Retailers, and in 2010 it wrote to the chief executives of fourteen major retail companies urging them to take precautions, and the guidelines cover planning, pre-event setup, the sales event, and emergencies, and retailers adopted barricades, rope lines, numbered wristbands, staggered openings, and Thanksgiving-night openings that spread the crowd across more hours. The guidelines are voluntary, and voluntary is how the system chose to respond.2425

Its fourth is the ledger. Sixteen of the seventeen deaths were not crush injuries, and the shootings and stabbings in parking lots are crimes, and the collapse was a heart condition, and the single trampling in twenty years is a tragedy, not a pattern, and a pattern requires more than one point. The tracker counts everything that happens on the day, and the day is the busiest shopping day of the year, and the busiest day will always have the most incidents.20

Its fifth is the internet. The crowd has moved online, and over the 2025 Thanksgiving weekend one hundred thirty-four point nine million people shopped online against one hundred twenty-nine point five million in stores, and the door is no longer the only entrance, and the sale that once required a body at a doorway now requires only a browser. The density problem is solving itself, one checkout at a time.22

Its sixth is the opt-out. In 2015, REI closed all one hundred forty-three of its stores on Black Friday, and paid twelve thousand employees to take the day off and go outside, and the company forfeited one of its ten best sales days of the year, and the market did not collapse, and no law compelled the closure. A store can simply not hold the sale, and the sale is voluntary for the seller as well as the shopper, and nobody is compelled to queue.26

Its seventh is the fine. Seven thousand dollars was the statutory maximum for a serious violation, and the law's number is the law's number, and the county's settlement of about two million dollars was the real penalty, and the company paid it, and it paid the fine as well, and it improved crowd control at its New York stores, and the system worked as designed, and the design is the law.1617

The defense rests. It is overruled.

VIII. The Prescription

The ledger is now complete, and it admits of only one reading.

Every Black Friday sale is a mass gathering, and every mass gathering requires a permit, and the advertisement, which states the price and the hour, states no permit. Before a store advertises a doorbuster, it must file a crowd management plan with the local fire marshal and the police, and the plan must state the expected crowd, the number of trained crowd managers at one per two hundred and fifty persons, the barricades, the rope lines, the separate entrance for staff, the maximum occupancy, and the emergency procedures, and the fire marshal must approve it, and the permit must be posted in the window before the doors open, and no permit means no sale. The federal guidelines already tell retailers to ensure that all permits and licenses are obtained, and the guidelines have been waiting since 2009 for a retailer to follow them.1424

Every doorbuster is a lottery, and every lottery requires a license. The store advertises a prize, a television priced below cost, and it offers a small number of prizes to a large number of entrants, and entry is obtained by queuing in the dark, and the odds are never published, and the drawing is held at the door, at five in the morning, by force. The doorbuster must be registered as a sweepstakes, and the odds must be published in the advertisement, in the same type as the price, and the advertisement must state the number of units, the number of entrants expected, and the odds of winning, and a shopper who reads the advertisement must know that two thousand entrants are competing for ten prizes before the shopper leaves the house.715

Every entrance must be managed to the density the science allows. No doorway may admit a crowd denser than five persons per square meter, and the line must be kept away from the entrance so the crowd can be divided into small groups, and numbered wristbands or tickets must give the earlier arrivals first access, and the doors must not open until the crowd managers are in place at the sides of the entering public, and the store must stop admitting customers when it reaches maximum occupancy, and the exit doors must never be blocked or locked, and the employee who opens the door must be the employee who can close it.1224

The fine schedule must be indexed to the sale it fails to govern. Seven thousand dollars was the maximum the law allowed for a death, and the maximum must be set as a fraction of the day's sales, and the fraction must be large enough to be noticed, and the fine for a death must exceed the profit on the doorbuster that caused it, and the fine must be assessed per shopper summoned, not per violation found, and the company that spends millions fighting a seven thousand dollar fine must find that the fine is no longer worth fighting, because it is no longer seven thousand dollars.1622

The queue should be cleared in order. The plan should be filed, and the permit should be issued, and the odds should be published, and the wristbands should be numbered, and the managers should be posted, and the doors should be opened, and the sale should be held, and the gathering should be permitted at last, since the sale has carried the police name for sixty-five years, in the open, on the public record, one shopper at a time.324

The test case is today. Black Friday falls on November 27, 2026, the day after Thanksgiving, sixty-five years after the Philadelphia police gave it its name, the eighteenth year since a man died opening the doors of a store in Valley Stream, and the doors will open at five in the morning, and the televisions will be priced below cost, and the crowd will number in the thousands, and the permit will not exist in the numbers the arithmetic demands, and the largest unpermitted mass gathering in American retail will be held for the sixty-fifth time, in the open, over eleven point eight billion dollars of online sales and one hundred and sixty-two million shoppers, one shopper at a time.31622
