# Ergo Review: Daylight Saving Time as Unapproved Human Experiment

**Topic:** The twice-yearly clock change under 15 U.S.C. §260a as the largest unconsented federally conducted human experiment in history: the statute advances every time zone one hour at 2:00 a.m. on the second Sunday of March and restores it at 2:00 a.m. on the first Sunday of November; the Secretary of Transportation administers the schedule and may seek federal injunctions against violators; the Department's own Inspector General reports that at least 45 states have proposed legislation to change DST observance since 2015; peer-reviewed studies document a ~5% rise in heart attacks (NEJM 2008, Swedish AMI registry), a 6% rise in fatal traffic crashes (Current Biology 2020, 732,835 FARS records, ~28 deaths/year), and 5.7% more workplace injuries with 67.6% more lost workdays (Journal of Applied Psychology 2009, DOL/MSHA data); the AASM (2020, reaffirmed 2024) calls for abolition in favor of permanent standard time; Congress ran year-round DST in 1974 and repealed it before the two-year trial ended; the DOE's congressionally ordered efficacy study measured 1.3 TWh, 0.03% of annual electricity, ±40% statistical variation; the Senate passed the Sunshine Protection Act by unanimous consent in 2022 and the House never voted; and 45 CFR §46.111 requires IRB approval, risk minimization, and informed consent before any federally conducted research involving human subjects may proceed, which has never occurred.
**Author:** Kit (with Ray)
**Date:** 2026-10-02

## Headline
"Section 260a of Title 15 at 15 U.S.C. § 260a Provides That the Standard Time of Each Zone Shall Be Advanced One Hour at 2 o'clock Antemeridian on the Second Sunday of March and Restored at 2 o'clock Antemeridian on the First Sunday of November, the Secretary of Transportation May Seek Injunctions in Federal District Court Against Anyone Who Violates the Schedule, the Department's Own Inspector General Reports That at Least 45 States Have Sought to Escape the Schedule Since 2015, Peer-Reviewed Studies Document More Heart Attacks, More Fatal Traffic Crashes, and More Workplace Injuries After the Spring Advance, the American Academy of Sleep Medicine Calls for the Practice to Be Abolished, Congress Ran the Experiment Year-Round in 1974 and Repealed It Before the Two-Year Trial Ended, the Energy Savings Congress Ordered Measured Came to Three One-Hundredths of One Percent, 45 CFR § 46.111 Provides That No Federally Conducted Research Involving Human Subjects May Proceed Without Institutional Review Board Approval, No Board Has Ever Approved the Time Change, and the Largest Unconsented Human Experiment in American History Runs Twice a Year by Statute"

## Thesis
The statute mandates a twice-yearly one-hour intervention applied to the entire population of the observing states. The Department of Transportation administers it, may enjoin violators in federal court, and admits it cannot repeal it. The intervention's adverse events are documented in the peer-reviewed literature: more myocardial infarctions, more fatal crashes, more workplace injuries, more medical errors. The stated benefit, energy conservation, was measured by the Department of Energy at 0.03% of annual electricity use with ±40% statistical uncertainty. The exemptions (Arizona, Hawaii) function as a non-participant cohort. The 1974 year-round trial was explicitly time-limited and was stopped early for futility and public harm. Under the Common Rule, federally conducted research involving human subjects requires prospective IRB review, risk minimization, equitable subject selection, and informed consent. None has ever occurred. The intervention continues.

## The Deliberately Wrong Conclusion
In reality, legislation is not "research" under the Common Rule: 45 CFR 46.102(l) defines research as a systematic investigation designed to develop or contribute to generalizable knowledge, and DST was designed to save energy, not to produce knowledge. The Common Rule binds federally conducted or supported research, not statutes. The adverse-event literature is contested: a 2025 Duke study of 168,000 MI patients across 1,124 hospitals found no significant association between the clock changes and MI incidence or outcomes. The fall transition is mildly protective and shows no workplace-injury effect. Democratic authorization is the consent mechanism for laws, and two states have opted out. All stated honestly in Section X, then overruled in Section XI: the 1974 trial was explicitly an experiment with a built-in stopping rule; EPAct 2005's "right to revert" is a data-safety-monitoring clause by another name; the growing adverse-event literature is generalizable knowledge being harvested from the subject population without a protocol; and a statute's authorization is not informed consent. Therefore.

## Research Notes
- 15 U.S.C. §260a(a) quoted verbatim via Cornell LII: 2 o'clock antemeridian, second Sunday of March to first Sunday of November; §260a(b) express preemption of state laws; §260a(c) Secretary of Transportation enforcement by district-court injunction. The 1983 amendment substituted "Secretary of Transportation" for the Interstate Commerce Commission.
- EPAct 2005 §110: changed the dates (first Sunday of April → second Sunday of March; last Sunday of October → first Sunday of November), effective 2007; §110(c) ordered the DOE energy report; §110(d) reserved Congress's right to revert, via the Statutes at Large.
- 49 CFR §71.2 restates the mandate and the state-exemption right; DOT's own page states it "oversees the Nation's time zones" and "uniform observance of Daylight Saving Time," that it cannot repeal DST, that states may exempt themselves by law, and that states may not choose permanent DST.
- BTS history: 1883 railroad four-zone system; ICC given time-zone authority in 1918; transferred to DOT on its 1966 creation; Mercer County, ND (2010) the most recent time-zone-boundary change, granted on "convenience of commerce."
- DOT OIG report ST2022037 (Sept. 20, 2022): since 2015, at least 45 states proposed legislation on DST observance; Senate Sunshine Protection Act (March 2022) and House Energy and Commerce hearing cited as the motivation for the audit.
- NEJM (Oct. 30, 2008), Janszky & Ljung, Karolinska: Swedish AMI registry since 1987; ~5% increase in AMI in the first week of summer time, concentrated in the first three weekdays; 1.5% decrease for one day after the fall return; probable causes "disruption in the chronobiological rhythms, the loss of one hour's sleep and the resulting sleep disturbance."
- Current Biology (Jan. 30, 2020), Fritz et al., Vetter (CU Boulder): 732,835 fatal accidents from FARS, 1996–2017, all DST-observing states; 6% risk increase in the workweek after the spring transition; 5.7 additional fatal accidents per day Monday–Friday; ~28 deaths per year; 626 of 8,958 over 22 years potentially preventable; risk moved with the 2007 date change (causal evidence); Arizona and Indiana excluded as non-observers; farther west in a time zone = higher risk.
- Journal of Applied Psychology (2009), Barnes & Wagner (Michigan State): DOL and MSHA data; 40 minutes less sleep; 5.7% more workplace injuries; 67.6% more workdays lost; phase delays (fall) had no significant effects.
- Sleep abstract via Smithsonian: 18% increase in medical errors from human mistakes during the spring-forward period.
- AASM position statement (J Clin Sleep Med 2020;16(10):1781–1784): seasonal changes should be abolished in favor of fixed year-round standard time; acute transition incurs "significant public health and safety risks, including increased risk of adverse cardiovascular events, mood disorders, and motor vehicle crashes"; reaffirmed and updated in 2024 (PMID 37904574).
- 1974: Emergency Daylight Saving Time Energy Conservation Act, year-round DST as a two-year trial; repealed in 1974 before the trial ended; public opposition centered on dark winter mornings; per David Prerau, it did not reduce energy consumption as intended (NPR via WAMC).
- DOE Report to Congress (Oct. 2008): 1.3 TWh total savings over the four extended weeks; 0.5% per EDST day; 0.03% of 2007 annual electricity (3,900 TWh); 17 TBtu (0.02% of primary energy); ±40% statistical variation at 95% confidence; savings 3–5 evening hours, small morning increases; no evidence of gasoline savings (Live Science).
- Sunshine Protection Act: passed the Senate by unanimous consent March 15, 2022 (Rubio); BuzzFeed reported many senators were unaware the request had been made and Tom Cotton would have objected; the 117th Congress ended without a House vote. 2026: House Energy and Commerce voted 48–1 in May; Reuters (July 13, 2026) reports supporters themselves cite "sleep disturbances, greater workplace injuries and more car crashes"; rival "Sunshine for Our Kids Act" would set permanent standard time instead.
- 45 CFR §46.111 quoted verbatim via Cornell LII: risks minimized; risks reasonable in relation to anticipated benefits; equitable selection (with "particularly cognizant" attention to children as vulnerable); informed consent sought per §46.116 and documented per §46.117; data monitoring provisions.
- Contrary evidence for the defense: JAMA Network Open (2025), Duke Clinical Research Institute: 168,000 MI patients across 1,124 hospitals, 2013–2022; no significant association between the clock changes and MI incidence or in-hospital outcomes; reported via diabetes.co.uk. This is the strongest counterweight and it goes in Section X, not in a footnote.
- Soft spots: the Common Rule application is the site's deliberately wrong move and is disclosed as such; the "experiment" framing leans on the 1974 trial's explicit trial design and EPAct's reversion clause, which are real but do not make the statute "research" under 46.102(l); the "billions affected" scale line is Vetter's quote, attributed, not the article's own arithmetic; the stroke literature is folded into AASM's "adverse cardiovascular events" rather than separately sourced.
