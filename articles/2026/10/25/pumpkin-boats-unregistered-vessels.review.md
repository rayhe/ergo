# 7-Critic Panel Evaluation: "Pumpkin Boats as Unregistered Vessels"

## 1. General Editor — 9.0/10
The article follows the Ergo canon precisely: long factual headline, scholarly deck, Roman-numeral sections building a systematic case from record to fleet to waterway to carriage rules to numbers to paperwork void to state permit to asymmetry to definitional escape hatch to conclusion, genuine footnotes with a Sources section, and the deadpan "Ergo." close. The arc escalates cleanly: the voyage, the fleet that produced it, the river at night, the checklist, the hull-number system, the empty file, the 2025 Oregon permit expansion that removes the length excuse, the licensed-boater comparison, the honest hatch, the conclusion. The load-bearing beam is the Section V hull-number move, that a numbering system built on manufacturers, transoms, and permanently affixed characters has no purchase on a carved rind, and it holds through all ten sections. The Section IX escape-hatch beat, declining to use a hatch marked manually propelled, recreational, operator-held, and Washington, mirrors the earthworms piece's sharpest structural choice without repeating its wording. Minor weakness: the vessel-definition move overlaps the older bathtub article's use of 1 U.S.C. § 3, though this piece leans on it far less and its subject is an actually navigated craft rather than a hypothetical one.

## 2. Voice Coach — 9.0/10
Deadpan investigative register throughout, matching the earthworms and bats pieces, with the humor emerging exclusively from bureaucratic precision rather than jokes. Standout lines: "The hull's only marking, applied by hand, was a denial that the vessel was a hallucination," "The chain of custody for a hull number runs from a manufacturer, or from a state administrator, to a hull. It does not run from a vine," "It has a pollination date instead of a model year," and "it requires nothing still, but faster." The piece never winks; the closest it comes is "which is nothing, until it is carved," which lands as straight-faced procedural suspicion. Zero em dashes in the draft, well within house discipline. Excellent voice match.

## 3. Ethics — 9.0/10
All regulations and event facts cited are real and quoted accurately where quoted: the Guinness record (1,214 pounds, 169 inches around, 45.67 miles, 26 hours, three legs, previous record 39.17 miles), the regatta history (since 2004, founders, Windsor inspiration, quarter-mile heats, forklift launches), the 2026 regatta date and venue from the City of Tualatin, the Coast Guard carriage rules (one approved life jacket per person, efficient sound-producing device under 39.4 feet), Rule 25(d)(ii) for vessels under oars, the 33 CFR Part 181 hull-number placement and character rules, the Oregon Waterway Access Permit expansion under HB 2982 with its $6/$20/$35 options and $115 fine, and the Washington human-power and racing exemptions. The deliberately wrong conclusion, that pumpkin boats constitute an unregistered fleet in violation, is the disclosed conceit, and the piece is unusually honest about the limits of its own evidence: Section IV states that the published account does not mention a life jacket, whistle, or lantern, not that Kristensen carried none, and Section IX discloses the operator-held nature of the Oregon permit and the Washington exemptions. No ethical concerns.

## 4. Social/Shareability — 9.0/10
"A man paddled a 1,214-pound pumpkin 45 miles down the Columbia, at night, with a soup can for a bailer" is immediately graspable and funny at the headline level alone, and the hero image, a boating inspector studying a pumpkin boat beside a clipboard, is built for the thumbnail. The most shareable paragraphs are the soup-can bailer, the painted words "it's real" as the hull's only marking, and the pollination date instead of a model year. The one-week-old 2026 regatta gives it a news hook the earthworms piece lacked. High share potential.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 8.5/10
The citations are accurate: the BoatUS summary of Coast Guard carriage requirements, 46 CFR 25.25-5, 33 CFR 83.25(d)(ii), 33 CFR Part 181 Subpart C and 181.31, Oregon ORS 830.615 to 830.630 as amended by HB 2982, and Washington WAC 308-93-030(4). The piece correctly states the lenient vessels-under-oars lights rule rather than overstating it, and correctly notes that Coast Guard inspection applies to commercial and passenger vessels, not to a grower paddling his own gourd. The deliberately wrong conclusion, that the fleet is an unregistered fleet in violation, is the genre's disclosed conceit, and Section IX states the escape hatch explicitly. Passes threshold, with the standard Ergo deduction for creative inference from regulatory text and for the definitional overlap with the bathtub article.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0/10
Strong sourcing across independent authorities: Guinness World Records for the voyage (primary event record, with dates, weights, distances, and conditions all taken from the single account), the City of Tualatin and Tualatin Life for the 2026 regatta, Travel Portland for the over-2,000-pound figure and the event's annual cadence, Cornell LII for the CFR text, the Oregon State Marine Board for the permit program, and Washington's administrative code for the exemptions. Competing characterizations are handled carefully: the account's silence on safety equipment is presented as a documentation gap, not as proof of absence, and the author's arithmetic (fine-to-permit ratio, hypothetical fleet sizes) is explicitly labeled as such. Minor weakness: the regatta's early history (founders, Windsor inspiration, spectator growth) rests on Wikipedia, though the 2026 event facts are corroborated by the city and local press.

## 7. Data Presentation — 9.0/10
Numbers are presented with consistent precision and always sourced: 1,214 pounds, 169 inches around, 555.2 kilograms, 429.26 centimeters, 73.50 kilometers, 45.67 miles, 26 hours, three legs, previous record 39.17 miles, 35-mile-per-hour wind, 7:03 a.m. landing, 5 hours 22 minutes rest, quarter-mile race, over 2,000 pounds for regatta pumpkins, over 1,000 pounds for growers' boats, $6/$20/$35 permit options, $115 fine, 19.17 fine-to-permit ratio, one-fourth-inch hull characters, 39.4-foot sound-device threshold. The comparison framework, licensed boater versus pumpkin on the same river at night, is devastating and verifiable. Clear, consistent, sourced.
