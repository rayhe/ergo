# 7-Critic Panel Evaluation: "Ogallala Aquifer as Unregistered Underground Storage Tank"

## 1. General Editor — 9.0/10
The article follows the established Ergo format precisely: statute-dense headline, scholarly deck, Roman-numeral sections building a systematic case, 23 genuine footnotes with a Sources section. Structure escalates cleanly from notification requirement → tank definition → performance standards → regulated universe → the tank → inventory → leak → contents → defense → arithmetic → conclusion. The "Defense" section (IX) plays the same honest-escape-hatch role as the fungi piece's, disclosing the regulated-substance problem in the open. Minor weakness: Section VIII (The Contents) is the thinnest section, essentially setup for the notification-form punchline; it earns its place but does the least analytical work.

## 2. Voice Coach — 9.5/10
Dead-serious investigative tone throughout, matching the fungi and earthquake pieces. Humor emerges exclusively from deadpan precision, never from jokes. Standout lines: "The tank's corrosion protection consists of being made of dirt," "The tank's spill prevention equipment consists of the Great Plains, which slope gently eastward," "It misses by approximately 948 trillion gallons minus 1,100," "The implementing literature uses the word 'leakage' without irony and without a permit application attached," "The tank took more than ten thousand years to fill. The notification form takes approximately twenty minutes," "roughly the gross domestic product of the entire planet, assessed against a hole in the ground with no owner of record." The closing "Therefore." lands the house cadence. Excellent voice match.

## 3. Ethics — 9.0/10
All statutes and regulations cited are real and quoted accurately: 42 U.S.C. 6991a(a)(1)-(3) (18-month notification, 30-day new-tank rule, retired-tank reporting), 6991(1) (UST definition with all nine exclusions correctly enumerated), 6991(2) (regulated substance), 6991(8) (release), 6991e(d)(1) and (a)(3) (penalties), 40 CFR 280.20 and the 2016 secondary-containment rule. All USGS figures verified against primary sources: 174,000 sq mi, eight states, 2.91 billion acre-feet (2015), 273.2M acre-feet decline, +84/-234 ft water levels, <1 in/yr recharge, McNutt "10,000 years" quote, 81,000 acre-ft/yr "leakage to streams," arsenic/uranium detections. All EPA program figures verified: 534,189 active tanks, 583,313 releases, 530,454 cleanups, 52,859 remaining, 81M people within a quarter mile, 60.9% compliance, 1985 bare-steel finding. The article's deliberately wrong conclusion, that an aquifer is a UST, is the genre's disclosed conceit, and Section IX honestly concedes the regulated-substance escape hatch. Minor caveat: the "65 percent under Nebraska" figure is from 1990 data; the proportion is approximately right but dated. No ethical concerns.

## 4. Social/Shareability — 8.5/10
"It misses by approximately 948 trillion gallons minus 1,100" and "$91 trillion assessed against a hole in the ground with no owner of record" are immediately shareable. The gas-station scale comparison (47 billion service-station tanks) gives readers a concrete anchor, and "the notification form takes twenty minutes; the tank took 10,000 years to fill" is the piece's thesis in one line. Slightly less universally graspable than the cicadas or earthquake pieces because RCRA Subtitle I is more obscure than noise law or explosives; the deck does the setup work, but the premise needs two sentences where the best pieces need one. Still strong share potential.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 9.0/10
Statutory citations verified against the current Code: the May 8, 1986 deadline is correctly derived (18 months after November 8, 1984); the nine exclusions at 6991(1)(A)-(I) are all correctly stated, including the 1,100-gallon farm/residential threshold the article resists rounding to 110; the 40 CFR 280.20 purpose clause is quoted verbatim; the April 11, 2016 secondary-containment trigger and 280.43(g) interstitial monitoring are correct. The penalty provisions are correctly attributed: $10,000 per tank for knowing failure to notify (6991e(d)(1)), $25,000 per day of continued noncompliance (6991e(a)(3)). The deliberately wrong conclusion is disclosed as wrong in Section IX, which finds the defense "unpersuasive on the merits and dispositive as a matter of law." Passes threshold.

## 6. Research Rigor — 8.5/10

Neither affects the thesis. Meets the bar.
