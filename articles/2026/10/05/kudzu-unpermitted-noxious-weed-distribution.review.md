# 7-Critic Panel Evaluation: "Kudzu as Unpermitted Noxious-Weed Distribution"

## 1. General Editor — 9.0/10
The article follows the Ergo canon precisely: long factual headline, scholarly deck, Roman-numeral sections building a systematic case from introduction to subsidy to reversal to repeal to present violation, genuine footnotes with a Sources section, and the deadpan "Ergo." close. The arc escalates cleanly and the load-bearing beam, the subsidy-reversal-repeal triptych (the government paid to plant it, listed it, then repealed the list instead of enforcing it), holds through all six sections and delivers the strongest closing paragraph of the recent run: "The law paid for the vine, changed its mind, repealed itself, and left the South to the plant." The 7.4-million versus 227,000 acre discrepancy beat in Section V is the piece's sharpest single observation and is handled with unusual honesty for the genre. Minor weakness: Section IV's "the vine is not a person" disclosure, while ethically required, briefly deflates the prosecutorial momentum before Section V rebuilds it; and the headline runs long even by Ergo standards, though length is the house style.

## 2. Voice Coach — 9.5/10
Deadpan investigative register throughout, matching the coffee and bird-nest pieces, with the humor emerging exclusively from bureaucratic precision rather than jokes. Standout lines: "The vine kept the receipt," "It grew a foot a day throughout," "Each new vine is a distribution event. The statute prohibits distribution. The arithmetic follows," and the closing triad "subsidized, then listed, then delisted by repeal." The piece never winks; the closest it comes is "the first documented case of a weed with better press than the agency tracking it," which lands as straight-faced statistical suspicion. The "it must be said" disclosure mirrors the bird-nests piece's honest escape hatch without breaking register. Excellent voice match.

## 3. Ethics — 9.0/10
All statutes, dates, and figures cited are real and sourced: the 1876 Centennial Exposition introduction, the 85-million-seedling distribution and $8/acre payments, the 3-million-acres-by-1946 figure, the 1953/1970/1997/2000 reversal sequence, the Plant Protection Act's prohibition and penalty provisions as summarized by Congress, the nine-state noxious weed listings, and the Texas "illegal to sell, distribute or import" language quoted verbatim from the Native Plant Society of Texas. Three pre-publication judgments were applied during drafting: the Mississippi State Extension figure of 300,000 acres planted by 1946 was set aside in favor of the 3-million-acre figure supported by three independent sources (Wikipedia, UGA Extension, Scientific American); the 1997 versus 1998 federal-listing date discrepancy was resolved in favor of 1997 per Wikipedia with the Ingenta 1998 date noted as the minority; and the 70-million-grown versus 85-million-distributed seedling figures were reconciled in the prose as grown versus distributed. The deliberately wrong conclusion, that the vine itself is the distributor in violation, is the disclosed conceit; the piece is honest about the persons-only scope of the statutes in the same way prior pieces disclosed theirs. No ethical concerns.

## 4. Social/Shareability — 9.0/10
"The government paid farmers $8 an acre to plant a weed across 3 million acres, listed it as a federal noxious weed, then repealed the weed law instead of enforcing it" is immediately graspable and funny at the headline level alone. "The vine kept the receipt" is the single most shareable line of the recent run; the 400-pound taproot and foot-a-day growth figures are vivid pull-quote material; the hero image, an inspector holding a violation notice before kudzu-swallowed telephone poles, is built for the thumbnail. Slightly less universally graspable than the coffee premise because the payoff requires the four-decade chronology, but the deck does that work and the subsidy-repeal irony needs no expertise. Very high share potential.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 8.5/10
The statutory summaries are accurate: the Plant Protection Act's prohibition on unauthorized interstate movement, the Secretary's authority to restrict noxious-weed movement, and the criminal and civil penalty provisions, all per the Congress.gov bill summary; the Federal Noxious Weed Act repeal of sections 2801-2813 in 2000 accurately described; the Texas noxious-weed distribution prohibition quoted verbatim. The deliberately wrong conclusion, that the vine's self-propagation constitutes unpermitted "distribution" under state law, is the genre's disclosed conceit, and the piece never pretends the statutes apply to plants, stating plainly that they prohibit persons. Passes threshold, with the standard Ergo deduction for creative inference from regulatory text.

## 6. Research Rigor — 8.5/10
Strong sourcing across independent authorities: Congress.gov for the Plant Protection Act, the Native Plant Society of Texas and two land-grant extensions (Mississippi State, UGA) for the botany and state listings, Penn State Sea Grant for the economics, Scientific American for the cultural history, and a peer-reviewed 2014 paper via Ingenta for the 31-state range and soybean-rust host findings. Weakest link: the Channing Cope and Kudzu Club of America color (20,000 members) rests on a single September 2026 Space Daily history piece, a space-news outlet running outside its beat; the claim is peripheral color rather than load-bearing, and is attributed in the footnote. The Wikipedia chronology is tertiary but each date is corroborated by at least one extension or primary source in the footnotes.

## 7. Data Presentation — 9.0/10
Numbers are presented with consistent precision and always sourced: 1876, 1883, 1902, 1905, $8/acre, 85 million seedlings, 3 million acres by 1946, 1953/1970/1997/2000, nine states, 31 states, one foot per day, 60 feet per season, 400-pound taproots, 30 vines per crown, $100-500 million per year, $5,000 per hectare per year, $1.5 million per year in power-line damage, 7.4 million versus 227,000 acres with the 2,500-acre annual increase. The dueling acreage figures are presented side by side with both attributions rather than cherry-picked, which is the honest choice and also the funnier one. Clear, consistent, sourced.
