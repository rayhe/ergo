# 7-Critic Panel Evaluation: "Maple Trees as Unregistered Food Facilities"

## 1. General Editor — 9.0/10
The article follows the Ergo canon precisely: long factual headline, scholarly deck, Roman-numeral sections building a systematic case from the registration requirement through the manufacturing process, the scale of the enterprise, the tree-is-the-manufacturer inversion, the failed exemptions, cGMP, the inspection void, the adulteration paradox, and the arithmetic of non-registration, genuine footnotes with a Sources section, and the deadpan "Ergo." close. The arc escalates cleanly, and the load-bearing beam, the manufacturer/dehydration-contractor inversion built on FDA's own B.1.15 line-drawing, holds through all ten sections. Minor weakness: Section V's exemption-by-exemption catalog is necessarily list-like, and the headline's final clause telegraphs the punchline the body earns more gradually.

## 2. Voice Coach — 9.5/10
Deadpan investigative register throughout, matching the salmon and weather-forecast pieces, with the humor emerging exclusively from bureaucratic precision rather than jokes. Standout lines: "The sugarhouse is a dehydration contractor," "The regulatory framework assumes a counterparty with hands," "The compliance rate is not low. It is undefined," "a corporation is, like a tree, an entity that cannot hold a pen but can be held liable," and "The trees have not renewed. They did not register in the first instance, so there is nothing to renew, which the regulation does not list as an excuse." The piece never winks; the closest it comes is "The forms are available by calling 1-800-216-7331," which lands as straight-faced public service. Excellent voice match.

## 3. Ethics — 9.0/10
All statutes, regulations, and guidance cited are real and accurately characterized: FD&C Act §415 (21 U.S.C. §350d), FSMA §102, 21 CFR 1.225/1.227/1.230/1.231, 21 U.S.C. §331(dd), 21 U.S.C. §333, 21 CFR Part 117, and 21 U.S.C. §374, all verified against Cornell LII, eCFR mirrors, and FDA sources. Biological and industry facts verified: Jones's Rule of 86, the 40:1 ratio at 2% sugar, 66-67° Brix at 219°F, Vermont 2024 figures (3.108M gallons, 8.4M taps, $95M) via USDA NASS as reported by the Vermont Agency of Agriculture, national figures (5.86M gallons, 17.1M taps) via NASS, and FDA's B.1.15 maple Q&A quoted in substance via the National Sustainable Agriculture Coalition. The 124M/234M-gallon sap figures are transparent arithmetic derivations from the 40:1 ratio. The deliberately wrong conclusion, that maple trees are unregistered food facilities, is the disclosed conceit; the piece states the honest escape hatch explicitly in the conclusion, that the statute regulates persons and a tree is not one, in the same way prior pieces disclosed theirs. No ethical concerns.

## 4. Social/Shareability — 9.0/10
"The largest unregistered food manufacturing operation in American history is conducted by organisms that cannot hold a pen" is immediately graspable and funny at the headline level alone. "The sugarhouse merely dehydrates. The tree manufactures" and "The compliance rate is not low. It is undefined" are strong pull-quote material. The hero image, an FDA inspector with a clipboard confronting a tapped maple tree in a snowy sugarbush, is built for the thumbnail. The timely hook, that the 2026 biennial renewal window opened three weeks ago and the trees have not renewed, gives it news peg energy. Very high share potential.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 8.5/10
The food-law citations are accurate: §415's registration mandate and pre-operation requirement, §1.227's facility definition, §1.230's biennial renewal window, Form FDA 3537, the §331(dd) prohibited act and §333 criminal penalties, Part 117's preventive-controls framework including the PCQI and food safety plan, §704 inspection authority, the B.1.15 harvesting-versus-manufacturing distinction, the §1.227(b)(2) private-residence exemption, and the foreign-facility U.S.-agent and UFI requirements. The deliberately wrong conclusion, that trees are facilities subject to registration, is the genre's disclosed conceit, and the piece never pretends any court has held a tree to be an establishment or structure. Passes threshold, with the standard Ergo deduction for creative inference from regulatory text.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0/10
Strong sourcing across independent authorities: USDA NASS via the Vermont Agency of Agriculture and Morning Ag Clips for production figures, FDA guidance via NSAC for the B.1.15 Q&A, the Connecticut Maple Syrup Producers Association for the prohibited-act language, Cornell LII for the statute text, Champlain Orchards and Northern Woodlands for process chemistry, the Stuyvesant educational materials for freeze-thaw mechanics, king.senate.gov for the 2016 congressional letter, The Maple News for the FDA "drop dead" response, and FERN for the fake-maple and Canada figures. Minor weakness: the private-residence exemption exposition leans on a maple-trader forum post quoting FDA text rather than the eCFR directly, though the quoted language is accurate.

## 7. Data Presentation — 9.0/10
Numbers are presented with consistent precision and always sourced: 3.108M gallons, 8.4M taps, 0.37 gal/tap, $95M, 5.86M gallons, 17.1M taps, 846K (NY), 701K (ME), ~80% (Canada world share), 40:1, 43 gallons at 2% via the Rule of 86, 66-67° Brix, 219°F, 60,000 BTUs/gallon, 392,217 registered (Dec 2009), 182,147 (Jan 2021), 48%/7% (OIG), 124M and 234M gallons of sap derived transparently. The comparison framework, registered human facilities versus the unregistered arboreal sector, is devastating and verifiable. Clear, consistent, sourced.
