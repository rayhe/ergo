# Ergo Review: Birds as the Largest Unregistered Aircraft Operation

**Topic:** Birds as unregistered, uncertificated, unpiloted-by-licensed-airman aircraft under 49 U.S.C. 40102(a)(6) as construed in Administrator v. Pirker: 296,613 documented collisions with unregistered operators 1990-2023, 19,603 in 2023 alone, zero N-numbers issued, zero pilot certificates held, zero ADS-B transponders carried.
**Author:** Kit (with Ray)
**Date:** 2026-10-15

## Headline
"Section 40102(a)(6) of Title 49 Defines 'Aircraft' as 'Any Contrivance Invented, Used, or Designed to Navigate, or Fly In, the Air,' the National Transportation Safety Board Held in Administrator v. Pirker That the Definition Is as Broad as It Is Clear and Reaches Any Device Used for Flight in the Air, Manned or Unmanned, Large or Small, Federal Regulation Requires Every Civil Aircraft to Be Registered Under 14 CFR 47.3, to Carry a Current Airworthiness Certificate Under 14 CFR 91.203, to Be Operated by a Certificated Pilot Under 14 CFR 61.3, and to Broadcast ADS-B Out in Controlled Airspace Under 14 CFR 91.225, Billions of Birds Occupy American Airspace, Not One Has Ever Been Issued an N-Number, Not One Carries a Transponder, Not One Holds a Pilot Certificate, and the Largest Unregistered Aircraft Operation in American History Has Feathers"

## Thesis
The statutory definition of "aircraft" (49 U.S.C. 40102(a)(6)) reaches any contrivance invented, used, or designed to navigate, or fly in, the air. The NTSB held in Pirker (Order EA-5730, 2014) that the definition is "as broad as [it is] clear, but clear nonetheless," expressly contemplating paper airplanes. A bird satisfies the disjunctive "used" prong continuously. The registration (14 CFR 47.3), airworthiness (14 CFR 91.203), pilot certification (14 CFR 61.3), and ADS-B (14 CFR 91.225) requirements therefore apply to billions of operators. Compliance is zero across all four regimes.

## The Deliberately Wrong Conclusion
Birds are not "contrivances" in the human-artifice sense; the definition was written for machines, the Chicago Convention governs aircraft of contracting states, and the Migratory Bird Treaty Act shields the violators from enforcement. The article discloses all three defenses explicitly in Section IX, then overrules them on the Board's own reasoning: warned the definition would reach paper airplanes, the Board reached anyway, and the statute joins its verbs with "or."

## Research Notes
- 49 U.S.C. 40102(a)(6), 44101, 46301, 46306(b)(6)-(7) (uscode.house.gov, verified this run; 46306 carries up to 3 years for knowingly operating unregistered aircraft and for serving as an airman without a certificate).
- Administrator v. Pirker, NTSB Order No. EA-5730 (Nov. 18, 2014): "any device used for flight in the air," "manned or unmanned, large or small," "as broad as they are clear, but clear nonetheless"; $10,000 penalty for Oct. 2011 UVA flight of five-pound styrofoam Zephyr; ALJ Geraghty dismissal March 6, 2014 with paper-airplane warning.
- FAA/USDA Wildlife Strikes to Civil Aircraft in the United States, 1990-2023 (June 27, 2024): 296,613 reports from 2,299 U.S. airports; 19,603 in 2023 (+14% over 2022); avg $248M/yr ($205M direct + $43M other), 100,105 hrs downtime; 2023: $461M, 62,761 hrs; voluntary FAA Form 5200-7.
- FAA Draft AC 150/5200-32C: ~97% of strikes involve birds; 620 bird species identified 1990-2021; waterfowl/gulls/raptors most damaging; European starlings greatest loss of human life.
- FAA 2024 GA & Part 135 Activity Survey: 213,756 active GA aircraft; FAA Air Traffic by the Numbers FY2024: 848,770 active pilot certificates (CY2024), excl. flight instructor/remote.
- Rueppell's griffon vulture confirmed at 11,300 m (37,000 ft) over Abidjan, Nov. 29, 1973, ingested into airliner engine; identified from five complete and fifteen partial feathers (Laybourne/Smithsonian).
- Rosenberg et al., Science 366:120-124 (2019): North America lost ~2.9 billion breeding birds since 1970 (-29%).
- 14 CFR 1.1, 47.3, Part 45, 91.13(a), 91.203(a), 91.225 (ADS-B effective Jan. 2, 2020), 91.409, Parts 21/43/67, 61.3, 61.56, 61.87, 61.109 (40 hrs), 139.337; 16 U.S.C. 703(a); Chicago Convention Arts. 12, 17-21.
- Arithmetic (verified): 19,603/365 = 53.7 per day, stated as "more than fifty"; 2020-1973 = 47 years.
