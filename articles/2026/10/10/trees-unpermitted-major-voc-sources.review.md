# 7-Critic Panel Evaluation: "Trees as Unpermitted Major VOC Sources"

## 1. General Editor — 9.0/10
The article follows the Ergo canon precisely: long factual headline, scholarly deck, Roman-numeral sections building a systematic case from threshold to operator to scale to visible evidence to ozone record to new construction to definitional void to enforcement impossibility, genuine footnotes with a Sources section, and the deadpan "Ergo." close. The load-bearing beam is the Section III inventory inversion: the agency's own table prints 38,679 thousand tons of biogenic VOC beside 16,478 thousand tons of anthropogenic, and the piece converts that into 386,790 times the Title V threshold. The Section VI Tailoring Rule beat is the sharpest single observation: the agency has admitted in writing that its own thresholds produce absurd results, then confined the absurdity to greenhouse gases. Minor weakness: Section IV (visible evidence) is the shortest section and functions partly as color; the Blue Ridge material earns its keep with the "named for its own unpermitted emissions" line but the haze chemistry could have carried one more paragraph of mechanism. The "1.4 trillion tons" lifetime figure assumes the 2014 national number as representative across thirty-six years, transparently flagged as computed.

## 2. Voice Coach — 9.5/10
Deadpan investigative register throughout, matching the thunder and lightning pieces, with humor emerging exclusively from bureaucratic precision rather than jokes. Standout lines: "The permitting authority has no form for no one," "unless the agency is prepared to invoice a stand of oaks, which it is not, although the stand could afford it," "The word 'tons' is doing the work. The threshold is doing the rest," "the absence of leaves." The piece never winks; the closest it comes is "the stand would deserve it," delivered as straight-faced procedural analysis. The Section II pivot, taking the agency at its word on the government's own inventory, is the voice at its best. Excellent voice match.

## 3. Ethics — 9.0/10
All statutes, regulations, and agency statements cited are real: the Title V 100 tpy threshold and the 50/25/10 tpy serious/severe/extreme VOC thresholds from the CRS Clean Air Act summary, the Section 185 $5,000-per-ton fee provision, the 2014 NEI biogenic and anthropogenic VOC totals, the EIIP Volume V "dominant natural source is vegetation" and "natural source VOC estimated to be higher than anthropogenic" statements, the Annals of Botany 0.5 x 10^15 g C isoprene figure, the 90% global biogenic share, the Blue Ridge isoprene naming from EBSCO and PNNL, the Beijing 2026 vegetation-ozone findings, and the Tailoring Rule "absurd result" admission from the CRS permitting report. The deliberately wrong conclusion, that forests are unpermitted Title V major sources, is the disclosed conceit; Section VII discloses plainly that the agency has never treated a forest as a stationary source. No invented figures: the threshold-exceedance factors and the 1.4-trillion-ton lifetime figure are computed from cited sources and flagged as computed. The 70 ppb 2015 ozone NAAQS is standard EPA fact. No ethical concerns.

## 4. Social/Shareability — 9.0/10
"No tree has ever applied for a Title V permit" is immediately graspable and funny at the headline level alone. "A mountain range named for its own unpermitted emissions" is the single most shareable line in the piece; "no form for no one" and "the stand could afford it" are strong pull-quote material. The hero image, an EPA inspector holding a NO PERMIT ON FILE clipboard beneath a towering oak with the blue-hazed Blue Ridge behind, is built for the thumbnail. The setup requires one paragraph of Clean Air Act exposition, which the deck does; slightly more specialized than the mosquito premise but the inventory table does the heavy lifting in plain numbers. Very high share potential.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 8.5/10
The regulatory citations are accurate: Title V's 1990 origin and operating-permit requirement, the 100 tpy major-source definition, the 50/25/10 tpy VOC thresholds for serious/severe/extreme ozone nonattainment, the twelve-month application deadline, the annual fee program, and the Section 185 fee provision, all rendered faithfully from the CRS summary. The New Source Review and PSD/BACT description from the CRS permitting report is accurate. The deliberately wrong conclusion, that forests are major sources within the meaning of Title V, is the genre's disclosed conceit, and the piece never pretends the agency has ever applied the program to vegetation; Section VII discloses the gap plainly. Passes threshold, with the standard Ergo deduction for creative inference from regulatory text.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.5/10
Exceptional sourcing across independent authorities: the Congressional Research Service (two reports) for the statute and permitting history, the EPA's own 2014 National Emissions Inventory profile for the 38.7M/16.5M ton figures, the EPA's EIIP Volume V for the natural-exceeds-anthropogenic finding, the Annals of Botany (Oxford) for the global isoprene flux, the Caltrans technical memo for the 90% global biogenic share, EBSCO and Pacific Northwest National Laboratory for the Blue Ridge naming, Physics Today for the 2026 Beijing vegetation-ozone study, the USDA Agricultural Research Service for the emitting species, and Eos for the conifer monoterpene and warming-amplification findings. No trade-press dependency for any load-bearing number; the one recent-development citation (Beijing 2026) is a primary research report via a science publication. Strongest sourcing of the October run.

## 7. Data Presentation — 9.0/10
Numbers are presented with consistent precision and always sourced: 100 tpy, 50/25/10 tpy in nonattainment tiers, 38,679 vs 16,478 thousand tons, the 2.3-to-1 ratio, 0.5 x 10^15 grams of carbon, 90% of global VOC, thirty-five percent of Beijing's urban trees, one-fourth of Beijing's ozone, seven times reactivity at 35 C, 30-45% warming amplification, $5,000 per ton, six million sources in the Tailoring Rule analysis, thirty-six years of the program, and the computed 386,790 / 773,580 / 3,867,900 exceedance factors. The comparison framework, the permitted industrial world versus the unpermitted biogenic sector on every metric, is devastating and verifiable. Clear, consistent, sourced.
