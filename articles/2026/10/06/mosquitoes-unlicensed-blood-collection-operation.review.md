# 7-Critic Panel Evaluation: "Mosquitoes as Unlicensed Blood-Collection Operation"

## 1. General Editor — 9.0/10
The article follows the Ergo canon precisely: long factual headline, scholarly deck with the honest disclosure front-loaded, Roman-numeral sections building a systematic case from collector to volume to license to consent to enforcement, genuine footnotes with a Sources section, two pullquotes, and the deadpan "Ergo." close. The arc escalates cleanly and the load-bearing beam, the donor-eligibility comparison (Red Cross requires 110 pounds, 17 years, ID, and 56 days; the mosquito requires nothing and returns nightly), holds through all six sections and delivers the strongest closing line of the recent run: "It operates on wings." Minor weakness: Section II's Gates 725,000 versus ScienceAlert 830,000 dual figures briefly read as a footnote dispute rather than a beat, though the "the mosquito's publicists and its actuaries differ" framing recovers it; and Section V's enforcement inventory covers vector control districts without naming a specific one, a smaller evidential base than the kudzu piece's Texas statute.

## 2. Voice Coach — 9.5/10
Deadpan investigative register throughout, matching the kudzu and coffee pieces, with the humor emerging exclusively from regulatory precision rather than jokes. Standout lines: "No mosquito has ever weighed its donor," "The five-day window closed before mammals existed," "The entire federal counterstrategy fits in a spray bottle," "She reuses the needle across every donor she will ever meet," and the Section IV triad "The donor is not compensated. The donor is not thanked. The donor is, in most cases, asleep." The piece never winks; the closest it comes is "the donor's immune system filing the only complaint it can," which lands as straight-faced administrative suspicion. The "it must be said" disclosure mirrors the kudzu piece's honest escape hatch without breaking register. Excellent voice match.

## 3. Ethics — 9.0/10
All statutes, dates, and figures cited are real and sourced: the 3,500 species and 200-plus US types, the female-only feeding biology, the 5-microliter and three-times-body-weight figures, the Gates 725,000 (2014) and 830,000 death estimates, the WHO 249-million-case and 608,000-death 2022 figures, the West Nile 2,000/1,300/130 domestic figures, the February 1, 2016 Zika PHEIC, the California 40/40/50 certification requirements, the FDA five-day registration rule, and the Red Cross 110-pound, 17-year, 56-day donor standards. Two pre-publication judgments were applied: the Gates 725,000 and ScienceAlert 830,000 figures were presented side by side as a disclosed range rather than cherry-picked; and the MegaCatch female-bite claim was corroborated by Smithsonian and CDC citations in the same paragraph rather than standing alone. The deliberately wrong conclusion, that the mosquito operates an unlicensed blood-collection program, is the disclosed conceit; the piece states plainly in the deck and in Section V that the statutes define phlebotomy for clinical laboratory purposes and regulate persons, neither of which describes the insect. No ethical concerns.

## 4. Social/Shareability — 9.0/10
"No mosquito has ever weighed its donor" is immediately graspable and funny at the headline level alone, and it doubles as the piece's thesis. "The entire federal counterstrategy fits in a spray bottle" is the most quotable single line of the recent run; the 725,000-deaths-versus-Shark-Week comparison and the 56-day-versus-nightly redonation contrast are vivid pull-quote material; the hero image, an inspector holding a violation checklist beside a giant mosquito in a red cross armband hovering over a donation chair, is built for the thumbnail. Slightly less universally graspable than the coffee premise because the payoff requires the phlebotomy-licensing setup, but the deck does that work in two sentences and the bite-versus-donation contrast needs no expertise. Very high share potential.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 8.5/10
The statutory summaries are accurate: California Business and Professions Code section 1220's certification requirement for unlicensed persons performing phlebotomy duties and the 40-hour didactic, 40-hour practical, 50-venipuncture CPT I pathway, per the code excerpts; the CDPH regulatory definition of phlebotomy quoted verbatim including the "clinical laboratory test or examination purposes" qualifier; the FDA blood-establishment registration requirement within five days of beginning operation under section 510 of the FD&C Act, per the FDA's own guidance page; and the Red Cross donor eligibility standards as published. The deliberately wrong conclusion, that the mosquito's feeding constitutes unlicensed blood collection under these regimes, is the genre's disclosed conceit, and the piece never pretends the statutes apply to insects, stating plainly that the code regulates persons and defines phlebotomy for laboratory purposes. Passes threshold, with the standard Ergo deduction for creative inference from regulatory text.

## 6. Research Rigor — 8.5/10
Strong sourcing across independent authorities: the CDC for species counts, germ-spreading types, West Nile statistics, and malaria history; the Smithsonian for feeding biology, saliva chemistry, and the four repellents; Reuters reporting the WHO World Malaria Report; JAMA for the Zika PHEIC and the Aedes aegypti disease chronology; the FDA for establishment registration; the California Department of Public Health for the phlebotomy definition; Massachusetts General Hospital for the 56-day interval. Weakest links: the 5-microliter-per-bite figure rests on obnews.co, an Indian content aggregator outside any scientific beat, and the 75-to-500-eggs figure on factsanddetails.com, a general reference site; both are peripheral quantities rather than load-bearing, and each is attributed in the footnote. The Wikipedia Zika chronology is tertiary but corroborated by the JAMA viewpoint in the adjacent footnote.

## 7. Data Presentation — 9.0/10
Numbers are presented with consistent precision and always sourced: 3,500 species, 200-plus US types, 12 germ-spreading types, 5 microliters, three times body weight, 75 to 500 eggs, 725,000 versus 830,000 annual deaths with both attributions, 249 million cases, 608,000 deaths, 2,000 West Nile diagnoses, 1,300 severe illnesses, 130 deaths, February 1 2016, 40 hours, 40 hours, 50 venipunctures, April 9 2006, five days, 110 pounds, 17 years, 56 days, four repellents. The dueling death estimates are presented side by side with both attributions rather than cherry-picked, which is the honest choice and also the funnier one. Clear, consistent, sourced.
