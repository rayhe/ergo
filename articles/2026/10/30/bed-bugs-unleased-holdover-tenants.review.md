# 7-Critic Panel Evaluation: "Bedbugs as Unleased Holdover Tenants"

## 1. General Editor — 9.0/10
The article follows the Ergo canon precisely: long factual headline, scholarly deck, eight Roman-numeral sections building a systematic case from occupancy to lease to rent to notice to holdover to disclosure to remedy to defense, genuine footnotes with a Sources section, a pullquote, and the deadpan "Ergo." close. The arc escalates cleanly: the biology of the occupant, the absent lease, the two-tenancies-one-lease rent inversion, the unservable defendant, the holdover that outlasts vacancies, the two New York disclosure regimes, the thermal eviction, and the disclosed-and-overruled defense. The load-bearing beam is the procedural impossibility, that the entire apparatus of tenancy law operates around an occupant it cannot name, serve, or remove except by heating the building, and it holds through all eight sections. Minor weakness: Section II's closing "tenant at will" paragraph states the analogy rather than extending it, and the Doe-defendant beat in Section IV, while procedurally grounded, is the piece's one flourish that explains itself slightly.

## 2. Voice Coach — 9.0/10
Deadpan investigative register throughout, matching the deciduous-trees and starling pieces, with the humor emerging exclusively from bureaucratic precision rather than jokes. Standout lines: "The building therefore contains two tenancies and one lease," "The writ of possession is thermal. The sheriff is a heater," "The occupant was there first, never left, and never gave the thirty days' notice it was never asked for," "No other eviction in American law requires the plaintiff in possession to vacate the premises to effect service on the defendant," "The landlord collects the rent. The bug collects the rent's collector." The piece never winks; the closest it comes is "The schedules do not overlap," which lands as straight-faced procedural record. No em dashes, no exclamation points in prose, no slang. Excellent voice match.

## 3. Ethics — 9.0/10
All statutes, regulations, ordinances, and data cited are real and verified this run: the CDC-EPA 2010 joint statement ("a pest of significant public health importance," not known to transmit disease), NYC Local Law 69 of 2017 and Admin. Code sections 27-2018.1 and 27-2018.2 (annual HPD filing December 1-31 covering November 1-October 31, mandatory even with zero cases, DBB-N vacancy-lease disclosure, Class B violation with thirty days to correct), Chicago Municipal Code sections 7-28-830, 7-28-850, and 5-12-101 (ten-day licensed-PMP deadline, treat until no evidence remains, adjacent-unit inspection, three-year records, brochure at signing or renewal, tenant five-day written notice, two months' rent or twice actual damages plus fees, unlawful to re-rent an infested unit), Civil Code section 1624(a)(3) quoted from the legislature's own text, the Javins holding quoted from Justia, heat-treatment kill temperatures and cost ranges (122 degrees kill, 135-140 applied, $500-$5,000 via Virginia Tech Extension, ~$2,500 national average via Angi 2026), and the bedbug biology (200-250 eggs, five molts each requiring a blood meal, up to one year unfed, five-minute feeds, apple-seed size). The deliberately wrong conclusion, that bedbugs are tenants under landlord-tenant law, is disclosed via the Section VIII defense per the genre's honest-satire contract. No person or company is harmed. Infestation distress is treated factually, never mocked; the satire targets the legal apparatus, not its victims.

## 4. Social/Shareability — 8.5/10
"The most secure tenant in the building" is immediately graspable and funny at the headline level alone, and "The writ of possession is thermal. The sheriff is a heater." is excerpt-ready. Renters are a near-universal audience, and the process-server-with-magnifying-glass hero image is built for the thumbnail. It lacks a single viral sentence of the "the moon is stealing your water" grade, but the premise travels on its own: everyone who has ever signed a lease understands the injustice of the occupant who signed nothing. High share potential.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 9.0/10
The statutory citations are accurate: Civil Code section 1624(a)(3) quoted verbatim from leginfo, the Javins habitability holding quoted verbatim from Justia, the New York filing and disclosure duties accurately described from HPD guidance, and the Chicago ten-day deadline, adjacent-unit rule, record retention, brochure duty, tenant notice duty, and damages formula all accurately stated from municipal-code sources. The general procedural passages, notice sequences, substituted service, Doe defendants in unlawful detainer captions, are accurate statements of standard practice. The deliberately wrong conclusion, that an insect holds a tenancy, is the genre's disclosed conceit, and the piece never pretends any statute defines the bug as a tenant. Passes threshold.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0/10
Strong sourcing across independent authorities: the CDC-EPA joint statement PDF for the public-health framing, the Bugwood Northern Plains IPM guide and a Virginia Tech-cited industry guide for the biology, the California Legislature's official code text for the statute of frauds, Justia for Javins, HPD-focused compliance guides for the New York regime, a Chicago law firm's ordinance summary for the ten-day and damages rules, and industry pricing data for treatment costs. Eighteen footnotes, all linked to real pages, every load-bearing number verified in this run's searches. Minor weakness: the bedbug-city ranking claim rests on an industry reporter's summary rather than the underlying ranking release.

## 7. Data Presentation — 9.0/10
Numbers are presented with consistent precision and always sourced: five-minute feeds every five to ten days, 200 to 250 eggs per female, five molts, up to one year unfed, apple-seed size at 5 to 7 millimeters, 122-degree kill temperature against 135 to 140 applied, $500 to $5,000 per whole-home treatment, ~$2,500 national average, ten days to hire, five days to notify, three years of records, two months' rent or twice actual damages, thirty days to correct a Class B violation, December 1-31 filing window over a November-to-October reporting year. The comparison framework, the papered human tenancy versus the unpapered insect tenancy on every procedural metric, is devastating and verifiable. Clear, consistent, sourced.
