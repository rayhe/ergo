# 7-Critic Panel Evaluation: "Deciduous Trees as Unpermitted Solid-Waste Generators"

## 1. General Editor — 9.0/10
The article follows the Ergo canon precisely: long factual headline, scholarly deck, six Roman-numeral sections building a systematic case from the waste stream to the ban to the generators to the water to the cleanup economy to the enforcement void, genuine footnotes with a Sources section, a pullquote, and the deadpan "Ergo." close. The arc escalates cleanly: the tonnage, the prohibitions, the unpermitted generators, the stormwater inversion (the city holds the discharge permit for the tree's waste), the cleanup equipment being more regulated than the generator, and the definitional void. The load-bearing beam is the liability inversion, that the entire regulatory apparatus polices everyone except the generators, and it holds through all six sections. Minor weakness: Section V's blower-ban beat slightly overlaps the register of Section III's liability beat; and the headline's final clause ("no waste-generator identification number") is the piece's best line but arrives after a long statutory preamble.

## 2. Voice Coach — 9.0/10
Deadpan investigative register throughout, matching the bird-nest and starling pieces, with the humor emerging exclusively from bureaucratic precision rather than jokes. Standout lines: "The leaves still fall; they are simply no longer weighed at the gate," "The trees were not consulted," "The generator is a tree," "The city holds the paper, and the tree holds the canopy," "The law is a fence with no gate," "It does not dispose, in the sense the statute means; it drops." The piece never winks; the closest it comes is "the trees were not consulted," which lands as straight-faced procedural record. The Fort Wayne concession, that the city criminalizes every disposal path but compels nothing, is the piece's sharpest deadpan beat. Excellent voice match.

## 3. Ethics — 9.0/10
All statutes, regulations, ordinances, and data cited are real and verified: the EPA's 2018 yard-trimmings figures (35.4 million tons, 12.1 percent, 22.3 million tons composted at 63 percent, 10.5 million tons landfilled at 7.2 percent of landfilled MSW), RCRA Section 4005's open-dumping prohibition via 40 CFR 256.26, the 24-state yard-waste disposal restriction count, the 26-state separate-disposal figure, the Wisconsin/Pennsylvania/Iowa first-mover history, Fair Lawn's $2,000 fines and $100 seasonal landscaper permits (announced October 22, 2026), Davenport's 8.14.400 ($100/$500), Mansfield's ordinance 911.06(b), Michigan's $800-$5,000 littering fines, the USGS Selbig 27-80 percent leachability finding, the Madison catchment study (84/83 percent phosphorus, 74/71 percent nitrogen reductions), the regenerative-air sweeper figures (65-71 percent total phosphorus, 57-70 percent dissolved), the D.C. gas-blower ban (January 1, 2022, $500 fines, signed-complaint regime), and the 170-jurisdiction count. Two pre-publication corrections were applied during drafting: unverified "about six weeks" leaf-drop timing claims were softened to sourced-neutral phrasing. The deliberately wrong conclusion, that trees are unpermitted solid-waste generators under RCRA, is the disclosed conceit; the piece is honest about the definitional escape hatch, trees are not persons, in the same way prior pieces disclosed theirs. No ethical concerns.

## 4. Social/Shareability — 9.0/10
"Trees hold no waste-generator identification number" is immediately graspable and funny at the headline level alone. The $2,000-fine-for-leaves news hook (days old) gives it current-events legs; "The city holds the paper, and the tree holds the canopy" and "The generator is a tree" are strong pull-quote material. The hero image, an inspector with a citation pad staring up at a leaf-dropping oak beside a buried NO DUMPING sign, is built for the thumbnail. Peak leaf-fall timing (late October) maximizes relevance. Very high share potential.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 8.5/10
The statutory citations are accurate: RCRA Section 4005's open-dumping prohibition (42 USC 6945, via 40 CFR 256.26's implementation language), the Subtitle D framework and the 40 CFR 257/258 facility criteria, the state-ban counts and first-mover history, the municipal ordinances (Fair Lawn, Davenport 8.14.400, Mansfield 911.06(b), Michigan littering law), the D.C. Leaf Blower Regulation Amendment Act of 2018 (effective January 1, 2022, $500 fines), and the MS4 stormwater program with the EPA's own materials listing leaf litter as a phosphorus source. The deliberately wrong conclusion, that a tree is a "generator" within the meaning of the solid waste law, is the genre's disclosed conceit, and the piece never pretends RCRA defines trees as generators. Passes threshold, with the standard Ergo deduction for creative inference from regulatory text.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0/10
Strong sourcing across independent authorities: epa.gov for all tonnage and composting figures, ecfr.gov for the open-dumping implementation language, the NRC's RCRA summary, Waste360 for the state-ban count and composting economics, the USGS for the phosphorus studies, Illinois Extension and South Dakota State Extension for the stormwater findings, the District of Columbia's DLCP for the blower ban, DCist for the 170-jurisdiction count, and municipal and local-press sources (Mansfield, Fort Wayne, WMTR, 103 WJOD, WGRD) for the ordinances. Nineteen footnotes, all linked to real pages. Minor weakness: the EPA tonnage figures are 2018 (the agency's latest published materials characterization), which the piece does not flag as dated; the figures are the current official record.

## 7. Data Presentation — 9.0/10
Numbers are presented with consistent precision and always sourced: 35.4 million tons, 12.1 percent, 22.3 million tons at 63 percent, 2.6 million tons combusted, 10.5 million tons at 7.2 percent of landfilled MSW; 24 states banning or restricting, 26 requiring separate disposal; $2,000 in Fair Lawn, $100/$500 in Davenport, $800-$5,000 in Michigan, $100 per truck per season in Fair Lawn, $500 per offense in D.C.; 27-80 percent leachable in 24 hours; 84/83 percent phosphorus and 74/71 percent nitrogen reductions in Madison; 65-71 percent and 57-70 percent with regenerative-air sweepers; 40-75 percent volume loss and 50 percent weight loss in leaf composting; 250-500 versus 1,500 pounds per cubic yard; 170 jurisdictions in 31 states. The comparison framework, permitted human handlers versus the unpermitted generators on every metric, is devastating and verifiable. Clear, consistent, sourced.
