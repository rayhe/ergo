# 7-Critic Panel Evaluation: "Crows as an Unlicensed Biometric Surveillance Network"

## 1. General Editor — 9.0/10
The article follows the Ergo canon precisely: long factual headline, scholarly deck, Roman-numeral sections building a systematic case from statute to database to network to retention to consent to disclosure to quantification to jurisdiction to objection to conclusion, genuine footnotes with a Sources section, two pullquotes, and the deadpan "Ergo." close. The arc escalates cleanly: the statute's five duties, the Seattle enrollment experiment, the social-learning network, the retention deadline the birds missed by fourteen years, the empty consent file, the disclosure chain that defeats the legislature's single-recovery cap, the $235,000 single-walk arithmetic, the Illinois nexus, the honestly stated objection, the conclusion. The load-bearing beam is the Section VI maneuver, that SB 2979 caps repeat collections by the same entity and repeat disclosures to the same recipient while the crow system recruits new entities and new recipients by breeding, and it holds through all ten sections. Minor weakness: the closing "It should not" inverts the usual "It should" verdict sentence, a deliberate variation that reads slightly less cleanly than the canon close.

## 2. Voice Coach — 9.0/10
Deadpan investigative register throughout, matching the deer and pumpkin pieces, with the humor emerging exclusively from bureaucratic precision rather than jokes. Standout lines: "The database recruits its own administrators," "The legislature wrote a rule for a turnstile. The crows operate a franchise," "It is the equivalent of a company informing you that it has your face by pointing at you and shouting," and "it satisfies it only because no crow has ever discovered commerce." The piece never winks; the closest it comes is "which is to say, by having chicks," which lands as straight-faced procedural suspicion. Zero em dashes in the draft, well within house discipline. Excellent voice match.

## 3. Ethics — 9.0/10
All regulations and event facts cited are real and quoted accurately where quoted: the BIPA definitions and Section 15 duties from the Illinois General Assembly text, the Section 20 penalty figures, the Marzluff 2010 findings (7 to 15 birds, five sites, at least 2.7 years, discrimination across wearers and crowds), the Cornell 2012 social-learning findings (scolding doubled, 1.2 km spread, 6.4-bird mobs), the Cothron accrual rule, and the SB 2979 single-recovery amendment signed August 2, 2024. The deliberately wrong conclusion, that crows violate BIPA, is the disclosed conceit, and the piece is unusually honest about the limits of its own evidence: the seventeen-year arc is explicitly flagged as the university's long-running account rather than a peer-reviewed finding, the 47-of-53 walk is attributed to Marzluff's account, the Washington location of the experiments is disclosed in Section VIII, and Section IX states the private-entity, scan-versus-sight, and non-commercial objections in full before declining them. No ethical concerns.

## 4. Social/Shareability — 9.0/10
"Crows run a facial-recognition network with no written release" is immediately graspable and funny at the headline level alone, and the hero image, a privacy investigator with a blank consent form staring up at a crow on a streetlamp, is built for the thumbnail. The most shareable paragraphs are the franchise line, the $235,000 single-walk arithmetic, and the retention schedule that outlived the collectors themselves. The Illinois biometric-privacy hook gives it a news-adjacent frame the purely natural-history pieces lack. High share potential.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 8.5/10
The citations are accurate: 740 ILCS 14/14 definitions, 14/15(a) retention and destruction, 14/15(b) notice and written release, 14/15(c) through (e), 14/20 remedies, Cothron v. White Castle System, Inc., 2023 IL 128004, and SB 2979's same-method and same-recipient single-recovery rules. The piece correctly states the three-year destruction deadline as "purpose satisfied or three years, whichever first," and correctly notes that enforcement runs through private suits rather than an agency. The deliberately wrong conclusion, that the crow system violates BIPA, is the genre's disclosed conceit, and Section IX states the escape hatch explicitly. Passes threshold, with the standard Ergo deduction for creative inference from regulatory text, here the application of "scan of face geometry" to avian vision, which the piece labels as author's application in the footnotes.

## 6. Research Rigor — 8.5/10
Strong sourcing across independent authorities: the Illinois General Assembly text for the statute, the peer-reviewed Marzluff 2010 and Cornell 2012 papers for the enrollment and transmission findings, the Illinois Supreme Court and legislature for accrual and amendment, and Partners in Flight via Audubon for the 28,000,000 population estimate. Competing characterizations are handled carefully: the peer-reviewed retention floor (2.7 years) is kept distinct from the longer university account (seventeen years), and the author's arithmetic is explicitly labeled as such. Minor weakness: the Illinois presence claim rests on birding guides rather than a primary range authority such as the Cornell Lab, though two independent guides agree and the claim itself is uncontroversial. The PNAS brain-imaging study was omitted rather than cited loosely, which is the right call.

## 7. Data Presentation — 9.0/10
Numbers are presented with consistent precision and always sourced: 7 to 15 birds, five sites, at least 2.7 years, seventeen years, fourteen years past the deadline, 82 percent of 126 scolding events, mobs averaging 6.4 crows, 47 of 53 scolding, 1.2 km spread over five years, $1,000 and $5,000 penalty tiers, $235,000 for a single walk, 28,000,000 birds, hundreds of thousands per winter roost, 24 percent of summer checklists and 29 percent of winter checklists in Illinois. The comparison framework, commercial data center versus crow roost holding the same architecture in trees, is devastating and verifiable. Clear, consistent, sourced.
