# Ergo Review: Lemonade Stands as Unpermitted Food Establishments

**Topic:** Children's lemonade stands as unpermitted food establishments under the FDA Food Code: §8-301.11 prohibits operating a food establishment without a valid permit issued by the regulatory authority; a food establishment is any operation that stores, prepares, packages, serves, or vends food directly to the consumer; the permit application is due thirty calendar days before opening under §8-302.11; cottage food laws (Texas HB 970) exclude foods requiring time/temperature control, which officials have held includes lemonade; sixty years of documented enforcement (1967 Satellite Beach FL, 1975 Indianapolis, 1984 Coronado CA, 1987 Pacific Grove CA, 2010 Multnomah OR, 2011 Coralville IA, 2011 Midway GA, 2015 Overton TX, 2018 Ballston Spa NY); Country Time's Legal-Ade fund ($60,000 initial, up to $300 per child under 14, $1/retweet up to $500,000, $30,000 in documented fines paid in one program year); Texas HB 2847 (2019) unanimously legalizing minors' stands; fourteen to sixteen states with express exemptions, thirty-four to thirty-six without; zero prosecutions, zero permits.
**Author:** Kit (with Ray)
**Date:** 2026-10-01

## Headline
"Section 8-301.11 of the FDA Food Code Provides That a Person May Not Operate a Food Establishment Without a Valid Permit to Operate Issued by the Regulatory Authority, a Food Establishment Is Any Operation That Stores, Prepares, Serves, or Vends Food Directly to the Consumer, Lemonade Is Food, the Stand Is the Establishment, the Permit Application Must Be Filed Thirty Calendar Days Before Opening, No Child Has Ever Filed One, Texas Had to Pass a Law in 2019 Because Police Shut Down Two Sisters Aged Seven and Eight for Selling Lemonade Without a Health Permit, Thirty-Four States Still Require the Permit, a Lemonade Company Has Budgeted Hundreds of Thousands of Dollars to Pay the Children's Fines, and the Largest Unpermitted Food Service Operation in American History Is Run by Children Under Fourteen"

## Thesis
The FDA Food Code prohibits any person from operating a food establishment without a valid permit, defines the establishment as any operation that stores, prepares, serves, or vends food directly to the consumer, and requires the application thirty calendar days before opening. A lemonade stand stores, prepares, serves, and vends. No child has ever filed the application. Cottage food exemptions affirmatively exclude temperature-controlled foods, and officials have classified lemonade as one. The enforcement record runs sixty years and reaches children as young as four. A major food company maintains a legal defense fund for the violators. Fifteen states have had to carve out express exemptions, which are admissions that the baseline rule covers the conduct in the other thirty-five. Zero prosecutions, zero permits, compliance rate zero, every summer.

## The Deliberately Wrong Conclusion
Real law and practice defeat the case five ways: enforcement discretion is near-total, shutdowns make news because they are rare; lemonade's pH of roughly 2-3 sits far below the 4.6 pathogen-growth threshold, making the temperature-control rationale scientifically shaky; temporary permits cost $25-30 where they exist and the thirty-day rule is honored in the breach for bake sales nationwide; fourteen to sixteen states have expressly exempted minors' stands with more following; and sixty years with zero prosecutions is the system's own verdict. All stated honestly in Section X, then overruled in Section XI: the Code's text does not know the operator's age; discretion is not an amendment; the statute classifies by operation, not acidity; the exemptions are confessions; and Country Time's fund is a loss reserve priced at $300 per child.
