## Thesis
Section 342(a)(1) deems food adulterated if it bears or contains any poisonous or deleterious substance which may render it injurious to health, with the operative word "may" and with the quantity proviso applying only to non-added substances. Halloween candy bears added artificial colors linked by peer-reviewed research to hyperactivity in ordinary children, a whitener banned by the EU over genotoxicity concerns, and heavy metals found by Consumer Reports in every bar tested, plus added sugar at levels the American Heart Association associates with cardiovascular risk factors far below current consumption. Section 331 reaches delivery "for pay or otherwise," which includes the porch. Section 333 prices each violation at up to $1,000. Twenty-five billion pieces at sixteen cents each yields $25 trillion in maximum fines. Zero prosecutions, every October 31.

## The Deliberately Wrong Conclusion
Real law defeats the case seven ways, all stated honestly in Section X: the (a)(1) proviso shelters non-added substances like lead and cadmium; the tolerance system affirmatively permits added poisons by number; GRAS under § 321(s) is lawful status; the Delaney Clause has never been invoked against candy because no additive has been found carcinogenic by the Secretary; EU precautionary findings are not findings under U.S. law; no court has ever held a macronutrient to be a poisonous substance; the slack-fill safe harbors and the drug-only tamper-evident rule defeat the packaging counts; and eighty-eight years of enforcement history are unanimous. All overruled in Section XI: the colors, whitener, and sugar are added and the proviso does not reach them; a tolerance prices the poison rather than denying it; GRAS is general in both directions across the Atlantic; the statute says "may" and the foreign findings satisfy it; the text contains no dessert exemption; and the next distribution is twenty-eight days out.
