# Ergo Review: Earthquakes as the Largest Unpermitted Blasting Operation

**Topic:** Earthquakes as unpermitted blasting operations under 27 CFR Part 555 (ATF explosives licensing) and 29 CFR 1926 Subpart U (OSHA blasting safety): ~20,000 located discharges per year, ~55 per day, zero licenses, zero qualified blasters, zero warning signals, zero fixed times of blasting; the 1964 Alaska M9.2 released ~950 million tons of TNT equivalent with effects registered in 47 states and no user permit for interstate transport.
**Author:** Kit (with Ray)
**Date:** 2026-10-16

## Headline
"Federal Regulation at 27 CFR 555.41 Provides That Each Person Intending to Engage in the Business of Manufacturing Explosives Must Obtain a License Before Commencing Business, OSHA Regulation at 29 CFR 1926.909 Provides That Before a Blast Is Fired a Loud Warning Signal Shall Be Given by the Blaster in Charge, the United States Geological Survey Locates Approximately 20,000 Earthquakes Each Year, a Magnitude 6.0 Earthquake Releases Energy Equivalent to Approximately 15,000 Tons of TNT, the 1964 Alaska Earthquake Released Energy Equivalent to Approximately 950 Million Tons of TNT Without a Blasting License, No Warning Signal Was Given, No Blaster in Charge Has Ever Been Identified, and the Largest Unpermitted Blasting Operation in American History Is the Ground Itself"

## Thesis
Commerce in explosives requires a federal license before commencing business (27 CFR 555.41), with user permits for interstate transport; construction blasting requires a blaster qualified by training, knowledge, or experience (29 CFR 1926.901), a loud warning signal before the blast is fired, flagmen on highways through the danger zone, and a blaster who fixes the time of blasting (29 CFR 1926.909, Table U-1). The USGS locates ~20,000 earthquakes a year (~55/day); a M6.0 releases ~15,080 tons of TNT equivalent; the 1964 Alaska M9.2 released ~950 million tons with effects registered in 47 states. No license, no blaster, no signal, no fixed time. The NEIC catalog is a real-time, federally operated log of 20,000 annual violations with zero referrals.

## The Deliberately Wrong Conclusion
Earthquakes are not "blasts" under the explosives regulations; 27 CFR Part 555 and 29 CFR 1926 Subpart U govern human commerce in and use of manufactured explosives, and tectonic strain release is a natural process no blaster could schedule. The article states all three defenses explicitly in Section IX (the persons defense, the blast defense, the time defense) and overrules them on the regulation's own terms: Table U-1 contains no geological exception, the personal-nonbusiness exemption cannot stretch to a 950-million-ton interstate discharge, and the inability to fix the time is the violation, not an excuse. The conceit is disclosed by the defense section, per the genre's contract.

## Research Notes
- 27 CFR 555.41(b)(1)(i)-(iii) (govinfo.gov PDF, verified this run): license before commencing business as importer/manufacturer/dealer; user permits for acquisition and interstate transport.
- ATF Q&A (atf.gov): manufacturer's license required for blasting-service businesses; personal-nonbusiness manufacture exempt.
- 29 CFR 1926.901(c)-(d) (govinfo.gov PDF): blaster qualified by training/knowledge/experience; satisfactory evidence of competency.
- 29 CFR 1926.909 (osha.gov): Table U-1 (warning signal 1-min long blasts 5 min prior; blast signal short blasts 1 min prior; all-clear prolonged blast); 909(b) loud warning signal; 909(c) flagmen; 909(d) "It shall be the duty of the blaster to fix the time of blasting."
- USGS Earthquake Hazards Program FAQ: ~500,000 detected/year worldwide, ~100,000 felt, ~20 M7.0+/year; NEIC locates ~20,000/year (~55/day); ~20,000/year in the US.
- Energy: M6.0 = 15,080 tons TNT; M7.0 = 476,879 tons; M8.0 = 15.08M tons (easycalculation table); M6.5 = 84,802 tons, ratio 31.6^0.4 used to compute Loma Prieta M6.9 at ~340,000 tons (corrected from an initial 240,000 during drafting).
- IRIS/USGS one-pager: Northridge 1994 M6.7 = almost 2 billion kg of explosive, ~100x Hiroshima.
- NASA SpaceMath: 1964 Anchorage M9.2 = ~950 million tons TNT; 950M/15k = ~63,333x Hiroshima, stated as "more than 60,000 times."
- Earth Magazine: 1964 released equivalent of nearly 2,000 magnitude-7 quakes; 5:36 p.m. Good Friday; 4-5 min shaking; some Alaskans thought the Soviets dropped a nuclear bomb; felt over 1.3M sq km.
- S. Res. 400 (congress.gov): largest in US history, second-largest ever with modern instruments, felt to Seattle, registered by water-level recorders in 47 states, 131 fatalities.
- USGS Alaska Science Center: M9.2; 4.5-min shaking; 131 fatalities; $3.1B (2024 dollars); 36-ft uplift Montague Island; 580-mile rupture in ~240 sec; 185,000 sq mi destruction; 67-ft max lateral movement.
- USGS Loma Prieta: M6.9, Oct 17 1989, 5:04 p.m. PDT; strongest since 1906 M7.9; 63 deaths; 3,757 injuries; ~$6B damage; Cypress Viaduct 41 deaths.
